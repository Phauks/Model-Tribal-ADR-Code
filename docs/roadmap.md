# Roadmap for documenting the model code

**Status:** draft, Nov. 21, 2026. This roadmap sets the order of work from the data now in the repository to a drafted model code. It has gates: points where work stops until the lead author, Trevor Goldsmith, has reviewed and decided. **No model provision is drafted before Gate 1 and Gate 2 are passed.** The roadmap itself is written now so the review can be aimed at what drafting will need; it will be revised after each gate (project choice).

The phases below follow the charter's commitments, the provision schema and the [provisional analysis](../research/analyses/2026-11-21-dispute-resolution-practice.md) of the documented practice. Where a step rests on that analysis, the analysis is cited; everything else is project planning.

| Phase | What happens | Can start | Ends with |

Peacemaking runs as its own track through every phase. In the model code it is its own division, never a kind of mediation or arbitration ([charter, commitment 3](charter.md#3-peacemaking-as-its-own-track); [decision record 0026](decisions/0026-model-peacemaking-code.md)).

## Phase 0: preparation (now)

These steps do not depend on reviewed data and can proceed in parallel with Trevor's review:

- **Methods vocabulary.** The open list of methods (pull request #5) has merged. Rename profile forum kinds to method ids in one pull request. The methods thread has proposed a mapping, for example mediation-program to mediation and employment-tribunal to administrative-adjudication; it is not yet applied (review queue item 6 in the analysis).
- **Archived copies.** Run the archive workflow. Only 4 of 1,970 source records have an archived copy, and the citation rules require one for every web source ([citation-style.md](citation-style.md#rules-the-project-adds); analysis, review queue item 9).
- **Citation style for tribal law.** Finish the open tasks in [citation-style.md](citation-style.md#open-tasks): how the *Indigo Book* treats tribal codes and court decisions, and the statute pinpoint fix.
- **Law library pulls.** Westlaw and other pulls listed in the United States dispute resolution bibliography (pull request #5).
- **Reference texts.** Gather the uniform acts a drafter will compare against, such as the Uniform Mediation Act and the Revised Uniform Arbitration Act, as source records with rights noted ([charter, commitment 9](charter.md#9-copyright-and-permissions)).
- **Analysis tooling.** Keep [`scripts/analysis/dispute_resolution_practice.py`](../scripts/analysis/dispute_resolution_practice.py) current so the analysis can be re-run after each review pass.

- **Editing essays on the website.** Essays live in the private bucket and are edited, versioned and published in the workspace ([editing.md](editing.md), decision record 0021). Next: live co-editing on Durable Objects, comments on drafts, the print and ebook builds reading the published copy, figures, and the bucket lock on `essays/` ([editing.md, "Built so far and still to build"](editing.md#built-so-far-and-still-to-build)).

Not in Phase 0: choosing chapters, writing provision text, writing commentary or illustrations.

## Gate 1: review of the pull request #2 data

The analysis found that most of the dispute resolution record has not been read closely: 938 provisions are unreviewed bulk database records and 291 rest on section titles alone, against 98 drawn from read text. Sixty of the 185 nations with any record have at least one record from read text ([analysis, summary](../research/analyses/2026-11-21-dispute-resolution-practice.md#summary)). Drafting on that base would risk describing practice the sources do not support, so the review comes first (project analysis).

Gate 1 is passed when Trevor has:

1. **Decided the scope question for the bulk records**: whether diversion, treatment and restorative sentencing provisions belong in the dispute resolution data, a separate field, or neither (analysis, section 5). *Decided Nov. 21, 2026: a separate field, `dispute_resolution.database_listings` ([decision record 0015](decisions/0015-database-listings-apart-from-provisions.md)).*
2. **Triaged the bulk records** in line with that decision: kept, relabeled or dropped. Sampling is acceptable if the sample and its result are recorded. *Decided Nov. 22, 2026: the pre-review's recommendations accepted, with diversion to a consensual process and restorative justice kept; 258 listings and 229 provisions remain, and 41 records wait for their sources ([decision record 0027](decisions/0027-diversion-and-restorative-justice-in-the-research-base.md)).*
3. **Confirmed the text-read tier** for every record a draft is likely to rely on: forums, clauses and text-read provisions, re-reading OCR and summary-tool reads where they matter (analysis, review queue item 10). *Confirmed by Trevor Nov. 23, 2026: 575 of the 627 forums, provisions, clauses, materials, training programs and compact dispute clauses now rest on the nation's own text, read. Of the 172 not yet confirmed, 120 were read (43 confirmed, 76 corrected, 1 flagged as not dispute resolution), all 30 OCR reads were re-read from page images, and the 52 still unread are labeled as such and on the pull list ([review session log](../research/sessions/2026/2026-11-23-text-read-tier.md)). On his go-ahead two flagged records were then removed (Nisqually 24.19, which the current Title 24 does not contain, and a Minnesota Chippewa Tribe compact record the Tribe is not a party to), leaving 575 of 625 read and 50 unread.*
4. **Resolved the duplicates**: 71 repeated provision titles, four doubled source records and forums recorded under two kinds (review queue items 3 to 5). *Provision duplicates removed Nov. 22, 2026 (decision record 0027).*
5. **Decided how bands inside one profile are counted**, starting with the Minnesota Chippewa Tribe profile (review queue item 7). *Decided Nov. 21, 2026: each of the six component bands has its own profile, linked by `part_of` ([decision record 0016](decisions/0016-component-bands-own-profiles.md)).*
6. **Accepted or set aside the proposed method records** (32 at the merge of pull request #5), so every forum kind maps to a method or stays "other" for a stated reason. *Decided Nov. 22, 2026: all 33 method records adopted as written, with no changes asked; every forum kind maps to a method, and the one forum recorded as "other" (Kalispel's Traditional Peacemakers Panel) states its reason ([review session log](../research/sessions/2026/2026-11-22-method-records-review.md)).*

A review session log records what was checked and decided, as for any research session ([research/README.md](../research/README.md)).

### Scope questions for Gate 2

Each question comes from what the data shows (project analysis). The [working outline](outline.md#open-scope-questions) restates them with a recommendation for each and adds four more:

| Question | Why it arises |
| --- | --- |
| Does the code cover diversion and restorative sentencing, or only disputes between parties? | Most bulk records concern criminal, juvenile and child welfare processes (analysis, section 5) |
| Does it include commercial arbitration and the link to sovereign immunity waivers? | Four arbitration ordinances read share one structure tying awards to express waivers (analysis, section 4) |
| Are disputes between governments, such as compact disputes, a chapter or an essay? | Compact clauses mostly follow state templates (analysis, section 6) |
| Are employment disputes in scope? | One employment tribunal and one employment mediation clause were found (analysis, sections 3 and 4) |
| What does the code say about peacemaking, if anything? | *Decided Nov. 22, 2026: model peacemaking provisions of their own, which set up or recognize a nation's forum and connect it to the court without defining the process ([decision record 0026](decisions/0026-model-peacemaking-code.md)).* |
| Which family and child welfare matters are in scope? | Family and child welfare matters appear in both forums and bulk records (analysis, sections 3 and 5) |
| Does the code or its commentary address nations divided by an international border? | Akwesasne, the Blackfoot Confederacy, Tohono O'odham and others live on both sides of the Canadian or Mexican border, and each country sets their status by its own law ([Canada note](../research/analyses/2026-11-21-canada-dispute-resolution.md#7-what-the-comparison-suggests-project-analysis); [Mexico note](../research/analyses/2026-11-21-mexico-indigenous-dispute-resolution.md)) |

### Candidate outline, to confirm at Gate 2

The full working outline of the code, the book and the website, with recommendations on each scope question, is now kept in [outline.md](outline.md) (version 0.1, Nov. 22, 2026). The list below is the starting point it grew from, kept for history.

A starting point for discussion, not a decision. It follows the provision schema's tracks (general, peacemaking, mediation, arbitration, restorative, family and administration) and the recurring features in the analysis ([schemas/provision.schema.json](../schemas/provision.schema.json); analysis, section 2):

1. General provisions: purpose, definitions, relation to the Tribal court, confidentiality, qualifications of neutrals.
2. Referral and transfer: consent, screening (including for domestic violence), return to court when a process ends without agreement.
3. Mediation.
4. Agreements: entry as a court order or judgment, appeal and modification.
5. Arbitration: enforceability of agreements, confirmation of awards, and the relation to immunity waivers.
6. Other chapters as the scope memo decides, such as restorative processes, family matters, employment or intergovernmental disputes.

Adding a track beyond the schema's list (for example intergovernmental) is a schema change made at Gate 2.

## Phase 2: drafting

- One file per provision and one per commentary, numbered as in [repository-layout.md](repository-layout.md#identifiers).
- Provisions follow the drafting rules in the [style guide, section 4](style-guide.md#4-model-provisions): must, must not and may; a named actor; template variables for nation-specific words; and lettered alternatives that each work alone.
- Commentary follows the Restatement pattern, with Source Notes citing every law relied on ([style guide, section 5](style-guide.md#5-commentary-illustrations-and-essays)).
- Illustrations use fictitious nations unless a nation approves its use ([charter, commitment 4](charter.md#4-consultation-before-publication)).
- Each chapter is drafted on its own branch and pull request, with status `draft`, then `in-review`.
- Comparative sidebars on Canada and Mexico: start from the research notes ([Canada](../research/analyses/2026-11-21-canada-dispute-resolution.md), whose section 8 holds a draft sidebar; [Mexico](../research/analyses/2026-11-21-mexico-indigenous-dispute-resolution.md)). Before the text is final, clear the open pulls for both in the project's Westlaw list (sections 11 and 12) and re-check the 2026 Criminal Code restorative justice part and the Teslin Tlingit Peacemaker Court's status.

## Gate 3: consultation

Before publication, every nation whose law is cited or described in commentary is offered the chance to review what is said about it, through its own offices, and the result is recorded in its profile's review status ([charter, commitment 4](charter.md#4-consultation-before-publication); [requests.md](requests.md)). Every profile is now "drafted from public sources" (analysis, review queue item 12).
