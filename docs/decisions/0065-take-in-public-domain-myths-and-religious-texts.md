# 0065. Take in public-domain myths and religious texts, including ceremonial ones

- **Status:** Accepted (lead author, Nov. 26, 2026, 5:21 PM Pacific); to be reviewed by the project's Indigenous advisory council once it is formed
- **Date:** 2026-11-26
- **Amends:** [0019](0019-story-records.md) and [0063](0063-widen-stories-to-law-justice-and-governance.md), for sacred and ceremonial material; [charter](../charter.md), commitment 5, for published texts

## Context

Decision record 0019 set up story records and said a story "whose content is sacred or ceremonial is recorded, if at all, with no excerpt of that content and with access `restricted`."[^0019] Decision record 0063 widened stories to law, justice and governance and kept that rule.[^0063] The charter says "ceremonial, restricted or community-held knowledge is not added without the community's written approval, and is marked restricted if it is held at all."[^charter-5]

On Nov. 26, 2026 the lead author asked for more stories, "moreso into mythology and religion."[^ask] Asked which mythology and religious stories the Stories section should take in, the lead author chose "Everything in the public domain," the option described as "Any published myth or religious text, including ceremonial ones; this reverses the sacred-material rule and decision 0019." The other options were openly published myths with no ceremony (recommended), and that set plus myths written down by outside recorders such as the Bureau of American Ethnology.[^card] The lead author added: "these decisions and others will be reviewed by the council we are delineating."[^council]

## Decision

- **Scope.** A story record may hold any myth, origin story, religious teaching, prayer, song text or account of a ceremony that is in the public domain in the United States, whoever wrote it down. That includes texts that Native authors published and texts that outside recorders (ethnologists, missionaries, government agents) wrote down.
- **Labels.** Each telling says who told it and who recorded it. A text written down by an outside recorder is labeled as an outside recording (`authority` in the record), so readers can tell a nation's own published words from an outsider's account. A protocol that the source states (season, setting, clan ownership, ceremony) is recorded as before.
- **Unpublished knowledge is unchanged.** Ceremonial, restricted or community-held knowledge that has not been published, including anything sent to the project by a person, still needs the community's written approval under charter commitment 5.
- **Nations still control.** Every record is first marked for the nation's review. A nation may correct, restrict or remove any record under charter commitment 6, and its request wins.
- **Copyright.** Only texts in the public domain are shown in full: in practice, works published in the United States before 1931, and United States government works. Later works follow decision records 0034 and 0036.
- **Verification.** Full texts are quoted exactly as printed, checked word for word against independent copies and the page images of the printing.

## Consequences

- Charter commitment 5 now limits its written-approval rule to unpublished knowledge and points to this decision for published texts. The style guide and the pull request checklist say the same.
- The first records under this decision are in session [2026-11-27-myths-and-religion](../../research/sessions/2026/2026-11-27-myths-and-religion.md).
- The council, once formed, may confirm, narrow or reverse this decision. Records taken in under it are labeled, so a narrower rule can be applied by changing their access rather than finding them again.
