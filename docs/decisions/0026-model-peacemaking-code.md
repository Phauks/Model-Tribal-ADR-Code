# 0026. Write model peacemaking provisions as part of the project's model code

- **Status:** Accepted (lead author, Nov. 22, 2026)
- **Date:** 2026-11-22

## Context

The charter keeps peacemaking as its own track, and the first working outline
(`docs/outline.md`, version 0.1) followed the roadmap's earlier option of
code provisions that only connect a court to a nation's own peacemaking
forum.[^roadmap] On Nov. 22, 2026 the lead author said that the Native
American Rights Fund's words, which the charter then used as its reason, are
not the project's own words, and that part of the project's goal is to
create a model peacemaking code: "so we shall include it."

The research supports a peacemaking code that stops short of the process
itself (project analysis):

- Nations already legislate peacemaking. A pass over the codes in the dataset
  found 580 peacemaking provisions from 39 nations, and the Native American
  Rights Fund collects nations' enacted peacemaking laws.[^programs][^narf-laws]
- Those codes turn on the same points: where the forum sits (37 nations), how
  a case enters (32), return to court (27), how peacemakers are chosen (24)
  and agreements entered as orders (20).[^programs]
- The programs differ most in who serves and how sessions run, which rest on
  each nation's own tradition; the Navajo Nation, for example, directs its
  program to conform its procedures to Navajo concepts such as K'é and
  clanship.[^programs][^comparative]
- Leaving peacemaking out would leave nations without model safeguards the
  research flags, such as screening for domestic violence and protection of
  sacred teachings.[^programs][^evidence]

## Decision

- The project writes model peacemaking provisions with commentary, following
  the same drafting rules as the rest of the model code (style guide,
  section 4).
- They set up or recognize a nation's peacemaking forum, say how peacemakers
  are chosen, how matters come to and leave peacemaking, how agreements take
  effect, and how sacred and confidential matters are protected. They do not
  define how peacemaking is done; that is left to each nation's tradition.
- Peacemaking stays its own track: its provisions sit under their own heading,
  apart from the alternative dispute resolution provisions, and are never
  presented as a kind of mediation or arbitration. The charter's commitment 3
  is reworded to give the project's own reason.
- There is one model code. The lead author decided on Nov. 22, 2026 that it
  has divisions for mediation, arbitration, peacemaking and possibly
  negotiation, so peacemaking is a division of that code, not a separate
  code. Shared rules, such as confidentiality and the entry of agreements, sit
  in a general division, and each division can be enacted with the general
  division alone ([working outline](../outline.md)).
- No provision text is drafted before Gate 1 and Gate 2, as for every other
  part of the code.[^roadmap]

## Consequences

- The outline's Part II becomes one code in divisions, with peacemaking as
  its own division, and the roadmap's peacemaking scope question is closed.
- The book's working title, "Model Tribal ADR Code" in `pdf/book.yaml`, may
  need to change to name both tracks; that is a separate decision.
- Commentary on peacemaking provisions needs extra care under the charter's
  commitments 4 and 5: illustrations stay fictitious unless a nation approves,
  and no ceremonial or restricted knowledge is described.
