# 0067. Form an Indigenous Advisory Council, starting with a small unpaid group

- **Status:** Accepted (lead author, Nov. 26, 2026, 7:37 PM Pacific); the full council's powers and pay are still open
- **Date:** 2026-11-26

## Context

The charter adopts the CARE Principles, which hold that "Indigenous Peoples must be the ones to determine data governance protocols,"[^care] and says the project "does not present itself as an Indigenous voice" (commitment 10). Yet most decisions that touch Native people, such as replacing children's names in Tribal opinions (0057), showing estate cases (0060), picture rules for biographies and taking in ceremonial texts (0065), were made by the lead author alone. A nation can already correct, restrict or remove material about itself (commitment 6; decision records 0041 and 0056), but nobody speaks to choices that cut across many nations or concern no single nation.

On Nov. 26, 2026 the lead author asked for "a separete council or entity within the Tribal Law Project" that "would only be made up of indigenous ppls" and would review "items like redaction of names, pictures, and other decisions which we have made but should otherwise get approval for."[^ask] A proposal compared precedents (the CARE Principles, Local Contexts Labels and Notices, Mukurtu, the American Philosophical Society's protocols and its Native American Advisory Board, the Navajo Nation Human Research Review Board, NAGPRA consultation and Review Committee, the National Museum of the American Indian's trustees and Native Land Digital) and set out a full council with veto powers and paid members.[^proposal] The lead author chose to start smaller: "no money and less restrictions for the first council, the idea is there, but we don't have the resources and connections quite yet to fully implement."[^phase] He kept the name "Indigenous Advisory Council" and one safeguard (below), and left open whether the full council will be paid.

## Decision

The project forms an Indigenous Advisory Council in two phases. The plan is in [docs/indigenous-advisory-council.md](../indigenous-advisory-council.md).

- **First council (now).** Three to five Indigenous volunteers, invited by the lead author and Danielle Finn. Unpaid, and credited by name unless they ask not to be (charter commitment 7). Advice only: the council reviews the decisions listed in the plan and new decisions that affect Native people, and says ratify, change or reverse. The lead author decides, and adds a short note to the decision record when he does not follow the council's advice. No bylaws change, quorum rule or formal conflict policy yet; a member says when a matter is about their own nation, and that nation's own view is sought.
- **The safeguard.** Any member may ask that a sacred, ceremonial or privacy-sensitive item be held from public view while the council discusses it. The project holds it until the council has answered. This follows commitment 6's rule that material comes out of public view first and is reviewed second.
- **A nation's own decision comes first.** The council never speaks for a nation. A nation's request about its own material, through its government, designated office or review body, wins over the council's advice.
- **Full council (later).** When the project has the resources and the connections to ask nations' offices for nominations, a council of five to seven members replaces the first one, named in the nonprofit's bylaws, with consent over a written list of matters (sacred material, pictures of Native people, privacy of Native individuals, reuse of Native-created data and the charter's Native-facing commitments). Whether its members are paid is undecided.

## Consequences

- Decision records that touch Native people stay in force and are treated as interim until the council has reviewed them. New ones say that they await the council's review, as 0065 does.
- The nonprofit setup steps on the workspace's Nonprofit page gain "Invite the first Indigenous Advisory Council", and the bylaws step names the council.
- The charter is not changed yet. A commitment naming the council is added once members have agreed to serve, so the public page never describes a council that does not exist.
