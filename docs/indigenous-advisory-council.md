# Indigenous Advisory Council

**Status:** first council being formed; adopted by the lead author on Nov. 26, 2026 ([decision record 0067](decisions/0067-form-an-indigenous-advisory-council.md)). The full council's powers and pay are still open.

The Indigenous Advisory Council is a group made up only of Indigenous people. It reviews the project's decisions that affect Native people, such as replacing names, showing pictures and handling sacred material. It starts small and unpaid, and grows into a council with binding powers when the project has the resources and connections. A Native nation's own decision about its own material always comes before the council's advice.

## Phases

| | First council (now) | Full council (later) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Members | 3 to 5 Indigenous volunteers | 5 to 7 Indigenous members, no two from the same nation, from at least three regions |
| Chosen by | Invitation from the lead author and Danielle Finn | A Native nominating group convened by Danielle Finn, from nominations by nations' offices and Native organizations; then the council fills its own seats |
| Terms | Open | Three years, staggered |
| Pay | None; credited by name unless a member asks not to be | Undecided. If paid, a starting point is an annual honorarium of $1,000 per member and $250 per review outside meetings (project estimate, about $5,000 to $7,500 a year for five) |
| Powers | Advice only. The lead author decides, and notes on the decision record when he does not follow the council's advice | Consent (veto) over a written list; consultation on a second list; notice of a third (below) |
| Safeguard | Any member may ask that a sacred, ceremonial or privacy-sensitive item be held from public view while the council discusses it | The same, plus a protective default: while a consent matter waits, the more protective choice stays in force |
| Form | No bylaws change, quorum or formal conflict policy | Created by board resolution and named in the nonprofit's bylaws; conflict policy adapted from Appendix A of the [Instructions for Form 1023](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1023); members do not vote on their own nation's material |
| Meets | Online, as members choose; review by email between meetings | Regular meetings; ordinary requests answered within 30 days; any one member may grant an urgent hold |

The full council moves to its phase when the nonprofit exists and can fund it, and when nations' offices can be asked for nominations. Whether to pay its members is the lead author's open choice.

## Order of authority

1. The nation's own decision, through its government or designated office (charter commitment 6).
2. The nation's own review body, if it has one, such as the Navajo Nation Human Research Review Board.
3. The council, for matters that cross many nations, when a nation has not answered within 60 days, or when a nation asks it to advise. The 60 days follow the American Philosophical Society's protocols.
4. The lead author and the board, for everything else.

## Lists for the full council

| Power | Matters |
| --- | --- |
| Consent | Sacred, ceremonial and religious material, including myths and teachings; pictures of Native people, living or dead; privacy rules for Native individuals in hosted records; reuse of data or knowledge that Native people or organizations created, such as Native Land Digital; the charter's commitments 1, 4, 5, 6 and 10 |
| Consultation | Model code provisions that rest on custom or tradition, including the peacemaking track, at Gate 2; essays and book chapters about named nations; terminology, biographies and recognition labels; outreach to nations; the Native artist contest; uses of AI on Native material |
| Notice | New data collections about Native people; new kinds of nation pages; counts and kinds of nations' requests to correct, restrict or remove material |

The first council advises on all of these.

## Precedents

The design borrows from these models. NAGPRA and the Museum's statute bind federal bodies and museums, not this project; they are used here as designs (project analysis).

| Model | What the project borrows |
| --- | --- |
| [CARE Principles](https://datascience.codata.org/articles/10.5334/dsj-2020-043) | "Authority to control" as the test for what goes to the council ([decision record 0001](decisions/0001-adopt-care-principles.md)) |
| [Local Contexts Labels](https://localcontexts.org/labels/about-the-labels/) and [Notices](https://localcontexts.org/notices/about-the-notices/) | Labels are "tools for Indigenous communities"; Notices are "tools for institutions and researchers". The project and the council place Notices only, and leave Labels to communities |
| [Mukurtu](https://www.neh.gov/article/mukurtu-digital-platform-does-more-manage-content) | Cultural protocols, "standards indicating who should have access to what information under what conditions", as a design rule |
| [American Philosophical Society, *Protocols for the Treatment of Indigenous Materials*](https://www.amphilsoc.org/sites/default/files/2017-11/attachments/APS%20Protocols.pdf), 158 Proc. Am. Phil. Soc'y 411 (2014) | The closest fit: the tribe of origin decides first; when a tribe has not answered within 60 days, staff may "Contact the APS Native American Advisory Board" (at 417) |
| [Navajo Nation Human Research Review Board](https://www.nptao.arizona.edu/sites/default/files/procedural_guidelines_for_pi.pdf) | A nation's own review body, which approves reports and manuscripts before publication, outranks the council |
| [43 C.F.R. § 10.1(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-43/subtitle-A/part-10/subpart-A/section-10.1) | "Deference to the Native American traditional knowledge" of those consulted |
| [25 U.S.C. § 3006(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/25/3006) | The NAGPRA Review Committee reserves seats for "traditional Indian religious leaders"; members "serve without pay" but are reimbursed |
| [20 U.S.C. § 80q-3](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/80q-3) | The Museum's trustees, "At least 12 of the 23" Indians, hold "sole authority" over "criteria for use of the collections", including "method of display" |
| [Native Land Digital](https://native-land.ca/about/) | An Indigenous-led board whose terms bar storing or redistributing its data without permission |
