# Petition to change a name

**{{court.name}}**, {{nation.name}}

Form `petition-to-change-a-name`, version 0.1.0. A court may adopt or change this form under its own law. If your court has its own form for this, use the court's form.

## Before you start

- Use this form to change your own name (adult) or your child's name (minor).
- Attach: a photo ID, a birth certificate for the person whose name will change, and proof of enrollment or descent if the court asks for it.
- {{court.publication_rule}}
- If publishing your name or address would put you at risk, check the safety box in section 5. The court can decide not to publish.
- Filing fee: {{court.fee_name_change}}. If you cannot pay, ask for the fee waiver form.
- Court staff can explain the steps but cannot give legal advice.

## Notes for courts adapting this form

These notes are not part of the form. They explain where each part came from.

- **What the forms share.** Six of the seven courts' materials ask for the present name, the new name, the reason, enrollment status and the person's residence. Five ask the person to state the change is not to avoid debts or crimes, five require a sworn or notarized petition, and five require publication (comparison of the forms listed in `based_on`, read Nov. 23, 2026).
- **Adult and child on one form.** Courts split these into two packets (an adult form at Ponca, Puyallup and the Southern Plains Court of Indian Offenses; a child form at Acoma, Sisseton Wahpeton and Wyandotte). One form with a child section is the project's choice, to cut paperwork.
- **Safety and publication.** Puyallup can exempt people in a victim or witness protection program or an address-confidentiality program from publication. The model offers that to anyone at risk and lets the judge decide.
- **Who may file.** Puyallup limits name changes to enrolled adult members; Acoma charges members and non-members different fees. That is a jurisdiction choice for the court's code, so the form only asks for enrollment status.
- **Child support.** Wyandotte's form names "child support orders" among the debts a name change must not avoid. The model keeps that wording.
- **Child's view.** None of the name change forms asks for the child's view; the guardianship forms of Little River Band and Nottawaseppi Huron Band let a child 14 or older nominate a guardian. The model asks for the child's view at `court.child_consent_age`; this is the project's choice.
- **Sex offender statement.** Among the name change forms, only Wyandotte asks for it: "The Petitioner is not a sex offender who is subject to any registration requirements." Four guardianship forms ask a similar question (Kaw, Miami, Ponca, Wyandotte). The model keeps it because a name change can be used to avoid registration; a court whose law does not require it can delete the box.
- **Other cases.** No name change form asks about other cases; six guardianship forms ask about other cases about the child (Kaw, Little River Band, Miami, Nottawaseppi Huron Band, Ponca, Puyallup). The model asks so the judge learns of a conflicting order; this is the project's choice.
