# Request to waive court fees

**{{court.name}}**, {{nation.name}}

Form `request-to-waive-court-fees`, version 0.1.0. A court may adopt or change this form under its own law. If your court has its own form for this, use the court's form.

## Before you start

- Use this form if you cannot afford the court's filing or other fees. File it with your other papers.
- If you get public benefits, check that box in section 2 and attach proof. You can then skip section 3.
- Your answers are used only to decide this request.
- If your money situation gets better while the case is open, you must tell the court.
- Court staff can explain the steps but cannot give legal advice.

## Notes for courts adapting this form

These notes are not part of the form. They explain where each part came from.

- **What the twelve forms share.** All twelve courts' forms name the applicant and the fees, and eleven ask for total income and have a signature line. Ten ask about public benefits, household size and the reason the person cannot pay, and ten have an oath or perjury statement (comparison of the forms listed in `based_on`, read Nov. 23, 2026).
- **Benefits shortcut.** Leech Lake tells benefit recipients to "skip to the signature line," and Kickapoo lets Social Security recipients skip the affidavit. The model uses that shortcut because it saves the person and the court time.
- **Income threshold.** Leech Lake and Lummi use 125 percent of the federal poverty guidelines; Habematolel Pomo of Upper Lake waives 80 percent of the fee in one income band. The model leaves the standard to the court's rules and offers a partial waiver in the order.
- **Duty to report changes.** Five forms say the person must report better finances or repay (Central Council Tlingit and Haida, Ho-Chunk, Kickapoo, Nottawaseppi Huron Band, Pyramid Lake). The oath includes it.
- **Per capita payments.** Three forms count per capita payments as income (Ho-Chunk, Nottawaseppi Huron Band, Puyallup), and two let the court garnish them for unpaid fees. The model lists them as income only; garnishment belongs in the court's code.
- **Trust land.** The model leaves trust land out of property, because a person usually cannot sell it to pay a fee. This is the project's choice; none of the forms says so.
- **Deadline after denial.** Kickapoo gives 10 days to pay after a denial; Lummi and Pyramid Lake hold the filing until the fee is paid. The model uses `court.fee_payment_days`.
- **Expiry.** Pyramid Lake waivers expire after 30 days and Leech Lake orders after one year. The order has a blank end date.
- **Appeal.** None of the thirteen files says how to ask the court to reconsider a denial. A court adopting this form should add its rule.
