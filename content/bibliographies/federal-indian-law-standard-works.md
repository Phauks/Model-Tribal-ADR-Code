# Federal Indian law and Tribal justice: standard works

This list names the treatises, histories and source collections that stand behind Part I, Foundations. It is meant for a reader who wants to go past the timeline and its cases to the books that lawyers, judges and scholars in the field rely on.

How to read an entry:

- Each entry is a source record, cited by its key. The record holds the full citation, where the book was confirmed (usually a law library catalog), and how to get a copy.
- The note after each entry says what the work is. A sentence marked "(project analysis)" is the project's own judgment of why it matters here.
- Inclusion is not endorsement. Several of these works are written from the federal government's side, and the list notes where an author writes as a Native scholar so a reader can weigh perspectives.
- Most entries were confirmed in a library catalog but not yet read for this project. Each record says so. A claim in the Foundations essays rests on the pages it cites, not on this list.

## Treatises and casebooks

- [@cohens-handbook-of-federal-indian-law-2024]. The standard treatise, now edited by Nell Jessup Newton and Kevin K. Washburn and published by LexisNexis. (project analysis) The first place to check any statement of doctrine in the timeline.
- [@cohen-handbook-of-federal-indian-law-1942]. Felix S. Cohen's original 1942 edition, prepared in the Interior Department. It is a U.S. government work and free to read. (project analysis) A primary source for the Reorganization era as well as a treatise.
- [@fletcher-federal-indian-law-2016]. Matthew L.M. Fletcher's hornbook, a single-volume survey by a Native scholar.
- [@canby-american-indian-law-in-a-nutshell-8th-2025]. William C. Canby Jr.'s short guide, in its eighth edition.
- [@getches-cases-and-materials-on-federal-indian-law-8th-2025]. The leading casebook, now in its eighth edition.
- [@ali-2022-restatement-law-of-american-indians]. The American Law Institute's Restatement of the field.
- [@pevar-rights-of-indians-and-tribes-5th-2024]. A question-and-answer guide that began as an ACLU handbook. (project analysis) The most readable entry point for a general reader.
- [@duthu-american-indians-and-the-law-2008]. A short introduction for general readers by a Native scholar.

## Histories of federal policy

- [@prucha-great-father-1984]. Francis Paul Prucha's two-volume history of federal Indian policy. (project analysis) The standard narrative to set beside the timeline's era boundaries.
- [@deloria-lytle-nations-within-1984]. Vine Deloria Jr. and Clifford M. Lytle on the Indian Reorganization Act and its aftermath.
- [@wilkinson-american-indians-time-and-the-law-1987]. Charles F. Wilkinson on treaties and sovereignty in the modern Supreme Court.
- [@wilkinson-blood-struggle-2005]. Wilkinson's history of Native nations from termination to the early 2000s.
- [@wilkins-lomawaima-uneven-ground-2001]. David E. Wilkins and K. Tsianina Lomawaima on the doctrines of federal Indian law, written from Native perspectives.
- [@wilkins-american-indian-sovereignty-and-the-supreme-court-1997]. Wilkins's critical reading of the Supreme Court's sovereignty cases.
- [@pommersheim-broken-landscape-2009]. Frank Pommersheim's constitutional history of the field.
- [@williams-american-indian-in-western-legal-thought-1990]. Robert A. Williams Jr. on the European legal ideas behind the discovery doctrine.
- [@barsh-henderson-the-road-1980]. Russel Lawrence Barsh and James Youngblood Henderson's early theory of treaty federalism.

## Treaties

- [@kappler-indian-affairs-laws-and-treaties-vol-2-1904]. Charles J. Kappler's compilation of the treaties, free online. (project analysis) The source the timeline cites for treaty texts.
- [@deloria-demallie-documents-of-american-indian-diplomacy-1999]. Vine Deloria Jr. and Raymond J. DeMallie's collection of treaties and agreements from 1775 to 1979.
- [@prucha-american-indian-treaties-1994]. Prucha's history of treaty-making.
- [@williams-linking-arms-together-1997]. Williams on treaty relations from 1600 to 1800 as Native nations understood them. (project analysis) The scholarly ground for reading early treaty clauses on injuries and complaints as peace-keeping terms.

## Tribal courts and Tribal law

- [@deloria-lytle-american-indians-american-justice-1983]. Deloria and Lytle's study of Native justice systems and Tribal courts. (project analysis) The standard early history of Tribal courts.
- [@hagan-indian-police-and-judges]. William T. Hagan's history of the Indian police and the Courts of Indian Offenses.
- [@fletcher-american-indian-tribal-law-2d-2020]. Fletcher's casebook on the law of Native nations themselves, built from Tribal court decisions.
- [@richland-deer-introduction-to-tribal-legal-studies-3d-2016]. Justin B. Richland and Sarah Deer's textbook on Tribal legal systems.
- [@pommersheim-braid-of-feathers-1995]. Pommersheim on Tribal courts and Tribal life.
- [@pommersheim-tribal-justice-2016]. Pommersheim's reflections from twenty-five years as a Tribal appellate justice.
- [@austin-navajo-courts-and-navajo-common-law-2009]. Former Navajo Nation Supreme Court Justice Raymond D. Austin on how Navajo courts apply Navajo common law.
- [@nielsen-zion-2005-navajo-nation-peacemaking]. Essays on Navajo Nation peacemaking, edited by Marianne O. Nielsen and James W. Zion.

## The leading cases in depth

- [@goldberg-washburn-frickey-indian-law-stories-2011]. Essays on the history behind leading cases, edited by Carole Goldberg, Kevin K. Washburn and Philip P. Frickey.
- [@robertson-conquest-by-law-2005]. Lindsay G. Robertson's archival history of Johnson v. M'Intosh.
- [@norgren-cherokee-cases-2004]. Jill Norgren's history of Cherokee Nation v. Georgia and Worcester v. Georgia.
- [@harring-crow-dogs-case-1994]. Sidney L. Harring on Ex parte Crow Dog and Tribal law in the nineteenth century.

## Native Hawaiian law and the territories

Native Hawaiians and the Indigenous peoples of the territories are not Tribes; these works are listed because their legal history runs beside the Tribal one.

- [@mackenzie-native-hawaiian-law-treatise-2015]. The standard treatise on Native Hawaiian law, edited by Melody Kapilialoha MacKenzie with Susan K. Serrano and D. Kapuaʻala Sproat.
- [@osorio-dismembering-lahui-2002]. Jonathan Kay Kamakawiwoʻole Osorio's history of the Hawaiian Kingdom's constitutional change to 1887.
- [@merry-colonizing-hawaii-2000]. Sally Engle Merry on how the Kingdom's courts took up Anglo-American law.
- [@silva-aloha-betrayed-2004]. Noenoe K. Silva's history of Kanaka Maoli resistance to annexation, drawn from Hawaiian-language sources.
- [@van-dyke-who-owns-the-crown-lands-of-hawaii-2008]. Jon M. Van Dyke's legal history of the Crown lands.
- [@kauanui-paradoxes-of-hawaiian-sovereignty-2018]. J. Kēhaulani Kauanui on Hawaiian sovereignty and the debate over federal recognition.
- [@neuman-brown-nagin-reconsidering-the-insular-cases-2015]. Essays on the Insular Cases and the constitutional status of the territories.

## Primary-source collections

- [@prucha-documents-of-united-states-indian-policy-3d-2000]. Prucha's one-volume collection of federal Indian policy documents.
- [@supreme-court-library-dates-of-decisions-1791-1882]. The Supreme Court Library's table of decision dates for the early U.S. Reports, which do not print them.
