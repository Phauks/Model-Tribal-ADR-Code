# Court and peacemaking media from Native nations: candidates

Search date: November 23, 2026. Status: candidates for the lead author's review. Nothing here is on the website, and no media file was downloaded.

Trevor chose this search on November 23, 2026, over a search for a picture of every nation ([recommendation](2026-11-23-per-nation-pictures-recommendation.md)). It looked for videos, sound recordings and pictures made by or about the courts, peacemaking programs and wellness courts of the 61 nations whose profiles record a peacemaking forum, and swept the web for any nation's court or peacemaking channel. How it was done is in the [session note](../sessions/2026/2026-11-23-court-and-peacemaking-media.md); every place looked is in each nation's [court-media search log](../search-logs/court-media/).

## What was found

Of the 61 nations, 19 have at least one candidate, 27 had nothing on the pages read, and 15 are only partly searched because their sites refused automated requests. The search found 21 candidates for those nations and 2 more from the sweep.

Most of the useful video is talks and short films made by organizations working with the nations (the Native American Rights Fund, the National American Indian Court Judges Association, the Native Nations Institute), not by the nations themselves. Only a few nations publish court media of their own (project analysis of the table below). The Chickasaw Nation has a Chickasaw TV episode on its Peacemaking Court, the Choctaw Nation of Oklahoma a tour of its Judicial Center, and the Salt River Pima-Maricopa Indian Community a Law Day video from its news service; several more show photos on their court pages.

The one public-domain find is the Office for Victims of Crime's film on the Leech Lake Tribal Court, which comes with a transcript and a download link. The office's reuse policy says "In general, OVC's content—text, video, and audio—is not copyrighted" ([OVC reuse policy](https://ovc.ojp.gov/reuse-policy)). It is part of a seven-film series, "A Circle of Healing for Native Children Endangered by Drugs", which also covers the Wind River Tribal Youth Program and the Santee Sioux Nation ([series list](https://ovc.ojp.gov/library/multimedia/list?series_filter=A%20Circle%20of%20Healing%20for%20Native%20Children%20Endangered%20by%20Drugs)). The film shows children and families talking about addiction, so it needs care (see its row).

## How to read the table

"Embed only" means a YouTube video whose page reserves all rights or shows no license: the website may show it in the YouTube player (youtube-nocookie) but may not keep a copy. "Permission needed" means a picture or video on a site that reserves its rights, or a Vimeo video, which the Video block cannot play: ask the owner, or link to it. "Checked" means the second pass opened the page or the YouTube listing on November 23, 2026 and quoted the rights line; rights can change, so check again before use. Sensitivity follows the [media survey](2026-11-23-media-source-survey.md).

## Left out

Ceremonies, songs and prayers were left out wherever the search saw them, among them a smudging picture on the Coos, Lower Umpqua and Siuslaw court page and a Pokagon Band song program. So were powwow, tourism, casino and general culture videos (on the Eastern Shoshone, Omaha, Northern Arapaho, Chitimacha, Sokaogon and Cow Creek pages), state court videos that only mention a nation, livestreams of hearings, and listings of sealed juvenile and child welfare matters. A PBS Wisconsin video of a Stockbridge-Munsee clan mother using circles with her family is about family practice, not a court, and was left out.

## What a person needs to do

Fifteen nations' pages refused automated requests or did not answer; each one's search log says which page to open in a browser. Before anything goes on the website, check the rights line again on the item's own page, watch the video for ceremony, children or people who have passed on, and send the permission requests listed in part c of the [media survey](2026-11-23-media-source-survey.md). For the pictures on nations' own court pages and the Choctaw Nation's tour, ask the nation's court; for the Native Nations Institute talks, ask the Institute.
