# State statutes sweep, New England and Mid-Atlantic: session notes

The method is the one in the first sweep's notes (research/sessions/2026/2026-11-27-state-statutes-sweep.md) and in `scripts/law/state_statutes/README.md`, with the readers' wrapper prompt in `research/state-statutes/reader-prompt.md` (the four states' notes are added there) and the spot check `scripts/law/state_statutes/spot_check.py`. Each state's results are in `research/state-statutes/<state>/`. All four states are light, as the fifty-state survey expected: 295 Indian law sections in all, 291 of them new records (four were already held by hand and were left as they were).

## Vermont

The General Assembly prints each chapter in full on one page; a title's page lists its chapters. Sections are numbered within a title, so the adapter numbers them `<title>:<section>` and cites them "Vt. Stat. Ann. tit. 1, § 853a", as Delaware's adapter does; the appendix titles (executive orders and municipal charters) number sections within each chapter and are cited "Vt. Stat. Ann. tit. 24 app. ch. 3, § 101". The Uniform Commercial Code title prints its numbers with an em dash ("9—101"), which the adapter reads as a hyphen. 24,980 sections, 21,897 in force (a section printed in two versions, one effective until a date and one after, keeps its first); 44 strong and 132 weak-only hits; 52 Indian law sections (13 core, 39 incidental). The core sections are the state recognition chapter (1 V.S.A. ch. 23: the Vermont Commission on Native American Affairs and the recognition of the Elnu Abenaki Tribe, the Nulhegan Band of the Coosuk Abenaki Nation, the Koasek Abenaki of the Koas and the Missisquoi, St. Francis-Sokoki Band), Abenaki place names in state parks (10 V.S.A. § 2613), unmarked burial sites (18 V.S.A. § 5212b), tribal notarial acts and the state's Indian Child Welfare Act sections. Title 1, § 853a was already held by hand (`vermont-statutes-1-853a`) and was left as it was. The readers marked no strong hit none. The near sample's four yes verdicts were all in the Truth and Reconciliation Commission chapter (1 V.S.A. ch. 25), whose subject is discrimination against several populations, Native Americans among them; the session kept the establishing section (§ 902) incidental and settled the four sections that name no Native term as none, so no miss. Of the 40 verdicts read at random the session settled two as none: the business day definition that only lists Indigenous Peoples' Day (8 V.S.A. § 11201, as South Dakota's session held for § 37-24-1) and an advisory council whose membership should reflect diversity (33 V.S.A. § 3205, as Minnesota's session held for § 15B.36).

## New Jersey

The Legislature publishes the whole of the statutes as one plain-text file, updated every weekday, and the adapter reads it, so the sweep made no request per section. The statutes database on the site (a Folio gateway) has no stable address for one section, so each record links to that database's search for the section's number, which lists the section first. The file prints no chapter names, so pool E (non-hits in a chapter named for Indian law) is empty; the near sample stands in for it. 56,068 sections, 55,921 in force; 34 strong and 505 weak-only hits (mostly "nation", "reservation" and "allotment" in other senses); 39 Indian law sections (9 core, 30 incidental). The near sample found the five sections of the act creating the New Jersey Commission on American Indian Affairs (P.L.1995, c.295, N.J. Stat. Ann. §§ 52:16A-53 to 52:16A-59) that name no Native term; the session settled them core with the act. Section 52:16A-53 was already held by hand and was left as it was. Most incidental sections are minority business definitions that name American Indians and definitions of "State" that include tribes. The readers marked three strong hits none, and the session agreed with all three: a STEM program's findings that cite workforce statistics (§ 18A:3B-99) and two census data sections whose Native term is "Native Hawaiian" (§§ 52:4-6 and 52:4-7). The 40 verdicts read at random all agreed.

## Pennsylvania

The General Assembly's viewer loads each title of the Consolidated Statutes as one page, with a marker before every section and its notes and the title's table of contents linking each section's own page. Chapter headings fall inside the preceding section's block, so the adapter cuts each block at the first centered heading. A section's enactment comes from the session law at the head of its own source note, or else from the chapter's "Enactment." note. 13,188 sections, 12,397 in force; 16 strong and 107 weak-only hits; 16 Indian law sections (2 core, 14 incidental): the child custody act's application to Native American tribes (23 Pa. Cons. Stat. § 5404) and tribal notarial acts (57 Pa. Cons. Stat. § 312) are core, and the rest are definitions that include tribes or tribal courts. Much of Pennsylvania's law is still in unconsolidated acts, which the General Assembly publishes act by act rather than section by section; they are not read here. The readers marked one strong hit none, the Historical and Museum Commission's power to excavate the remains of Native American, colonial and later cultures (37 Pa. Cons. Stat. § 302); the session agreed. Neither recall sample found a miss, and the 40 verdicts read at random all agreed.

## Connecticut

The General Assembly's site sends its certificate without the issuing intermediate (Go Daddy Secure Certificate Authority - G2), which is why the fifty-state survey could not reach it; the adapter downloads that certificate from Go Daddy's repository and trusts it, as a browser does. The chapter pages hold the revision of Jan. 1, 2025; the 2026 Supplement prints only the sections changed or added in 2025, and the adapter puts each in place of its older text and links the record to the supplement's page. Editorial notes printed before some catchlines ("(Formerly Sec. 17-314a).", "*(See end of section for ...)") are left out of the titles. 30,055 sections, 25,067 in force; 162 strong and 288 weak-only hits; 188 Indian law sections (96 core, 92 incidental), the most of the four. The core sections include chapter 824 on Indians (General Statutes §§ 47-59a to 47-66j, of which § 47-59a was already held by hand and left as it was), the Connecticut Indian Child Welfare Act, the Mashantucket Pequot and Mohegan Fund and the gaming, keno and sports wagering agreements with the two tribes, and the chapter on Native American cultures and archaeological preserves, where pool E found two sections with no Native term. One reader skipped an answer for § 53-278g; the session settled it incidental (the gambling law's exceptions let the two tribes possess gaming equipment). The readers marked no strong hit none, neither recall sample found a miss, and the 40 verdicts read at random all agreed.

## New Hampshire and West Virginia

Both legislatures' sites (gc.nh.gov/rsa and code.wvlegislature.gov) reset the connection during the handshake from cloud sessions, as in the fifty-state survey. They are not read in this session; Trevor chose to fetch them through Cloudflare, which a separate thread is doing.
