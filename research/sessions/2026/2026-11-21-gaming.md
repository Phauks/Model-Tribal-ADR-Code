## What was done

Compacts: scraped all 48 pages of the Office of Indian Gaming compacts list with curl (bia.gov did not block curl this time), parsed state, tribe column, title, document link, Federal Register link, status and date for 1,196 rows, then turned the 665 linked Federal Register documents into volume-page citations through the Federal Register API. Rows without a link were matched to a citation only when exactly one 'Indian Gaming' notice was published on the listed date. Names were matched to roster ids by normalized exact match, a reviewed list of spelling variants, and a scored fuzzy match with manual review of every non-exact match; where the list's Tribe column and the compact title disagreed the title was used. Ordinances: parsed the NIGC gaming ordinances table (321 rows) and recorded the original ordinance date and every amendment date with their document links. Operations: the NIGC Gaming Tribe Report PDFs were only reachable through the page-fetch tool, which returned partial machine-extracted text, so gaming-operation facts are medium confidence and partial. Dispute resolution: downloaded one current or representative compact per state from the Office of Indian Gaming list, extracted the text with pdftotext, read the dispute-resolution section and wrote a one-sentence neutral summary with the section locator. The summary is attached to the representative nation's own compact fact (cited to that compact document), and repeated for each other nation with an approved or deemed-approved compact in that state, noted 'state model compact; nation-specific text not checked', confidence medium.

`research/raw/2026-11-21/gaming.json`: 2822 facts about 318 nations or communities from 35 sources. What the build kept and dropped is in `research/raw/2026-11-21/build-report.json`.

## Review by the lead session

- The Office of Indian Gaming list names a different nation in its Tribe column than in the compact title in about 50 rows (for example Lumbee shown for the Mashantucket Pequot procedures). The assistant used the nation named in the compact title, set confidence to medium and explained each case in the note. This is the assistant's reading of the list, not a correction published by the Office.
- The list records 31 disapprovals and 26 sets of Secretarial procedures. The schema's compact `status` now allows `disapproved` and `secretarial-procedures` so these federal actions are kept rather than dropped (25 U.S.C. § 2710(d)(7)(B)(vii), (d)(8)(B); 25 C.F.R. part 291).
- `approved` is the date the list shows, often the Federal Register publication date, not the signing date. No compact is marked `in-effect` or `expired`, because the list does not say.
- 256 dispute-resolution summaries are copied from one representative compact per state and say so in the note (medium confidence). Nation-specific text was not checked for those.
- Gaming operation facts cover only part of the alphabet, because the report PDFs could not be read in full. A nation without an operation fact may still operate gaming.
- The six Minnesota Chippewa Tribe bands hold their own compacts and ordinances. They are attached to `minnesota-chippewa-tribe` with the band named in each note until the bands get records of their own.
