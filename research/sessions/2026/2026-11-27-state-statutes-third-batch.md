# State statutes sweep, third batch: session notes

The method is the one in the first sweep's notes (research/sessions/2026/2026-11-27-state-statutes-sweep.md) and in `scripts/law/state_statutes/README.md`. Two helpers that the earlier sessions kept only in their work folders are now in the repository: the readers' wrapper prompt (`research/state-statutes/reader-prompt.md`, with the state notes each state's readers were given) and the spot check (`scripts/law/state_statutes/spot_check.py`, which checks that every batch line has an answer, prints 40 random verdicts with the words around each section's first term, and with `--misses` lists the recall samples' yes verdicts). Each state's results are in `research/state-statutes/<state>/`.

Three adapters (South Dakota, Kansas, Idaho), like Minnesota's, take a section's enactment year only from a session law at the head of its history line: a line that opens with an older code ("SDC 1939, § 16.1903", "C.C.P. 1881, sec. 17") names only later amendments, so those sections carry no year. The chapter-name test for pool E (`CHAPTER_LAW` in core.py) now also matches "Tribally", after North Dakota's recall sample found an unflagged section of the chapter on tribally controlled community colleges.

## South Dakota

The Legislature's site is a script application over a statutes service that answers one call per chapter with every section's text and source note; each record links to the section's page. 37,252 sections, 35,660 in force; 209 strong and 217 weak-only hits; 262 Indian law sections (126 core, 136 incidental), fewer than the pilot's estimate of 400 to 1,000 for the state. Five Public Law 280 and tribal court sections of chapter 1-1 (S.D. Codified Laws §§ 1-1-12, 1-1-13, 1-1-18, 1-1-21 and 1-1-25) were already held by hand and were left as they were. The readers marked 23 strong hits none. The session agreed with 16 (15 county boundaries traced along reservation lines, and the business day definition in § 37-24-1, which only lists Native American Day among the holidays) and settled seven as incidental: Native Americans' Day as a legal holiday (§ 1-5-1) and six sections giving the Indian Health Service or the Bureau of Indian Affairs a role (a tax exemption for meals it pays for, § 10-45-14.6; school contracts with the Bureau, § 13-15-3; health costs of paroled inmates, § 24-15A-58; aid to be used before state aid, § 28-1-18; its review as accreditation of treatment facilities, §§ 34-20A-2 and 34-20A-44), as Alaska's session did. The near sample found two incidental sections with no Native term (§ 1-24-25, the term of a high school equivalency testing agreement with a tribe, and § 13-49-42, the foreign funding report whose definition leaves out tribes), under 1%. The session read chapter 1-1 whole and settled § 1-1-21 (the governor's proclamation needed before the state's Indian country jurisdiction takes effect) as core with the rest of the act. The 40 verdicts read at random all agreed, apart from two strong hits the review had already settled.

## Minnesota

The Revisor prints each chapter in full on one page, keeping the numbers of repealed sections, so 54,706 sections hold 28,290 in force. Catchlines are printed in capitals; the adapter gives them title case, with small words and a list of acronyms (HIV, MFIP, SNAP and the like) kept as they should be. 650 strong and 599 weak-only hits; 841 Indian law sections (179 core, 662 incidental), the most incidental of any state so far, because Minnesota's human services, child welfare, health and education chapters name Tribes and Tribal Nations throughout. The readers marked eight strong hits none. The session settled three as incidental (American Indians as a criterion for environmental justice areas, § 116.07; the minority group definition, § 116J.8751; the immunity of Tribal governments that administer the elderly waiver, § 256S.01) and agreed with five (Ethnic American Day, § 10.50; a committee's members with knowledge of Native American history, § 15B.36; a federal cost circular's title, § 165.15; hiring outreach at an American Indian organization, §§ 473.754 and 473J.12). Neither recall sample found a miss, and the 40 verdicts read at random all agreed. The Revisor has claimed copyright in its catchlines, numbering and history notes, though not the statute text (Copyright Office Review Board letter of Aug. 3, 2017, cited in the pilot); the records use each catchline as the section's title, as for every other state.

## North Dakota

The Century Code is published as one PDF per chapter; the Legislative Branch's Century Code page lists every section's number and name with a link into its chapter's PDF, and the adapter splits each PDF at those numbers in order. The PDFs print no source notes, so the records carry no enactment year and the near sample is drawn only from sections printed next to a strong hit. 29,116 sections listed, 25,326 in force; nine listed sections are not printed in their chapter's PDF and are left out. 208 strong and 232 weak-only hits; 281 Indian law sections (118 core, 163 incidental). The readers marked nine strong hits none. The session settled three as incidental (the Bureau of Indian Education's student information system accepted for schools, § 15.1-07-33, and Bureau of Indian Affairs contract money counted as revenue by political subdivisions, §§ 21-02-01 and 21-13-01) and agreed with six (four county boundaries, the coat of arms, § 54-41-02, and a riverbed survey segment that begins at the Fort Berthold boundary, § 61-33.1-03). The near sample's two core verdicts were one true miss (§ 54-60.2-04, in the chapter on grants to tribally controlled community colleges, which led to the "Tribally" change above) and one reader's error (§ 54-01-09.3, the retrocession of federal park jurisdiction, settled none), so one miss in 300. The 40 verdicts read at random all agreed.

## Kansas

The Revisor's chapter pages list every section with its catchline, including repealed and transferred ones, which are not downloaded. 41,495 sections, 26,552 in force; 104 strong and 226 weak-only hits; 126 Indian law sections (40 core, 86 incidental). The core sections are mostly the tribal-state gaming and sports wagering compact laws and the Tribal Gaming Oversight Act (Kan. Stat. Ann. §§ 74-9801 to 74-9809), the cigarette tax compacts with the four resident tribes (the Prairie Band Potawatomi Nation, the Kickapoo Tribe, the Iowa Tribe of Kansas and Nebraska and the Sac and Fox Nation, §§ 79-2984 to 79-2987), full faith and credit for tribal courts (§ 60-3020), the Indian Child Welfare Act's application (§ 38-2203) and conveyances of state land to tribes. The readers were told that many county and city names in Kansas are nations' names. They marked three strong hits none; the session agreed with two (the great seal and the house seal, whose designs show Native Americans, §§ 75-201 and 46-162) and settled one as incidental (American Indian/Alaskan Native as a racial group for HIV reporting, § 65-6001, as the earlier states held for race categories with legal effect). Neither recall sample found a miss, and the 40 verdicts read at random all agreed.
