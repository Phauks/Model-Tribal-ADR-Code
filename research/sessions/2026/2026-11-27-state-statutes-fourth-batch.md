# State statutes sweep, fourth batch: session notes

The method is the one in the first sweep's notes (research/sessions/2026/2026-11-27-state-statutes-sweep.md), the third batch's notes (research/sessions/2026/2026-11-27-state-statutes-third-batch.md) and `scripts/law/state_statutes/README.md`. Each state's results are in `research/state-statutes/<state>/`; the readers' state notes are in `research/state-statutes/reader-prompt.md`.

## Colorado

The free online code is LexisNexis's, which does not take scripts, but the Office of Legislative Legal Services publishes each year's code for download in PDF, Word and HTML. The adapter reads the 2026 HTML files. Each title's file prints its tables of contents before its sections, and the contents lines look like section headings; the adapter takes a heading for a section only when its next line is not another heading, and when a number is printed twice (a contents line before a part heading, or a section with one version in force until a date and another from that date) it keeps the first printing with a source note. Enactment years come only from a session law at the head of the source note ("L. 76: Entire article added" is 1976; "Source: G.L. § 1; ... CRS 53" gives no year); the act key is the bill number when the note names one, otherwise the session law's year and page. Each record links to its title's PDF on the same download page, since the HTML and PDF files have no section anchors. 35,377 sections, 32,036 in force; 326 strong and 418 weak-only hits; 403 Indian law sections (126 core, 277 incidental). The core sections include the Colorado Indian Child Welfare Act (Colo. Rev. Stat. art. 19-1.2), the Commission of Indian Affairs (art. 24-44), the rules for construing state laws as applied to the Southern Ute Indian Tribe and the Ute Mountain Ute Tribe (parts 5 and 6 of article 2-4), the Native American arts and crafts act (art. 6-15), the missing and murdered Indigenous relatives office (§§ 24-33.5-2601 to 24-33.5-2606) and the tribal sales tax exemption (§ 39-26-727). "Pueblo" is a city and county in Colorado, so the adapter takes out its common place phrases before the weak terms match and the readers were told; the weak-only pool was still the largest of any state so far.

The readers marked ten strong hits none. The session agreed with six (two boundary descriptions along the Southern Ute and Ute reservations, §§ 3-1-116 and 30-5-128; three legislative declarations that only describe Native people or federal aid to tribes, §§ 24-75-229, 25-4-2201 and 33-9-201; and an air toxics study that compares programs on tribal lands, § 25-7-109.5) and settled four as incidental: two reports that must count American Indians as a race category (§§ 13-3-101 and 24-21-114), a committee seat for a Native American-serving institution (§ 23-1-144) and a member chosen by the counties affected by tribal gaming (§ 44-30-1302). The near sample found one miss with no Native term, § 14-2-110 (the marriage prohibition's exception for the established customs of aboriginal cultures), one in 300; the random sample found none. The 40 verdicts read at random all agreed.

## Michigan

The Legislature prints each chapter of the Compiled Laws whole on one page, act by act; chapter 1 is the state constitution and is not read. Each section sits inside the public act that enacted it, so that act is the section's act key; enactment years come from the head of the history line ("1972, Act 195" or "Add. 2012, Act 167"), and a line that opens with an older code ("R.S. 1846, Ch. 2") gives no year. Each record links to the section's own page. 41,824 sections, 38,907 in force; 160 strong and 309 weak-only hits; 214 Indian law sections (52 core, 162 incidental). The core sections include the Indian Affairs Commission (Mich. Comp. Laws §§ 16.711 to 16.715 and 16.719), the Indian tuition waiver (§§ 390.1251 to 390.1253), Indian housing authorities (§§ 125.1551 to 125.1555), the Office of the Tribal Legislative Liaison (§§ 4.791 to 4.794), the casino and internet gaming laws' tribal parts and the tax agreements with tribes (§§ 205.30c and 205.94x).

The readers marked one strong hit none, the boxing law's exemption for the Native American Sports Council (§ 338.3612), and the session agreed: the council is a private sports body and the section gives no tribe a role. The session also settled one weak hit as none: § 206.506's "Indian Veteran" is a veteran of the Indian wars. The near sample's one yes verdict was a true miss with no Native term, § 324.76102 (the state's reservation of aboriginal records and antiquities, including burial sites), one in 300; the random sample found none. The 40 verdicts read at random all agreed.

## Wyoming

The Legislative Service Office publishes each title of the Wyoming Statutes as one PDF; the adapter splits each at the section numbers that start an indented line and reads chapter and article names from their capitalized headings. The PDFs print no history lines, so the records carry no enactment year and the near sample is drawn only from sections printed next to a strong hit. Each record links to its title's PDF at the page where the section starts. 18,752 sections, 15,650 in force; 111 strong and 127 weak-only hits; 135 Indian law sections (32 core, 103 incidental). The core sections include the Wyoming Indian Child Welfare Act (Wyo. Stat. Ann. §§ 14-6-701 to 14-6-715), full faith and credit for the Eastern Shoshone and Northern Arapaho tribal courts (§ 5-1-111) and Native American Day (§ 8-4-105). The readers marked three strong hits none and the session agreed with all three (a hunting area boundary along the Wind River Indian Reservation line, § 23-1-101, and two Hot Springs State Park sections that name the land's history as part of the Shoshone reservation, §§ 36-8-302 and 36-8-307). Neither recall sample found a miss, and the 40 verdicts read at random all agreed.

## Wisconsin

Wisconsin's legislature site (docs.legis.wisconsin.gov) resets every connection from a cloud session, so Trevor chose on Nov. 27, 2026 to run the download on GitHub from this batch's branch: a temporary workflow ran the adapter's download step and committed the result to the branch, and both are removed before the batch lands. The first run took each chapter's web page, but a chapter page shows only its first few sections and loads the rest as the reader scrolls (chapter 1's page held 6 of its 21 sections), so the second run took each chapter's PDF instead and converted it to text. The PDFs print two columns, and the text keeps them side by side, so the adapter cuts each line where the page's right column starts; a row set in the smaller annotation type puts that column further right, so a line with no gap at the main place is cut at its gap nearest it. Each chapter's contents list every section's number and catchline, and a section starts where its number and catchline are printed again; the text runs to the history line, and the annotations after it are not read. The contents' lines, which can fall beside the first sections on the chapter's first page, are taken out of those sections' text. The 17,559 sections with a history line come within 0.4 percent of the 17,624 times "History:" is printed, so the split misses few if any sections; 8 sections still run the two columns together, and the readers were told so. The history lines name only acts from about 1970 on (of the 16,959 history lines that open with a year, all but 2 open in 1970 or later, while the code still holds territorial law such as § 1.09, the territorial act that fixed the seat of government at Madison), so the head of a history line is not an enactment and the records carry no enactment year or act key; the near sample is drawn from sections printed next to a strong hit. Each record links to the section's own page and cites the 2023-24 edition (updated through 2025 Wisconsin Act 247, published Nov. 4, 2026). 18,104 sections; 552 strong and 295 weak-only hits; 633 Indian law sections (94 core, 539 incidental), one of them (§ 806.245, full faith and credit for tribal documents) already in the library and left as it was. The core sections include Indian gaming (Wis. Stat. ch. 569 and § 14.035), the Wisconsin Indian Child Welfare Act (§ 48.028) and its juvenile counterpart (§ 938.028), tribal and county law enforcement (§§ 165.89 to 165.92), the treaty hunting, fishing and Lac du Flambeau approval sections (§§ 29.071 to 29.2295), Indian student assistance and tribal college payments (§§ 39.38 and 39.382), American Indian language and culture education (§§ 115.71 to 115.745), the race-based mascots law (§ 118.134), the Native American Church exemption (§ 961.115) and Indian Rights Day (§ 995.23).

The first reading pass read section texts that still held the contents lines of the chapter's first page, so several first sections looked like hits for the contents' Indian law entries; the session fixed the split and the readers read the corrected pool again from the start. The readers marked six strong hits none. The session agreed with four (a county boundary that runs along a reservation line, § 2.01; a building program finding that names tribes' heritage, § 13.48; the budget schedule's tribal gaming revenue figure, § 20.005; and a highway named for American Indians as citizen soldiers, § 84.1031) and settled two as incidental: the nonmotorized trails council's seat for persons who represent tribal lands (§ 15.347) and the elk hunter education course, which must cover Native American hunting (§ 29.595). Neither recall sample found a miss, and the 40 verdicts read at random all agreed.

## Not read: Indiana and New Mexico

Indiana's code site (iga.in.gov) is a script application whose script files are not served to a cloud session (every address answers with the same empty page), and the General Assembly's data service (api.iga.in.gov) answers "x-api-key not found" without a key. New Mexico's official compilation, NMOneSource (nmonesource.com, run by the New Mexico Compilation Commission on Lexum's platform), serves each chapter of the New Mexico Statutes Annotated as a PDF, but after about ten requests it shows a captcha page saying it checks that users "are people and not programs"; the session stopped there and did not try to get past it. Each needs another route: an API key for Indiana (Trevor, Nov. 27, 2026: later), and, for New Mexico (also later), a bulk copy from the Compilation Commission or the chapter PDFs saved by a person for New Mexico.
