Output: the land acknowledgment in `content/front-matter/acknowledgments.md`, shown on the website's Acknowledgments page and printed as the book's Acknowledgments page.

Findings: the university's statement places UNLV on the traditional homelands of the "Nuwu (noo·woo), Southern Paiute (pai·oot) People" and names the Las Vegas Paiute Tribe and the Moapa Band of Paiutes. The law school's own acknowledgment, on its Indian Nations Gaming and Governance Program page, uses "Nuwuvi, Southern Paiute People" and names the same two nations. The university's companion page says the Nuwuvi "are also known as Nuwu" and lists both nations among the Southern Paiute tribes. The university's statement says land acknowledgments "do not exist in a historical context or past tense," which matches the style guide's rule on present tense for living nations, so the wording is in the present tense.

The Nevada Indian Commission's guidance lists five steps: evaluate the purpose, ground the statement in honesty and respect, do the research, cover past, present and future, and check the statement. Its adopted wording names "the Numa, the Newe, the Nuwuvi, and the Wa She Shu."
