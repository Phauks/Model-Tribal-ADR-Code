Findings:

- Stars: the Hipparcos and Tycho Catalogues are distributed "under the CC BY-NC 3.0 IGO licence" with the credit "ESA" (ESA, The Hipparcos and Tycho Catalogues, https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/hipparcos/catalogues; ESDC Terms and Conditions, https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/esdc/terms-and-conditions). Used for the globe, with "Stars: ESA Hipparcos" in the map's credit line.
- Ruled out: the Yale Bright Star Catalog, 5th revised edition. data.gov lists it as a U.S. government work (https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/bright-star-catalog), but NASA HEASARC says it provides the catalog "solely for the purposes of scientific research" and "does not own the copyright" (https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/W3Browse/catalog/bsc5p.html).
- Ruled out for now: the Ojibwe and D(L)akota star maps of Native Skywatchers. They are copyright 2012 by their authors (Annette S. Lee, William Wilson, Jeff Tibbets and Carl Gawboy; Annette S. Lee and Jim Rock), are sold, and carry no reuse terms (https://nativeskywatchers.com/resources.html; http://judy-volker.com/StarLore/Art/NativeSkywatchers.html). Stellarium withdrew its Ojibwe and Dakota sky cultures on 2022-10-16 "[d]ue to alleged infringement of copyrights by commercial exploitation" (https://stellarium.org/skycultures/2022/10/16/CopyrightIssue.html). The paper describing the maps is CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, which covers the paper, not the maps (https://escholarship.org/uc/item/58m4f9pq).
- Ruled out for now: Potawatomi constellations. The Citizen Potawatomi Nation's own articles name Mang (Loon), Mozo (Moose), Ojik (Fisher) and Jejak (Crane) but list no stars and no reuse terms, and note that "[s]ome communities believe that it is appropriate to share winter stories only when Pondésé Nëgos (Winter Maker) is in the sky" (https://www.potawatomi.org/blog/2021/01/15/traditional-star-knowledge-provides-connections-to-potawatomi-heritage-and-culture/; https://www.potawatomi.org/blog/2024/03/15/anishnabe-star-knowledge/). Michigan State University's Neshnabé Nengosêk Kenomagewen project, with Pokagon Band advisors, says its Potawatomi sky culture secured its approvals but its Creative Commons license is "forthcoming" (https://digitalhumanities.msu.edu/seed-funding/neshnabe-nengosek-kenomagewen-potawatomi-star-knowledge/); its Stellarium pull request was postponed (https://github.com/Stellarium/stellarium/pull/4869). The best candidate to ask once it is licensed.
- Ruled out: Stellarium's Navajo sky culture, contributed by an educator at Digitalis Education Solutions from Don Childrey's book Star Trails-Navajo, not by the Navajo Nation or its own educators; and its Blackfoot sky culture, drawn from anthropologists' books of 1908 and 1910 (Stellarium sky culture description files, https://github.com/Stellarium/stellarium-skycultures).
- Possible later: the Hawaiian star lines, contributed in 2017 by teachers and students of Kamehameha Schools Kapālama after Nainoa Thompson's star lines for the Polynesian Voyaging Society, under CC BY-SA (same repository, hawaiian_starlines). Native Hawaiians are not a tribe, and these are navigation star lines, so they wait for the Polynesian Voyaging Society's blessing.

No nation's constellations are drawn. Each would be its own named overlay, added only with that nation's written permission and following its own protocols, such as a season for winter stories.
