# Stories about law: session notes

## What this session added

Six story records, each shown in full on the website in the teller's exact words:

- The Mashpee council's resolutions of May 21, 1833 ("we, as a tribe, will rule ourselves"), and the first of July, when the Mashpee people unloaded the carts of men taking their wood, from William Apess, *Indian Nullification* (1835).
- Chief Joseph on the laws his fathers gave the Nez Perce people and on the treaty councils of 1855 and 1863 ("If we ever owned the land we own it still, for we never sold it"), *The North American Review* (April 1879).
- Black Hawk on the treaty of 1804, which began as a mission to cover the blood of a killing by payment, from his *Life* (1833).
- Sarah Winnemucca Hopkins on the Paiute chiefs and the council-tent ("The council-tent is our Congress"), *Life Among the Piutes* (1883).
- Andrew J. Blackbird on the Odawa and Chippewa treaties of 1836 and 1855 and the release clause no one explained, *History of the Ottawa and Chippewa Indians of Michigan* (1887).
- Zitkala-Ša's short story "The Widespread Enigma Concerning Blue-Star Woman," about proving membership for an allotment, from *American Indian Stories* (1921).

Four of the six are linked to the treaty records they name, so each treaty's page lists the account.

## How the texts were checked

Each full text was built from one copy and compared word by word, with punctuation and capitals, against a second copy made independently (a different scan, or a Project Gutenberg transcription). Every difference was then read on the page images of the first printing. The Gutenberg transcriptions of Apess and Zitkala-Ša silently correct the printings in a few places ("Fish" for the councillor Fiske; "pairie," "gilt," "beaucrates," "magestically"); the records keep the printing and say so in their notes.

## Leads for later

- Chitto Harjo's testimony before the Senate committee at Tulsa, Nov. 23, 1906 (S. Rep. No. 59-5013, pt. 2), on the Creek treaties and allotment.
- The Cherokee memorials to Congress of 1829 and 1830, and the Indian-Pioneer Papers.
- Charles A. Eastman, *From the Deep Woods to Civilization* (1916), on his work for the Sioux treaty claims.
- Nations' own published histories of their constitutions, courts and treaties (for example the Navajo Nation's accounts of the Treaty of 1868), which would be shown under decision record 0036.
