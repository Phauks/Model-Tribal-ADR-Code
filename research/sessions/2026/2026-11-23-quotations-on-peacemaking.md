# Quotations on peacemaking: session notes

The result is [the checked list of quotations](../../quotes/2026-11-23-quotations-on-peacemaking-and-self-government.md): 41 quotations checked against the source (35 to the printed page and 6 against a reprint), 3 seen only as another author quoted them, and 12 popular lines traced as misattributed or untraced.

The search started from the people the request named and the sources the project already holds. The project's private bucket already held Yazzie, Fletcher and the 1994 Zuni Cruz article, and Porter's article was on the Native American Rights Fund's site. Public-domain books by Native authors and leaders were read in Internet Archive scans, as in the ancestor-stories session of Nov. 22.

The search for direct quotations from Pommersheim, Deloria and Tom Tso inside the articles on hand found citations but almost no quoted text. The one usable line was Pommersheim's, quoted by Valencia-Weber.

Two famous lines turned out to differ from their earliest published form. Chief Joseph's own 1879 account of his surrender reads "From where the sun now stands I will fight no more," without "forever." Standing Bear's courtroom speech beginning "That hand is not the color of yours" is not in Tibbles's 1879 book, which instead prints his words to his lawyer about laying down the tomahawk.
