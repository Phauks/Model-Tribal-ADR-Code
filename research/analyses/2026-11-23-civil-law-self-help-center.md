# What to learn from the Civil Law Self-Help Center (Clark County, Nevada)

**Status:** research notes, written Nov. 23, 2026, in answer to Trevor's request to research the Civil Law Self-Help Center and "see what we can learn and also do from them." Nothing here has been built or decided. It builds on the [self-help forms note](2026-11-23-self-help-forms.md), which gave the center one row (section 3); this note goes deeper and lists only ideas that note, the [model forms](../../content/forms/README.md) and the [roadmap](../../docs/roadmap.md) do not already cover. Statements marked "project analysis" are my reading of the sources or of the project's own files, not a source's words. All pages were opened on Nov. 23, 2026.

## 1. Short answer

The Civil Law Self-Help Center is a walk-in center and website for people without a lawyer in Clark County's civil courts. It opened in December 2009, sits on the first floor of the Regional Justice Center in Las Vegas, and is run by Legal Aid Center of Southern Nevada under a contract with Clark County [1] [2]. Its website has nothing on Tribal courts: its own search returned no results for "tribal," "Indian" or "ICWA" [3].

Three things are worth taking (project analysis):

1. **Its page patterns.** Numbered step guides with jump links, TIP, CAUTION and FYI boxes, a flowchart PDF for each process, and form lists that say which version (fillable PDF, printable PDF, Word, or a packet with instructions) each form comes in (section 3).
2. **Two new model forms and one model code question.** A confidential information sheet, which our protection order petition already refers to but does not supply, and later a request to enter a mediated or peacemaking agreement as a court order. Its small claims mediation rules are a ready local example for Gate 2 (section 4).
3. **A local partnership.** The free class program it promotes is taught by Boyd students under Legal Aid Center and Nevada Legal Services supervision, and its court finder lists no Tribal court. Both are openings for Trevor (section 5).

## 2. What the center offers

## 3. How the site is organized

The top menu has seven doors: Self-Help (by topic), Courts and Case Lookup, Forms (by court and topic), Rules and Laws, Classes and Programs, Community Resources, and References and Links [2]. The home page shows each topic as a card with its three most-used guides, a "Get Started Here" column (court basics, lawyers and legal help, representing yourself, researching the law, resolving disputes out of court, using AI), a court finder by address and radius, and a dismissable banner for the most urgent task (tenants answering an eviction notice online) [2].

Topic guides follow one pattern [12] [23]:

- An overview that lists numbered steps, each a jump link ("Step 1: Verify that this is the appropriate application for you").
- Each step with the form it needs, inline, and a note when a guided interview can do the step instead.
- Boxes labeled TIP!, CAUTION! and FYI! for the points people get wrong (an eviction notice without a court stamp is still real; filed papers are public records, so black out Social Security numbers).
- A WARNING! banner at the top of a form list when the reader may be in the wrong court (domestic protection orders belong to family court) [9].

Every directory page ends with the same line: the center is not responsible for linked sites, and readers should check that information is current and fits their jurisdiction [24].

## 4. What the project can learn

Each idea says what exists in the project today. The ranking is project analysis, weighing usefulness to Tribal court users, fit with the dispute resolution focus, cost, and legal advice risk.

| # | Idea | What exists now | What to add |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Model confidential information sheet** | The model protection order petition tells the petitioner to give a private address "to the clerk on a separate sheet" ([petition-for-a-protection-order.md](../../content/forms/petition-for-a-protection-order.md), section 1) but no such sheet exists | A model form, kept out of the public file, for the petitioner's contact details and, as Clark County does, a second one for the respondent's details so they can be served [9] [12] |
| 2 | **Step guide pattern for Help pages** | The self-help note proposes four headings per guide (what it is, which court, general steps, your nation's forms) | Numbered, linked steps; the form inline at the step that uses it; TIP, CAUTION and FYI boxes; a wrong-court warning at the top |
| 3 | **A flowchart for each model form** | Court structure pictures per nation (design/court-flow) | A one-page process chart per form (file, serve, hearing, order), with placeholders like the forms, offered with the form's Word and PDF copies |
| 4 | **"Using AI with Tribal law" page** | Nothing public | Adapt the center's page: AI often mixes up nations and their codes and may not know a nation's current law; never paste case details, enrollment numbers or cultural knowledge into an AI tool; check every citation against the nation's own text. The project is built with AI help, so saying how readers should use AI fits [21] |
| 5 | **"What this site can and cannot do" page** | Working rules in section 4 of the self-help note, not yet public | Publish them in reader-facing words, with the center's point that nothing sent to the project is confidential; repeat it beside Report a problem and the contact form so people do not send case facts |
| 6 | **Form format labels and packets** | Each model form as Word and PDF with a hash | Say in the list what each format is for (fill in on screen, print and handwrite, edit); offer a packet PDF that puts the instructions in front of the form [7] [8] |
| 7 | **Mediation and agreement rules for Gate 2** | Parked research on confidentiality and on return to court and agreements (roadmap, Tribal rules of evidence row) | In Clark County small claims, Henderson requires mediation on the hearing day while Las Vegas and North Las Vegas keep it voluntary; a plaintiff who skips mandatory mediation is dismissed and a defendant can be defaulted; a breached agreement becomes a judgment by notice, and the defendant "will not have an opportunity to contest"; the center warns readers about penalty clauses [17]. A close, local set of choices for the outline's referral and enforcement items |
| 8 | **Model request to enter an agreement as a court order** | Model forms cover court filings only; peacemaking and dispute resolution forms wait for the code's general division (decision record [0049](../../docs/decisions/0049-draft-model-court-forms-before-gate-2.md)) | Park until Phase 2, then base it on the Las Vegas Justice Court motion and opposition the center links [17] |
| 9 | **Printable one-page handouts** | Print options for pages | A one-page handout per help guide that a Tribal court clerk can print, like the center's brochure and the Neighborhood Justice Center's English and Spanish brochures [24] |
| 10 | **Court practice fields for language** | Court practice and language facts from the jurisdiction pass | Record whether a Tribal court holds hearings in the nation's language and whether it supplies interpreters; the center's interpreter page shows the questions readers ask (who pays, who may interpret, disability access) [20] |

Ideas 2 to 6 and 9 only change how pages and forms are presented. Ideas 1 and 8 are new model forms. Idea 7 is input for Gate 2. Idea 10 is a data field (project analysis).

What not to copy: the center's site collects visitor data with Google Analytics and advertising features [25], which would conflict with this project's privacy approach (project analysis). Its guided interviews run on a court e-filing vendor's system; the self-help note's position stands that interviews wait for a partner nation's court.

## 5. Partnership and outreach

The outreach list has Nevada Legal Services' Indian Law Project, Boyd's Native American Law Student Association and the Saltman Center, but not Legal Aid Center of Southern Nevada, the Civil Law Self-Help Center or the Neighborhood Justice Center (research/outreach, checked Nov. 23, 2026).

1. **A "Tribal courts" class in the free class program.** The classes are presented by Boyd students and supervised by Legal Aid Center and Nevada Legal Services attorneys [13]. The Indian Law Project is Nevada Legal Services' tribal law program, "the only legal aid program in Nevada that specializes in tribal law"; it reports child custody, minor guardianships, evictions, child dependency, Indian wills, probate and criminal defense as its most common requests, and says many Tribal court litigants represent themselves [26]. A Boyd-taught class on Tribal courts and peacemaking, co-supervised by the Indian Law Project, would fit that program and could run on the project's events pages (project analysis).
2. **Ask the center to list Tribal courts.** Its Find My Court page lists only the eleven Justice Courts and the district court [27]. Two Native nations have land in Clark County: the Las Vegas Paiute Tribe, whose court lists civil, juvenile, criminal and traffic divisions and has forms, rules and legal resources pages [28], and the Moapa Band of Paiutes. A short entry pointing to those courts and the Indian Law Project would close a gap for local tribal members; the project could offer the text (project analysis).
3. **A conversation with the Neighborhood Justice Center.** It is a state-created community mediation program with victim offender mediation [18]. The project already cites the national Neighborhood Justice Center field test of 1978; the Clark County program is a living local comparison for the essays on community-based dispute resolution (project analysis).
4. **Collect the Las Vegas Paiute court's forms.** The court's page has a Forms tab [28], but no Nevada Tribal court forms are in the project's form captures yet (research/tribal-court-forms, checked Nov. 23, 2026). This belongs to the court forms collection the self-help note ranks first.

Outreach is the lead author's call, and Danielle Finn's view as a Tribal judge would help with item 2. Any contact goes through the asks log as usual.
