# Tribal law legal help and self-help centers nationwide (Nov. 24, 2026)

Trevor asked (Nov. 24, 2026) to identify tribal law self-help centers around the country and which states and Native nations they may serve, so they can be integrated into the nation pages and the assistance queue. This note reports what was found, how it was matched to nations, and what is left. The records are in [`research/legal-help/`](../legal-help/README.md); every fact there names its source.

### The Legal Services Corporation's Native American service areas

The Legal Services Corporation (LSC) funds civil legal aid in three kinds of service areas; one kind, "Basic Field-Native American", is "intended to provide legal services to Native Americans living in a specific geographical area, related to their status as Native Americans" and covers issues "cognizable in Indian law and tribal courts" ([LSC Service Areas](https://www.lsc.gov/grants/basic-field-grant/lsc-service-areas)). LSC says it has 27 such areas (same page). LSC's service area map loads its data from a public feed, `https://api.lscdataease.org/public/lsc/service_areas.json`, which lists all 190 service areas with each area's type, current grantee and area description (read Nov. 24, 2026; data dated Apr. 22, 2026). It lists exactly 27 Native American areas, held by 25 organizations: DNA-People's Legal Services holds two (NAZ-5, Navajo and Hopi; NNM-2, Jicarilla Apache), and Pine Tree Legal Assistance holds two (NME-1, Maine; NCT-1, Connecticut). LSC's notice of intent to award 2026 grants gives each area's grantee and amount, from $23,604 (Connecticut) to $3,934,252 (DNA, NAZ-5) ([Notice of Intent to Award, Oct. 31, 2025](https://lsc-live.app.box.com/s/fleq8j26yivz3potdw5hvpclth90icmp)).

Twenty of the 27 areas cover Native people in every county of a state; the other seven name reservations or nations (NAZ-5 Navajo and Hopi; NAZ-6 eleven Arizona nations; NMN-1 Leech Lake, White Earth and Red Lake; NMS-1 Mississippi Band of Choctaw; NMT-1 seven Montana reservations; NNM-2 Jicarilla; NNM-4 Mescalero and 19 Pueblos; NTX-1 Kickapoo Traditional Tribe of Texas). Two details a reader should know:

- South Dakota (NSD-1): the 2026 notice names "Dakota Plains Legal Services, Inc. or East River Legal Services, Inc.", and the area is up for competition again for 2027 ([2027 Service Areas Subject to Competition](https://www.lsc.gov/grants/basic-field-grant/lsc-service-areas/2027-service-areas-subject-competition)). The map data still names Dakota Plains.
- Wisconsin (NWI-1): LSC names the grantee "Judicare Legal Aid", while Wisconsin Law Help lists the office as "Legal Action of Wisconsin - Indian Law Office" ([directory entry](https://wislawhelp.org/node/132)). Which is current was not confirmed.

### Tribal court self-help centers

Only four were found, which matches the Nov. 23, 2026 survey's finding that no national tribal-law self-help service exists (`research/analyses/2026-11-23-self-help-forms.md`):

- Karuk Tribal Court Self Help Center helps tribal members and families with paperwork for the Karuk Tribal Court or California Superior Court, without legal advice ([Self Help Center](https://www.karuktribalcourt.org/self-help-center)).
- The Navajo Nation Judicial Branch hired four Judicial Navigators on March 23, 2026, to serve locations across the Nation from May 2026 while it renovates "the first self-help center on the Navajo Nation" in Window Rock ([Judicial Branch Second Quarterly Report, Spring 2026](https://www.navajonationcouncil.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/20APR2026_Judicial_Branch_Second_Quarterly_Report-Spring_2026.pdf)).
- The Bureau of Indian Affairs' Court of Indian Offenses of the Western Region posts self-help packets for custody, divorce, protective orders and civil suits ([Self-Help Center](https://www.bia.gov/regional-offices/western/court-indian-offenses/self-help-center)). Two Bureau pages disagree on which tribes that court serves (Skull Valley, Summit Lake and Yomba on one; Skull Valley, Te-Moak and Winnemucca on the other), so all five are matched until checked.
- Wilton Rancheria's Tribal Court Self Help Center is suspended "effective Monday, August 11, until further notice", and the Rancheria now points members to a new Legal Aid program ([Self Help Center](https://wiltonrancheria-nsn.gov/tribal-government/tribal-court/self-help-center/)).

### State courts and state legal help sites

State courts cover their half: California's Self-Help Guide explains registering a tribal court order in state court ([page](https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/tribal-court/registering-orders)); Self-Help Washington's tribal courts page says only "More information to come soon" and links the state's tribal court directory ([page](https://www.courts.wa.gov/self-help/tribal-courts)); the New Mexico Courts' self-representation site lists DNA-People's Legal Services and the Native American Disability Law Center ([list, June 2026](https://selfrepresentation.nmcourts.gov/wp-content/uploads/sites/62/2026/08/Native-American-Legal-Services-Resources-Jun2026.pdf)). The best state model found is MontanaLawHelp.org, which has a page for each of eight Montana reservations and the Little Shell Tribe, listing the tribal code, constitution and court contact ([Native American](https://montanalawhelp.org/issues/native-american)). The Minnesota State Law Library's county referral pages list the Native legal help for each county ([Carlton County](https://mn.gov/law-library/research-links/legal-referrals/carlton.jsp)) and could fill Minnesota's gaps county by county.

### Nations' own legal aid offices

Several nations run legal aid offices for their members, found while checking gaps: the Choctaw Nation prepares documents for uncontested cases; the Cheyenne and Arapaho Tribes represent members in tribal court and in 11 Oklahoma counties; the Puyallup Tribe has three attorneys serving King, Pierce and Thurston counties; Muckleshoot Tribal Court holds a weekly advice clinic; the Mille Lacs Band's Band Member Legal Aid serves members at no cost; the Umatilla tribes pay Legal Aid Services of Oregon to serve members above its income limits; and the Saint Regis Mohawk Tribe hosts a Legal Aid Society of Northeastern New York attorney. These were not searched for systematically; a sweep of each nation's site, like the contacts sweep, would find more.

## Gaps

After matching, 41 federally recognized nations have no Native legal aid, tribal legal aid office or self-help center in these records. They are in states with no LSC Native American service area (Alabama, Florida, Iowa, Kansas, Louisiana, Massachusetts, New York, Rhode Island, South Carolina, Virginia), or outside the named areas of Arizona, Minnesota, Montana and Texas. Some of them likely have programs not yet searched for: three of the six nations checked by hand had one, and the Poarch Band of Creek Indians has a partnership with Legal Services Alabama still to be read. Each still has its state's general legal aid, which LSC's same data lists by county.

## How this fits the site

Nation pages already have a `court_practice` list with a `legal-aid` kind (17 entries), read from each nation's own pages. The matches here are a second, nationwide layer: the provider, the basis of the match, and a link. A "Legal help" section on nation pages, and a list of matched providers beside a question in the requests queue, are proposed in a mockup for Trevor's approval before anything changes on the site.
