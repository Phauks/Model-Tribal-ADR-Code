# Evidence that nations were forced to move: a pilot on six journeys

**Status:** research pilot, written Nov. 27, 2026, after Trevor said the removals data "feels somewhat weak" and that "we will need strong evidence to showcase how tribes or peoples were forced to move." Nothing on the site changed. Two research subagents gathered the evidence; I re-matched every quotation by script against the text they fetched (all matched, allowing for line-end hyphens in scans, except the one marked below). The full records are in `research/historic-maps/removals/evidence.json`. Every item is Unverified until a person reads it in the source.

## 1. The standard

Until now each removal stage rested on one quotation from the treaty or statute that ordered it. That shows the United States ordered a move; it does not show the move happened or that it was forced. The pilot tests a three-part standard for each journey:

- **A. The order.** The removal clause of the treaty or statute, or the military order, quoted in full.
- **B. The move, recorded at the time.** A government record made during or just after the move, with dates, places, numbers of people and deaths.
- **C. The force.** A record of troops, prisoners, stockades, roundups, escorts, threats or deaths, or an official finding that the move was against the nation's will.

The nation's own account is linked, never copied, and the nation may ask for changes (charter commitment 6).

Crook* itself says whether they left "by force or otherwise does not appear" |

**What this shows (project analysis).** The three-part standard can be met from public-domain federal records for five of the six journeys, and the records are vivid: prisoners counted, deaths counted, orders to kill. The Choctaw journey meets A and B but needs better C. The common gap is the Commissioner of Indian Affairs' annual reports: the University of Wisconsin's copies and HathiTrust refused scripted reading, but the Internet Archive's scans worked and were used for the western journeys. The National Archives' muster rolls (microfilm M1831) are images only, so their counts need a person or a transcription.

## 3. Recommendation (project analysis)

1. Adopt the three-part standard for the Removals page. Draw a journey as a solid arrow only when it has A and B; draw it dashed, with "Record of the move not yet found," when it has only A. Show C as its own short list under each journey, because C is what answers "were they forced?"
2. Extend the evidence to the other 49 journeys in batches by region, with the same checks. The western and Indian Territory journeys of the 1860s to 1880s will be quickest, because the Commissioner's reports and congressional documents for those years are on the Internet Archive.
3. Read every item in its source before marking it Verified, starting with the six pilot journeys.

**The order** (6)

- Treaty with the Cherokee, Dec. 29, 1835, art. 16, 7 Stat. 478, 485 (proclaimed May 23, 1836). "It is hereby stipulated and agreed by the Cherokees that they shall remove to their new homes within two years from the ratification of this treaty"

"The United States also agree and stipulate to remove the Cherokees to their new homes and to subsist them one year after their arrival there and that a sufficient number of steamboats and baggage-wagons shall be furnished to remove them comfortably,"

- Act of May 28, 1830, ch. 148, sec. 1, 4 Stat. 411, 411-12. "into a suitable number of districts, for the reception of such tribes or nations of Indians as may choose to exchange the lands where they now reside, and remove there;"
