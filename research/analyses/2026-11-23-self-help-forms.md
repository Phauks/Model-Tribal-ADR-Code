# Self-help forms and guides for tribal members

**Status:** research notes, written Nov. 23, 2026, in answer to Trevor's question whether other websites already offer a tribal law help center and, if not, which forms and documents from state and other self-help centers would help tribal members. Nothing here has been built or decided. The readable version with a flow sketch is a Claude Doc: https://claude.ai/artifact/7S8SgUq1uWdXU6v9St7xKs. It builds on the [similar legal projects note](2026-11-23-similar-legal-projects.md). Statements marked "project analysis" are my reading of the sources or of the project's own data, not a source's words. All pages were opened on Nov. 23, 2026, unless the Sources list says otherwise.

## 1. Short answer

No website offers national self-help for people using tribal courts. The pieces are scattered across single tribal courts, federal Courts of Indian Offenses, legal aid programs and topic guides (section 2). State self-help centers describe tribal orders only from the state court's side (section 3). Nobody lets a person start from their own nation and see that nation's court forms, how to ask for peacemaking, and the state or federal paperwork that touches tribal orders (project analysis).

Three recommendations:

1. **Link, don't write.** Each nation's forms are its own law. Point to the court's own forms page, say in plain words what each common form is for, and date-check the link.
2. **Write the guides nobody else writes.** The cross-border topics: getting a tribal order honored by a state, protection orders across lines, the Indian Child Welfare Act (ICWA) for families, and asking for peacemaking.
3. **Stay on the legal information side.** Answer who, what, when, where and how; never should or whether [18]. Guided interviews that fill in forms wait for a partner nation's court.

| A tribal court's own forms | Comanche Nation Tribal Court pro se instructions [1] | Checklists for civil, divorce, custody, guardianship and name change cases; a Pauper's Affidavit; fees ($8.50 per summons) | One court; long PDF; clerks "CANNOT advise you" |

| A tribal court self-help center | Karuk Tribal Court Self-Help Center [3] | A family law facilitator helps people prepare their own forms in 13 case types | Karuk members, descendants and parents of Karuk children only; info sheet dated 2010 |
| A nation's peacemaking program | Navajo Nation Peacemaking Program [4] [5] | A Request for Services form; walk-in only at the district office; matters needing a court order must be filed in court first; a peacemaker fee | Paper and in person |
| Federal Courts of Indian Offenses | BIA Western Region Self-Help Center [6]; Southern Plains packets [7] [8] | 12 packets (custody, child support, divorce, guardianship, name change, paternity, protective order and more) plus motions, with sample filled-in forms | Western page says to contact the court for copies; one guardianship packet's instructions say "divorce" (copy error) |
| Legal aid with interactive forms | Anishinabe Legal Services (2016) [10] | Guided interviews on LawHelp Interactive for White Earth and Leech Lake courts | Not confirmed live; the 2026 LawHelpMN page links plain court forms [11] |
| Legal aid with a will tool | MontanaLawHelp, Wills for Native Americans [12] | "Will-in-a-Box", a free interview that builds an Indian will for trust land | Montana only |
| National topic guides | NICWA family guide [14]; MSU Inheriting Indian Land fact sheets [13]; Tribal Protection Order Resources [15] | Plain-language guides on ICWA, trust land inheritance (14 fact sheets) and enforcing tribal protection orders | Topic only; not tied to a nation's court |
| Training for courts | Empowering Tribal Courts through Self Help Resource Development webinar, Feb. 19, 2025 [17] | A roadmap for courts building self-help, with a Fort McDermitt Paiute-Shoshone pro se packet and case flow charts | For court staff, not the public |

In 2009 Yurok Chief Judge Abby Abinanti described building self-help centers for protection orders across four tribes and training tribal courts to make forms that "conform to each other's" [19].

## 4. Legal information, not legal advice

Legal information is "facts about the law and the legal process"; legal advice is advice about "the course of action a client should take" [18]. Who, what, when, where and how questions are information; should and whether questions are advice [18]. Nevada Supreme Court Rule 44 lets court staff describe available forms, check a form for completeness, cite rules and define terms [23]. Staff may not create forms for a person, give an opinion on how a rule applies, or tell someone what to say in court [23].

Working rules for the site (project analysis):

1. Explain what a form is for and where it is filed; never say which form a particular person needs.
2. Link to the nation's own form; never fill it in, rewrite it, or offer a substitute.
3. Sort by problem but stop at general steps; no tool that takes a person's facts and returns an answer.
4. Put a plain disclaimer and an as-of date on every help page, and send people to the court clerk to confirm.
5. Point to legal help by directory without recommending one lawyer.
6. Say whose law governs: the nation's code and court rules, then federal law, then state law where it applies.

These rules come from court staff guidance. Whether a law student's public website raises unauthorized practice concerns under Nevada law was not researched; it is a question for the advisors or the State Bar.

## 6. Mockup ideas

- **A. "Court forms and help" block on each nation page**, under the courts list. Links carry the court's own labels (forms, fee waiver, protection order petition, ask for peacemaking), each with a last-checked date and a report button. When nothing is online, it shows the clerk's phone.
- **B. Topic guides in a new Help section**, each with the same four headings: what it is, which court, general steps, your nation's forms.
- **C. "Orders between courts" table**, filterable, one row per state and order type, using the PR #185 tables.
- **D. Help home** with problem tiles in plain words and a "Which court hears my case?" explainer.

The site's /forms page already means contributor forms; court forms should live under /help.
