# Website design

**Status:** design exploration, Nov. 21, 2026. Nothing here is built yet. This document records the direction the website will follow: who it serves, how it is organized, how it looks and behaves, and why. Mockups of the key pages, in light and dark themes, are in a design canvas shared through the project thread. Changes to this document are made by pull request.

Where a choice rests on a published source, a footnote cites it. Where it is the project's own judgment, it says "project choice" or "project analysis." The reference sites were reviewed in the research session [2026-11-21-website-design-references](../research/sessions/2026/2026-11-21-website-design-references.md).

## 1. What the website is for

The website publishes the model code, its commentary, the profiles of Native nations, the glossary and the sources, and it is the place where a nation asks the project to correct, restrict or remove material about it ([charter, commitment 6](../docs/charter.md#6-the-right-to-correct-restrict-or-withdraw)). The style guide names the audience: "Tribal lawmakers, judges, peacemakers, court staff, advocates and students, not only lawyers" ([style guide, section 2](../docs/style-guide.md#2-plain-language)).

The design serves four jobs. They are composites drawn from that audience, not findings from user research, which has not been done (project analysis).

| Person | Job | The site succeeds when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reader: a Tribal council member or court clerk drafting an ordinance | Compare the alternatives in a section and understand what each choice does | They compare alternatives and read the comment without leaving the page |
| Researcher: a law student or scholar | Find which nations have published dispute resolution or peacemaking law, and cite it | Every fact is one step from its source, and a citation names the version |
| Nation representative: staff of a nation's court, legal department or cultural office | Check what the project says about the nation, and correct, restrict or remove it; or, if the nation is missing, ask to add it with proof from a government source | A request takes minutes, needs no account, and protects first |
| Contributor: a peacemaker, Indigenous author, reviewer or co-author | Offer sources, review, preferred terms or text, and be credited as they choose | They contribute without a GitHub account |

User research with people in each role is the most important next step for the design; see [section 13](#13-open-questions-and-next-steps).

## 2. The feel: a drafting desk

The site should read as a careful legal reference, the way the style guide asks the writing to: "precise, calm and specific" ([style guide, section 1](../docs/style-guide.md#1-voice-and-tone)). The design spends its boldness in one place, the marks a drafter works with, and keeps everything else quiet (project choice):

- **Hanging section numbers.** "§ 2-104" is set large, in the track's color, above the section title, so a reader always knows where they are in the code.
- **Lettered alternatives.** Alternative A and Alternative B are tabs a reader switches between, with a "Compare side by side" view.
- **Fill-in placeholders.** Template variables such as `{{nation.name}}` appear as highlighted fill-ins, "[Name of Nation]", the way the book prints them ([data model, built for templates](../docs/data-model.md#built-for-templates)). A future template builder fills them in.
- **Dotted glossary links.** Defined terms have a dotted underline and open a short card; ordinary links have a solid underline.
- **Track color.** The ADR track and the peacemaking track each have a color, always paired with the words "ADR track" or "Peacemaking track."

Each mark encodes something about the text; none is decoration.

### What the design avoids

- **Borrowed Native motifs.** No patterns, borders, colors or icons that imitate Native art. The IllumiNative industry guide advises against pan-Indian visuals and sacred objects and asks whether a project has "the blessing and consent of the community";[^illuminative] the project's art will come only from Native artists, chosen through a contest ([charter, commitment 7](../docs/charter.md#7-credit-authorship-and-art)). Until then, the spaces for art are left visibly empty and labeled.
- **A single "Native" look.** "There is no single American Indian culture or language,"[^nk360] so no part of the interface suggests one.
- **Official or governmental styling.** The site is not a government site. The U.S. Web Design System itself says not to use its official banner on non-government domains.[^uswds-banner] The site borrows government design patterns, not their look (project choice).
- **Custom icons.** The site and the book draw only from Lucide (see [section 6, Icons](#6-components)). No icons are drawn for the project.

## 4. Information architecture

### Main menu

The site's six sections sit in a row under the masthead on wide screens: Nations, Federal law, Courts and cases, Library, Writing and About (Trevor chose this on Nov. 27, 2026, from three models; project files `design/navigation-models`, model C, "Sections on top, nation at side"). They follow the groups of the Statistics page (`src/lib/server/collections.ts`), then the writing and the About pages, so the treaties, laws and cases have a home of their own and no section singles out dispute resolution. Each section opens a menu of its pages in groups, with counts beside the collections, and the Nations menu has a box to go straight to a nation (see [Section bar](#6-components)). The sections, their groups and the addresses each holds are listed once, in `src/lib/site-navigation.ts`; the longest address that holds a page names its section, so `/stats` belongs to the Library and `/stats/treaties` to Federal law. The side column beside the page shows only where the reader is: the pages the page belongs with (a nation's own pages, or the essays of Foundations or Essays), otherwise the pages of the section being read, then the page's own headings (see [Side column](#6-components)). Long lists, such as every nation, are not in the column. The masthead keeps one row: the site's name, then, evenly spaced, the search control, "Request a Change" as a flag, Print and Download as icons, the Settings cog, which opens the Page settings panel with the theme in it (Trevor, Nov. 26, 2026), and, at the far right, a "Sign in" link that becomes a profile button once someone has signed in (Trevor asked for these on Nov. 22 and 23, 2026). Contribute is in the About menu, under Take part, so the masthead holds only buttons (Trevor, Nov. 24, 2026). Downloads and the changelog are linked from the footer (see [Masthead](#6-components)). Nobody needs to sign in to read the site or to make a request. The footer of every page carries "Request a correction or removal," the requests log, the licenses and the three version numbers ([requests.md](../docs/requests.md#where-the-request-starts)).

Peacemaking has its own menu item and landing page, because the project treats it as its own track, not a variant of ADR ([charter, commitment 3](../docs/charter.md#3-peacemaking-as-its-own-track)).

### Nation pages

Every nation and community with a record in `content/tribes/` gets its own page, built from that record at build time: 575 federally recognized nations today, plus the Native Hawaiian community and territorial Indigenous peoples in their own groups, never described as tribes. Trevor asked for this so that each page makes it easy to report information and to show what is distinctive about each nation.

**Order of sections.** Every page uses the same order, so readers and the nation's staff always know where to look, as the Tribal Law Gateway does with its fixed sections on each nation's page. Trevor chose this layout, the "reference page", from three mockups on Nov. 22, 2026:

1. The nation's name, and the nation's own name for itself, exactly as the nation writes it, once the nation confirms it. The name shown, in the title and in every list, is the name on the recognizing list without the states the list adds after a comma: "Navajo Nation, Arizona, New Mexico, & Utah" shows as "Navajo Nation" (Trevor, Nov. 22, 2026; `displayName` in `src/lib/nation-labels.ts`). A state that belongs to the name stays, as in "Paiute Indian Tribe of Utah". When the two differ, a line under the title gives the listed name as an alias, "Alias: Alturas Indian Rancheria, California", with a citation marker that leads to the list's entry in References, where the citation and its link to the document are (Trevor, Nov. 23, 2026). The search also finds the listed name, and the CSV download keeps it. When they are the same, no line is shown and the name's source is only in the References list (Trevor, Nov. 22, 2026, removed "Name as the recognizing list gives it"). Every other name is a short labeled line in the same small, muted style, each with its citation marker, so a reader can tell what it is (Trevor, Nov. 24, 2026): "Alias:" for the listed name, "Own name:" for a name the nation uses for itself or its people, followed by its language in parentheses where the nation's record names a language with the same tag, as in "Own name: Tsistsistas (Cheyenne)", and "How to say" for a pronunciation copied from its source.
2. Who keeps the page: a badge on the same line as the recognition badges (Trevor, Nov. 23, 2026), "Unclaimed" with a clock until people the nation chose keep the page, then "Claimed by the nation" with a check. The badge opens a small panel (`ReviewStatus.svelte`, a `<details>` that a click elsewhere or Escape closes): for an unclaimed page, "Work for this nation? Claim this page" and "Changes to this page" (Trevor took off the sentence "No one from the nation keeps this page yet" on Nov. 23, 2026); for a claimed page, the offices of the people who keep it (never their names), the date the project confirmed them with the nation's office, "Write to the team" (Trevor, Nov. 23, 2026: "If you claim a page, you have to be willing to take the input"), "Work for this nation? Ask to join its team" and "Changes to this page". "Write to the team" opens the request form as "Write to the nation's team", with "Share my name and contact details with the nation's team so they can write back" ticked; the message reaches the team's list on its edit page and the project, and the team can answer "Wrote back to the sender". The Worker draws the claimed badge in the page's `status` place (`src/lib/server/nation-page-overlay.ts`). A story's "Unreviewed" or "Reviewed by the nation" badge opens the same kind of panel, with a link for each of its nations to claim its page and "Request removal". The panel never prints. Until Nov. 23, 2026 this was a line of its own under the title, with the review status, what it meant and the date the sources were last checked.
3. At a glance (see below), with the government contacts card under it.
4. Government: how the nation's government is set up, as its own constitution or other governing document, its website or an official directory states it ([decision record 0042](../docs/decisions/0042-governance-facts-from-governing-documents.md)). Groups, in this order: form of government (with the legal form federal sources record, such as organized under the Indian Reorganization Act), governing body (name, members, how chosen, term), general council, head of government (title, how chosen, term, other officers), elections, branches of government and separation of powers, courts and judges (courts, how judges are chosen, their term, each protection the document gives them), changing the governing document (how, and whether the Secretary of the Interior must approve), recall and removal, and a last row naming the version of each governing document the facts were read from, linked to its history under Laws and agreements. Each line is one plain fact in the source's own names ("Business Committee", "Chairman"), never a summary of the document; the words of the source that state it are quoted in the References list beside the fact, so a reader can check it. The leader's title from the Bureau of Indian Affairs directory is labeled "in the federal directory" (Nov. 23, 2026).
5. Courts and dispute resolution: one heading for each kind of service (Courts, Peacemaking, Mediation and so on), so a kind is never repeated after a name; under Courts, how appeals move from the court that hears a case first to the highest court, where sources record it ([data model](../docs/data-model.md#courts-and-the-path-of-appeal)). Where there is such a path, it is drawn as in the approved layout (Nov. 22, 2026): each court a bordered box with an icon (a gavel where cases start, a landmark for each court appeals go to), joined by "Appeals go to" on a line between them, across the section's full width with no label column, and the nation's peacemaking in its own box "Beside the courts", to the right where the section is at least 30 rem wide and under the courts where it is narrower (`NationServices.svelte`). A court's place follows its name and wraps whole, never leaving a separator behind. Rows of facts after the courts, such as admission to practice, continue the same list under one rule. A nation's judicial branch or judiciary (`judicial-branch`) is the court system as a whole, so it has its own "Judicial branch" heading before Courts and is never listed as a court, here or in At a glance; a directory's bare "Tribal Court" entry at the branch's own address is shown as the branch (Nov. 22, 2026).
6. Laws and agreements: where the nation publishes its laws and any published dispute resolution law, linked, not reproduced, until the nation permits more ([data model, permission statuses](../docs/data-model.md#permission-statuses)); then each of the nation's documents that has recorded versions, such as a gaming compact or a constitution ([decision record 0018](../docs/decisions/0018-document-version-history.md)). This one section replaced "Published dispute resolution law" and "Document history" (Trevor, Nov. 22, 2026). Two sources listing the same document (the same title, ignoring case, and the same address) make one line with both markers; two different links with the same title, such as two "Tribal code" links, are told apart by the site each is on, and a document that has its own history is not listed again as a link.
7. Jurisdiction: who has authority over what, as federal law and federal lists record it for the nation, such as Public Law 280 or a designated agent for Indian Child Welfare Act notices (`jurisdiction` in the record). Each line is the record's own cited wording.
8. Nearby Nations (heading renamed by Trevor, Nov. 23, 2026): for each state, the other nations sources record there, alphabetically, or a link to the state's list when there are more than 30. A plain list, never a ranking or comparison; not printed (Trevor, Nov. 22, 2026). It starts folded, as the approved layout has it.
9. Notices and Labels from Local Contexts.
10. Sources, each with links back to the markers that cite it, followed by the facts on the page that come from it.

**Legal sections always show.** Government, Courts and dispute resolution, Laws and agreements and Jurisdiction appear on every nation's page, even with nothing to show, because this is a legal reference and a reader needs to see whether recourse may be available (Trevor, Nov. 22, 2026). An empty section says so in one line: a short answer in bold, then, once a search is finished, how far it went, from the nation's search log (`src/lib/search-status.ts`). "None found" means every planned place was searched, and the line names how many places, the kinds of place and the date; "None Identified", with nothing after it, means the search is not finished or has not been made (Trevor, Nov. 23, 2026); "None shown yet" means a search found a lead that is still being checked. Laws and agreements gets a "Constitution" row and a "Published laws" row when either is missing (articles of association count as a governing document; a corporate charter counts as neither). The page never says that a court or a law does not exist, since a nation can settle disputes through its council or keep its laws off the web, and a finished search for courts ends by suggesting the reader ask the nation's government office.

**Coverage.** `/nations/coverage` (improvements report, idea 6; Trevor approved the mockup on Nov. 24, 2026, `design/coverage/`) shows where the nation pages have gaps, so contributors and nations can see where help is needed. A bar chart counts the nation pages with nothing on record in each section (Government contacts, Government, Courts, Constitution, Published laws, Jurisdiction), and each bar opens the table below filtered to those nations. The table has a row for each nation and a column for each section, with a check mark or "None Identified". The tests are the nation page's own (`src/lib/server/coverage.ts`, which the nation page shares for the Constitution and Published laws rows), so the two always agree. Government contacts is counted although the nation page leaves out an empty contacts section rather than saying None Identified.

Each section from At a glance on, including Sources, is collapsible (Trevor, Nov. 22, 2026): its heading is the summary of a `<details>` element that starts open (`Collapsible.svelte`), so it works without scripts, and every folded section opens before printing so paper always has the whole page. The glossary pages' References section uses the same component. The review status line is not a section and does not fold. Sources is spaced like the other sections, with no rule above it.

The page no longer has "About this Nation" or its recorded features, the short sentences generated from the record's facts (Trevor, Nov. 22, 2026): the facts they repeated are in At a glance and Jurisdiction. A short description the nation writes or approves can open the page once a nation sends one; until then the page shows no placeholder for it, and the site never writes that description for the nation.

**Document headings.** Each document with recorded versions is headed by a short name: the nation's name is dropped from the front, "State of Arizona Gaming Compact" reads "Gaming compact with Arizona", and the nation's own constitution reads "Constitution" (`documentHeading` in `src/lib/server/document-history.ts`). The sidebar lists the headings under Laws and agreements. Under a heading, one line names the versions in effect. Each version's name links to a public copy of its text, the source's own address or else its archived copy (Trevor, Nov. 22, 2026); the project's private captures are never linked. Beside it are the markers of its sources, then a pill: "In effect" filled, any other recorded status outlined ("Replaced" for a superseded text, as the approved layout words it), so color is not the only cue, and no pill where the sources record no status. A line under it gives its dates, grouped where they are the same ("Published and listed Feb. 5, 2003"), and what it did to earlier versions (amended, replaced). The versions are placed in time on the page's one Timeline (see Timeline under Components), not on a line of their own (Trevor asked on Nov. 23, 2026 for a single timeline). The section opens straight onto the documents, with no introductory sentence (Trevor, Nov. 22, 2026). It shows no clause summaries or clause comparisons and no generated summary of the document, because a small model wrote them from excerpts (Trevor, Nov. 22, 2026); the records keep them for review.

**At a glance.** A filled panel, like a reference work's fact box. The nation's seal or logo heads it, centered, when its reuse status allows showing it ([official-images.md](../docs/official-images.md)); it has no caption (Trevor, Nov. 22, 2026): its Sources entry credits the nation's website and says the image belongs to the nation, which does not endorse this site, and the nation asks for its removal through Request a Change. Selecting the seal opens it larger in the image viewer (see section 6, "Image viewer"). In dark mode the seal sits on a light plate (the `image-backing` token, class `.image-backing` in global.css), since many seals are dark lines on a clear ground drawn for light paper; it is never recolored. The same plate backs a seal wherever it shows: the search panel, link previews and the map's preview card (Trevor, Nov. 23, 2026). Under the seal is a label and value table:

- Recognition: one line for each status, with the markers of every source that gives it, and under it the short form of each legal basis, such as "91 Fed. Reg. 4102, 4104" (the nation's entry on the Bureau of Indian Affairs list) and "Pub. L. No. 93-197" (the Act that restored it), linked to the official text. The full wording is in the References list.
- States with a presence: the states in one line, and under it, in smaller type, what sources record in each, worded as in the approved layout: "Land in all three; government office in Arizona" (`presenceNote` in `src/lib/nation-labels.ts`).
- Bureau of Indian Affairs region, spelled out: the region, linked to the regions view of the big map, and under it the agency that serves the nation (Trevor asked for the region on Nov. 22, 2026).
- Governing body and Head of government: the name of the body and the title of the office, as plain text: a link right before the citation marker leaves the marker too small a touch target. The head's title from the federal directory comes first, being the newest; otherwise the governing document's (Nov. 23, 2026).
- Languages, and "Member of" for organizations of nations the nation belongs to, such as the Coalition of Large Tribes (`memberships` in the record), where sources record them. Each organization's name links to the home page of its own website, taken from the source that records the membership when the organization itself published it (Trevor, Nov. 22, 2026).
- No courts or laws: the page's own sections show them, so the panel does not repeat them (Trevor, Nov. 22, 2026, after a short time when it listed "Courts and forums" and "Published law").

The map comes last in the panel. In a narrow panel each label sits above its values.

**Layout.** A nation's page is a record page (see Record pages below): the text, held to the reading measure, and beside it the At a glance panel and the contacts card, whenever the page's own column has room for both; below them the sections widen to the full column. In the page's order At a glance comes right after the title, so on a phone the key facts come first, and every section of the main column follows it, so selecting text in the main column never takes in the panel (Trevor, Nov. 22, 2026, found a selection pulling in the map, recognition and states when the panel came first in the middle of the text).

**Info pages.** Every page about one item uses one layout, like a wiki article with its infobox (Trevor, Nov. 24, 2026: "We should create and use a generalized wiki info page for all items"): `src/lib/components/InfoPage.svelte` puts together the breadcrumbs, the title, other names (each with its source marker), a one-line lead, a status badge, At a glance with the picture and its credit over the key facts (`FactGroups`), the page's own sections and References. People (Justices, Presidents, the biographies, contributors and sponsors), the Courts of the Supreme Court, glossary terms, stories, forms, license texts and the Bureau of Indian Affairs regions and Indian Health Service Areas use it (Trevor, Nov. 24, 2026). The page's own references go in References, so a federal office lists the sources of the nation records that name it with the fact each supports. A page with needs of its own, such as a nation's map and contacts, builds on RecordPage directly. Every section of an item page folds, as a nation page's do (Trevor, Nov. 24, 2026: "each header section in a page, such as a case page, should be collapsible"): a page writes each section as `Collapsible.svelte`, never as a bare `<section>` with an `<h2>`, and a case's or a law's box parts (Background, Question, Holding, Summary, Quotes, Treatment) each fold on their own (`RecordBoxSections.svelte`). Every section starts open on each visit.

**At a glance only where it earns its place.** Trevor chose this on Nov. 26, 2026, after a review of 103 pages found the panel strong on Justices, Supreme Court cases, nations and federal offices but mostly repeating the title and lead on treaties, statutes, courts and stubs, and running about two screens before any section on a nation's page on a phone (project files `design/at-a-glance-review/`). The rule is in `src/lib/info-page.ts` and `GlancePanel.svelte`. A fact whose words the title or lead already say is left out, such as a treaty's citation, kind and date; a fact with its own source markers stays. The panel shows only with at least three facts left (`glancePanelMinimum`), or a picture, map or other panel. With one or two, the facts follow the lead in muted type, on its line when the lead is a citation and on the line under it when the lead is a sentence (`FactLine.svelte`); with none there is no panel. A court's lead carries the years of its opinions and cases, so its panel keeps only the nation and the reporters. On a phone, where the panel comes before the sections, it shows its first four facts (`glancePhoneRows`) and keeps the rest, and a nation's map, behind a "Show all" button; beside the text, without scripts and on paper, everything shows.

**Record pages.** Pages about one record use one layout, `src/lib/components/RecordPage.svelte`, so the pages read alike and a new kind of record gets the same layout for free (Trevor asked for a framework that is not tied to nations on Nov. 22, 2026). The layout knows nothing about the record. A page passes five parts as snippets: `title` (the page's heading), `introduction` (what belongs under the title, such as other names, a lead and badges), `glance` (the key facts and other short panels, optional), the record's sections as the body, and `sources` (optional). The key facts go in `GlancePanel.svelte`, a filled box with an optional picture on top, such as a seal, and an "At a glance" heading the reader can fold away. Inside it a page usually puts `FactGroups`, whose labels are plain text. When the page's own column is at least 52 rem wide, the glance column sits on the right under the title, its top level with the first line under the title on every page however much the introduction holds (Trevor, Nov. 24, 2026: "The right side starts much too low on some pages, it is never the same"; until then it started after the badges, other names and pronunciations, 65 to 296 pixels lower depending on the page), two fifths of the column or whatever room the text width leaves, whichever is wider, but at least 17 rem and at most the `side-column` token, and the introduction and then the sections run beside it on the left. The introduction floats left in the width the glance column leaves, and the sections start under it, so the space above the first section stays as it was. The glance column floats, as an infobox does: once the sections pass its bottom they use the whole width under it, so a long page leaves no empty column on the right (Trevor asked on Nov. 23, 2026). Running text stays at the reading measure there; a label and value list keeps its label column plus the measure; timelines, lists set in columns (such as nearby nations), tables and the References list use the full width. Blocks that draw a box or a rule (notices, panels, timelines, figures, rules) keep clear of the glance column rather than running under it. A section drawn for the full width, such as a Court's chart of its Justices, takes the class `clear-glance` and starts below the glance column, since beside it the chart shrank until its names and years were hard to read (Trevor, Nov. 26, 2026). The glance column is drawn above the sections, so a section whose box runs under it never covers its links (Trevor found the contact card's email, phone and contact page could not be clicked on Nov. 24, 2026). Near that width the text column narrows first, to about 45 characters at the threshold. The threshold follows the width of `<main>`, not the window, so it counts the sidebar. Until Nov. 22, 2026 it was 62 rem, which stacked the columns on a 1,280 pixel laptop with the sidebar open but not with it hidden (Trevor found this; the page had 915 pixels of room against the 992 it needed). When the glance column is under 22 rem, each fact's label sits above its values, so names such as a heritage language do not break mid-word. Links to other websites inside a record page open in a new tab and show the external-link icon after the link, kept on one line with the link's last word and its source markers.

**Section headings.** Each section heading of an item page has a rule under it, the width of the text column, so a reader can see where one section ends and the next begins (Trevor, Nov. 26, 2026: "we need better delineation like a line between sections on a page"; he picked a rule under each heading over a line between sections and a shaded heading bar, from mockups in `design/section-dividers`). The rule is 2 pixels in the `line` color and belongs to the heading row in `Collapsible.svelte`, so beside the At a glance column it stops at the column's edge. Sections inside the At a glance box have no rule, since the box sets them apart already. A list of facts that starts right under the rule leaves out its own top line, so the two never make a double line.

**The nation's map.** The map in At a glance has the big map's views, so a reader can see more than the states (Trevor, Nov. 22, 2026; [decision record 0033](../docs/decisions/0033-nation-page-map-has-the-big-maps-views.md)), each with its own caption naming its source. A "Map view" searchable dropdown (see [Searchable dropdown](#6-components)) picks the view, from the same list of views as the big map (`src/lib/map-views.ts`), in place of the earlier row of tabs (Trevor, Nov. 23, 2026). Without scripts every view shows, one after another. A view is offered only when the record has what it needs, and a page whose record lists no state has no map.

- **States** (the first view) shades the states and territories where the record's cited `states` facts place the nation, with each shaded state's postal code printed on it, so color is not the only cue. It shows only what is relevant to the nation (Trevor, Nov. 22, 2026): the boxes that hold its states and no others, with the contiguous United States zoomed to the region around them but never closer than 40 percent of the country's width, so neighbors stay in view. Alaska, Hawaiʻi or a territory shown without the main box is drawn up to three times larger. Zoomed boxes use more detailed outlines than the full-size small map, and only the states in view go into the page (`src/lib/presence-map.ts`).
- **Region** outlines the nation's Bureau of Indian Affairs region among its neighbors and names it on the map, zoomed no closer than a quarter of the country's width. Its caption says the lines are simplified for a national map and links to all the regions.
- **Land areas** draws the Census Bureau's 2020 areas whose codes the nation's record lists (`census_areas`), with a dot where an area is too small to see, and lists the areas by name and kind. Its caption says the outlines are the Census Bureau's, simplified, and are not a statement of the nation's own boundaries, as on the big map ([decision record 0028](../docs/decisions/0028-map-adds-census-land-areas.md)). Where the browser can draw it, this view is the tiled map instead (`TileMap.svelte`, see section 11), which Trevor chose on 2026-11-23 ([decision record 0039](../docs/decisions/0039-website-map-uses-maplibre-and-our-own-tiles.md)). It shows only the nation's own areas, at the Census Bureau's full detail over the basemap and fitted to them, so even a rancheria of a third of a square kilometer shows its streets. The drawn view stays for print, offline reading and devices without WebGL. The States and Region views stay drawn.

State outlines are written into the page once and shared by the views (`NationMap.svelte`), so three views do not triple the map's size.

**Requesting a change.** One "Request a Change" button sits in the masthead on every page (Trevor, Nov. 22, 2026), an icon-only flag named by its label and tooltip, right after search (Trevor, Nov. 23, 2026); pages carry no report links of their own. The dialog was named "Report a problem" until Trevor renamed it on Nov. 27, 2026. It has two columns (Trevor, Nov. 27, 2026), stacking into one on phones. At the top of the left column a dropdown (see [Dropdown](#6-components)) asks what the reader would like to do: correct (picked when the dialog opens), add a nation that is missing, restrict, remove, or ask a question, with the picked choice's hint under it (for a missing nation, that one federal or state government source naming the nation is enough). Contribute has its own button. Under the dropdown the left column holds the email address to reply to (email only, Trevor, Nov. 27, 2026) and the optional questions about the reader (name, nation, role, writing for the government). The right column holds "Web page", filled in with the record on a nation page (`tribes/<id>`) or glossary page or else the page's address; "Details", with the choice's question as its hint; and the files, added one at a time with "Add a file", each listed with a button to remove it, up to five (`FileAdder.svelte`). Under both columns, "Terms of Submission" lists each term the reader accepts on its own line, in a short box about four lines tall that scrolls for the rest (Trevor asked for it smaller, Nov. 27, 2026), with one required "I accept these terms" box, then the Submit button ([requests.md](../docs/requests.md), step 6). Changing the dropdown keeps what the reader typed. The request page itself keeps its section headings and asks everything in one column. The form is sent in place and the dialog shows the reference number and what happens next. Without scripts the button is a link to the request page with the record filled in, for example `/requests?record=tribes/<id>`, which asks the same questions. The pages no longer carry an "Is your nation missing?" line. This replaces the Nov. 21 rule of one link at the top of each page, which itself replaced a link beside every fact.

**Sources.** Each fact on a nation page ends with a numbered marker for each of its sources, as Wikipedia cites, and the sources are listed together in a "References" section at the end of the page (Trevor, Nov. 22, 2026; called "Sources" until Trevor renamed it on Nov. 23, 2026, so it never clashes with a story's "Source"; this replaced the rule of Nov. 21, 2026, that kept citations out of the text and listed them only at the end). Each source appears once, numbered in the order the page first cites it, with links back to the markers that cite it, followed by every fact on the page that comes from it, grouped by the heading the fact appears under, with the place in the source and the date the value was true. A fact with several facts from one source behind it, such as a state with both reservation and trust land, gets one marker for that source. See [Citations](#6-components) for how markers behave.

**A compact References list.** So the list stays short however many sources a page cites, Trevor chose on Nov. 23, 2026, from three mockups, a list grouped by the part of the page and folded (`SourceList.svelte`, `src/lib/source-groups.ts`). Each source is one row between thin lines, led by its number, which links back to the first place it is cited. Under the citation, a source cited more than once gets a line such as "Cited 7 times: a b c d e f g", with a link back to each place. The facts each source supports stay hidden until the reader presses the one "Expand all" icon button at the end of the References heading row (chevrons up and down; Trevor asked for a single control on Nov. 27, 2026, in place of the separate "Expand" and "Open all groups" buttons). It opens every group and shows the facts, opening the section first if it is folded, and reads "Collapse all" until a group is folded again; without scripts, and on paper, the facts always show. A list of more than 8 sources from two or more parts of the page folds into groups, one for each part, in the page's order. Each source goes under the part that first cites it (`SourceUses.part()`), so on a nation page the groups are At a glance, Map, Courts and dispute resolution, Laws and agreements and Jurisdiction. Each group is a `<details>` that starts folded and shows its count and its numbers, such as "12 sources · 15–26". Within a group that covers several documents, a small label names each document, such as "Gaming compact with Arizona", before its sources." Each group still opens on its own, and "Expand all" opens them all. A marker or a link back opens the group its source is in before the page moves there, and printing opens every group. A shorter list, or one with a single part, such as an essay's or a glossary entry's, stays one list of the same rows. The heading shows the number of sources after it in a count badge (`CountBadge.svelte`), as every count beside a heading or label does, never a loose number (Trevor, Nov. 23, 2026).

**Contacts.** A phone number is a `tel:` link that opens the device's dialer; an email address is a `mailto:` link. Fax numbers stay as text. A value that cannot be read reliably, such as a phone number that is not ten digits, stays as text (Trevor, 2026-11-21). The contacts appear as a contact card, described under [Components](#6-components).

**Missing information.** Empty fields are left out rather than shown as "unknown," so a page never suggests a nation lacks something it may simply not have published. The page has no "Add it" line for missed sources (Trevor, Nov. 22, 2026); readers offer them through Contribute in the sidebar or Request a Change in the masthead.

**Restricted and withdrawn records.** A record marked restricted gets no page at all. A withdrawn record's page says only that the material was withdrawn at the nation's request ([charter, commitment 6](../docs/charter.md#6-the-right-to-correct-restrict-or-withdraw)).

**In print.** The book's profiles follow the same order, so the website and Part III read alike. The page template uses relative units and no fixed page size, because the book's trim size is not yet decided (project choice).

- An icon sits beside words. The only icon-only controls are the search icon, the Settings cog and the theme symbols in its panel, the profile button (Trevor asked for the search icon, the cog and the profile icon on Nov. 22, 2026), the Star button, the Follow button, the X that closes the search panel and the Modal's close button (an X, asked for by Trevor the same day), and the same X on the image viewer and the viewer's Download button beside it (Trevor, Nov. 23, 2026). Each carries an `aria-label` that names it and a matching `title` tooltip, because Lucide hides icons from screen readers by default.[^lucide-a11y] The text alignment choice in Settings and in the Page settings panel also shows only symbols, as Trevor asked on Nov. 22, 2026; each symbol is a radio button with a hidden name that screen readers read, and the picked choice's name and hint show in words just below it.

- In print, Typst draws `currentColor` as black, which suits the black-and-white interior.
- The one exception is the GitHub mark (`GithubMark.svelte`), GitHub's own logo from Primer Octicons (MIT License, `LICENSES/Octicons.txt`), because Lucide no longer ships brand icons. It appears only beside the word "GitHub" on a link that goes there (see Links stay on the site, below).

**Links stay on the site.** Three standing rules from Trevor, Nov. 22, 2026:

- *No link sends a reader to GitHub unless its words say so,* such as the footer's "GitHub" link with the GitHub mark, or "Every change to it on GitHub." What the project wrote for readers is a page on the site: the charter is `/charter`, and the licenses by material are `/licenses`, with each full license text under it. They are rendered at build time from the same files, `docs/charter.md`, `LICENSE.md` and `LICENSES/` (`src/lib/server/project-documents.ts`), so the files stay the one source. Inside them, a link to the other shown document goes to its page; a link to any other repository file becomes plain words; a link to github.com is followed by "(GitHub)"; and each page ends with links to its file and its history on GitHub, which say so. In code, link to the site page (`/charter`, `/licenses`), never to `repositoryLink` plus a file path, unless the link's words name GitHub. The co-authors' workspace may link to a record's file and history on GitHub, saying so.
- *No page names the project's internal documents:* no "(decision record 0007)," no file names such as `requests.md`, and no pointers to the roadmap, the outline, the data model or other specifications in `docs/`. Those explain the project to its maintainers; the site explains things to readers in its own words. Where the charter or `LICENSE.md` cross-references an internal document, the reference sits between `<!-- repository-only -->` and `<!-- /repository-only -->`: GitHub hides the markers and shows the text, and the site leaves it out.
- *On nation pages, links to other websites open in a new tab* (sources, laws, constitutions, a nation's own website), with `rel="noopener noreferrer"` and a hidden "Opens in a new tab" note that screen readers announce through `aria-describedby` (`src/lib/new-tab-links.ts`, WCAG technique G201). Links within the site open in the same tab.

`tools/check-built-site.mjs` enforces the first two: it fails when a link to github.com names GitHub neither in its words nor just before them, and when a page's text says "decision record" with a number or names a `.md` file.

**Password fields.** Every password field is `src/lib/components/PasswordInput.svelte`: a borderless icon button inside the field's right edge, Lucide's `eye` (`eye-off` while the password shows), with no word beside it, as Trevor asked on Nov. 22, 2026. **Date fields.** Every date is `src/lib/components/DateField.svelte`, every time `TimeField.svelte`, and a date with a time `DateTimeField.svelte` (Trevor asked for one shared field on Nov. 23, 2026, and chose this style from mockups that day; the mockups are in the project files under design/date-field). Without scripts each field is the browser's own date or time box, styled like the other fields.

**Form errors.** A form that comes back with a problem follows the GOV.UK error summary and error message patterns.[^govuk-error-summary] `src/lib/components/ErrorSummary.svelte` puts a box headed "There is a problem" above the form, with each problem as a link to its field. The same words appear again above the field, in bold, after a hidden "Error:", and the field gets `aria-invalid` and `aria-describedby` pointing at them. The page title starts with "Error: ". Messages say what to do, not what went wrong in the code: "Use at least 15 characters (this one has 9)", not "Invalid password". This meets WCAG 2.2 success criteria 3.3.1 and 3.3.3.

**Settings and Account.** Choices live in two places (decision record 0045; Trevor chose this on Nov. 23, 2026). **Settings**, under the cog, change how the site looks and works on the screen in front of you: everyone has them, signed in or not, they apply at once and are kept on the device. **Account settings** hold who you are to the project and what it keeps about you: only signed-in people have them, they are kept with the account on the server and saved with a Save button, on full pages listed in the account menu under the masthead's profile button (Profile and Credit, Following, Your Uploads, Nation Team, Sign-In and Security, Email and Notifications, Your Orders, Sponsorship, Your Data; see [COLLABORATION.md](COLLABORATION.md#account-pages)). Settings for a workspace tool stay beside the tool. A signed-in person can choose, in This device, to use their settings on every device they sign in on.

**Page settings.** The masthead's Settings cog (Lucide's `settings` icon) opens a small panel right under it, its right edge on the cog's, with the settings that change how the page looks (Trevor asked for this on Nov. 26, 2026, so what is good for the page sits by the page, and so the theme button could leave the masthead; mockup `design/page-settings-popover`, option B, which Trevor chose). **Settings.** "More settings" in the Page settings panel opens the Modal with seven sections (project choice, asked for by Trevor on Nov. 21, 2026): **Text** (size, body font, heading font, alignment, text width), **Colors** (theme, contrast), **Motion**, **Citations**, **Glossary**, **Editing** (only for people with workspace access) and **This device** (saved pages, updates, reset and delete; "Saved for offline" was renamed Data on Nov. 22, 2026, at Trevor's request, and Data was renamed This device on Nov. 23, 2026, so it is not taken for "Your data" in Account settings). The reading settings are:

- **Text size** (a slider, asked for by Trevor on Nov. 22, 2026): eleven steps, 50, 62.5, 75, 87.5, 100, 112.5, 125, 137.5, 150, 175 and 200 percent, so a reader can reach the 200 percent WCAG 1.4.4 names for resizing text[^wcag-144] and, as Trevor asked on Nov. 22, 2026, go down to half size. The current size shows beside the label and is read out as "125 percent"; there is no hint line under the slider (Trevor removed it on Nov. 22, 2026). On paper, 50 and 62.5 percent print at 75 percent, about 10 points, so printed text stays readable. Arrow keys move one step, Home and End jump to the ends. Small and large letter A's at the ends show which way is larger. Only text grows, as Trevor asked on Nov. 22, 2026: the setting changes `--text-scale`, which multiplies every font size token (`src/lib/design-tokens.ts`), while boxes, spaces and controls keep their rem sizes. Controls still grow where their text needs room, because their heights are minimums. The root font size stays at the browser's own setting, so a reader's browser text size still scales the whole page.
- **Body font** (a dropdown): Atkinson Hyperlegible Next (the default), Noto Serif or OpenDyslexic (see [Type](#type)), with no description under them (Trevor removed the device's own font and the descriptions on Nov. 22, 2026; a stored "device" choice falls back to the default). Each choice in the list, and the picked choice in the closed dropdown, is shown in its own typeface, as Trevor asked on Nov. 22, 2026, so a reader sees a font before picking it. It is stored as `font`, the name it had when it set every font on the page.
- **Heading font** (a dropdown): Same as body (the default), Atkinson Hyperlegible Next, Noto Serif or OpenDyslexic, shown the same way, "Same as body" in the body font. Trevor asked on Nov. 27, 2026 to "allow changing body fonts and heading fonts" (mockup `design/spacing-and-fonts`). It sets `--font-family-heading`, which every `h1` to `h6` uses and which follows `--font-family-reading` until the reader picks another font.
- **No text spacing setting.** Settings had Standard and Wide spacing until Nov. 27, 2026, when Trevor removed it. WCAG 2.2, 1.4.12 does not ask a site to offer one: it asks that nothing be lost when a reader sets line height to 1.5, paragraph spacing to 2 times, letter spacing to 0.12 times and word spacing to 0.16 times the font size with their own tools, such as a browser extension or user style sheet.[^wcag-1412] Pages must keep meeting that, so text boxes have no fixed heights and nothing that holds text clips it.
- **Text alignment:** Left aligned (the default) or Justified, asked for by Trevor on Nov. 21, 2026, shown as Lucide's `text-align-start` and `text-align-justify` symbols in one bordered strip (see Icons above). The picked symbol is filled and has a bar under it, so color is not the only cue. Justified applies to reading text in the page's main area (paragraphs, list items, definitions and quotations), not to headings, tables, forms or the masthead, and turns on automatic hyphenation (`hyphens: auto`), which uses each element's `lang` attribute to pick the hyphenation rules.[^mdn-hyphens] Links are never hyphenated, so nation names, citations and addresses stay whole. Left stays the default because WCAG's enhanced visual presentation criterion asks that text not be justified, since uneven word spacing makes reading harder for some readers.[^wcag-148]
- **Text width:** a slider from 40 to 100 percent of the page's column, in steps of 10, with "Narrow" and "Full" at its ends. The default is 100 percent: running text fills the column. Trevor asked on Nov. 23, 2026 for one percentage setting for the whole site in place of the three fixed widths (Narrow 44ch, Standard 51ch and Wide 58ch, approved from the Wikiwand review on Nov. 22, 2026), because text, notices and form boxes broke lines early with space to spare. A reader who wants shorter lines, such as the 80 characters WCAG's enhanced visual presentation criterion asks for,[^wcag-148] can narrow it. It sets `--layout-measure` in `cqi` (a percentage of `<main>`) through the root's `data-line-width` attribute, so every block held to the text width follows it.
- **Theme:** Match this device, Light or Dark (see [section 8](#8-themes)).
- **Contrast:** Standard or More. More turns gray text to ink and light dividing lines to the control border color; no new colors are added.
- **Motion:** Match this device (the default) or Reduce motion. Reduce stops every slide, fade and glide on this device even when the device itself allows motion; it sets `data-motion="reduce"` on the root (see Motion below).
- **When you select a note number** (Citations): go to the References list and preview the source when you point at the number (the default), go to the References list with no preview, or show the note under the paragraph. The number stays a link to the source's entry in the References list, so without scripts it goes there.
- **Links to sources** (Citations): the original page and the archived copy (the default), the original only, or the archived copy where there is one. An archived copy still opens when the original has moved or gone, which matters for tribal codes whose addresses change ([citation style](../docs/citation-style.md)).
- **Previews** (Glossary): on (the default) or off, for defined terms and links to nation pages. See [Glossary link and hover card](#6-components) and Link previews below.
- **Groups in long References lists** (Citations): Folded (the default) or Open. Open shows every group of a long References list open when the page loads (`data-source-groups="open"`, read by `SourceList.svelte`); each group can still be folded.
- **Other people's cursors** (Editing): show cursors with names (the default), without names, or hide them, while editing an essay together. It changes only what this reader sees (`data-other-cursors` on the root, rules in `global.css`). The color others see for you belongs in Account, because other people see it.

**This device** holds five things. For a signed-in person it starts with "Use these settings on every device I sign in on" (`src/lib/settings-everywhere.ts`): turning it on copies this device's settings and theme to the account; later changes on any signed-in device are sent there; another device takes the account's copy after sign-in, when it is newer than the one that device last saw; turning it off deletes the account's copy and every device keeps what it has. Starred pages, print choices and saved pages stay on each device. The device's own copy still paints the page first, so nothing flickers and offline reading is unchanged. The other four are these. Saving for offline and updates are described in [section 10](#10-offline-and-installable). "Reset settings to defaults" puts the reading settings and theme back at once. "Export to a file" downloads `model-tribal-adr-code-data-YYYY-MM-DD.json` with the reading settings, theme, print choices, starred pages with their notes, and the list of parts kept for offline reading (not the saved pages themselves); "Import from a file" reads such a file, refuses anything else, and asks "Replace your settings, theme and print choices on this device with the ones in this file?" before applying it (asked for by Trevor on Nov. 22, 2026; `src/lib/reader-data.ts`). Starred pages in the file are added to the reader's own, and the reader's own notes are kept. If the file lists parts or pages chosen for offline reading, the reader is told to choose them on the Read offline page too. "Delete data on this device" lists what the site keeps (Settings, Print choices, Starred pages, Saved pages), all ticked; the reader can untick parts, and the button reads "Delete all data" or "Delete chosen data." Deleting asks once more in a box under the list, which takes focus: "Delete … from this device? This cannot be undone," with "Yes, delete" (danger color, with a trash icon and bold question so color is not the only cue) and "Cancel." Deleting saved pages forgets every choice and removes every saved page and copy through the service worker, but keeps the site's own files, so the site still opens offline.

Each change applies at once and is kept in the browser's local storage on that device, so it works offline; nothing is sent to the server unless the reader chose to use their settings on every device. An inline script in `app.html` applies the stored settings before the page paints. Focus returns to the cog, or to whatever opened it, when the dialog closes. Text size and font are dropdowns (see [Dropdown](#6-components) below), asked for by Trevor on Nov. 22, 2026; the other choices are radio buttons in fieldsets, labels above hints. Without scripts the Settings cog is hidden, since its settings cannot work. The theme was a one-press sun and moon button in the masthead from Nov. 21, 2026; on Nov. 26, 2026 Trevor moved it into the Page settings panel, where it is one press after the cog and offers "Match this device" too.

**Motion.** Trevor asked on Nov. 22, 2026 for one central framework for animation. He chose to build it without an animation library such as anime.js. Pages were already near the JavaScript budget (section 11), and CSS, Svelte's own transitions and the browser's Web Animations and View Transitions cover every use. The framework has three parts.

- *Tokens.* The `motion` group in `design-tokens.json` holds four durations and three easing curves (standard, enter and exit). Short (120 ms) is for small changes in place, such as a turning arrow. Medium (200 ms) is for something appearing or moving. Long (320 ms) is for larger movements, such as chart bars. Highlight (1.6 s) is how long the mark on a linked source takes to fade. The build turns them into `--motion-duration-*` and `--motion-easing-*` custom properties.
- *The module.* `src/lib/motion.ts` is the only place a script starts movement. It holds Svelte transitions (`fade`, `rise` and `slide`), `animate:move` for lists, a Web Animations helper, `glide` for numbers CSS cannot animate (such as the map's `viewBox`) and the page cross-fade. Every helper reads the tokens, and every one returns a duration of 0 when motion is reduced.
- *The check.* `tools/check-motion-comes-from-tokens.mjs` (`npm run check:motion`, run in continuous integration) fails on a time or easing written into a transition or animation. It also fails on a script animation started outside the module.

Motion happens only in answer to something the reader does, never on page load or to draw attention, with one exception: the rolling row of sponsors on the home page, which Trevor asked for on Nov. 27, 2026 (below). Nothing moves when the device asks for less motion[^mdn-reduced-motion] or the reader picks Reduce motion in Settings. Then every token duration is 0, and a site-wide rule turns off any transition or animation left. Paper shows only end states, because printing finishes every animation first.

Where motion is used:

- The map glides to a new zoom when a reader presses a zoom button or key, or tabs to a place off screen. A drag, pinch or scroll still follows the hand at once.
- The citation window eases in. Dialogs fade in as they rise into place.
- A folding section opens by its height in browsers that can animate to a height of `auto` (Chrome and Edge as of Nov. 2026). Other browsers open it at once.
- The source or note a link goes to arrives marked in the `selection` teal, which fades to the resting highlight (it was the fill-in yellow until Trevor asked for no more yellow boxes on Nov. 27, 2026). The highlight goes about 4 seconds after arrival, or as soon as the reader clicks or taps elsewhere (Trevor, Nov. 27, 2026: it "would not go away"; `src/lib/arrival-highlight.ts`). Without scripts the browser's `:target` keeps it until the address changes.
- Pages cross-fade through View Transitions. Changes within one page, such as a new search, do not fade.
- Folding arrows turn at the short duration.
- The row of sponsors at the bottom of the home page rolls to the left at an even speed, the roll duration for each sponsor's tile with the steady easing, only when the tiles are too many to fit (`src/lib/components/SponsorRow.svelte`; docs/sponsorship.md, "The home page row"). It stops while the pointer is over it or a tile has focus, and a Pause button stops it until pressed again, as WCAG 2.2 success criterion 2.2.2 asks of anything that moves by itself for more than five seconds.[^wcag-222] With Reduce motion it never moves, and the tiles wrap onto more lines instead.

Charts, when built, are the one place an animation library might be weighed again, loaded only on those pages.

**Searchable dropdown.** One shared dropdown a reader can type into, `src/lib/components/SearchableDropdown.svelte`, for picking one choice from a list that will grow, such as the map's views (Trevor, Nov. 23, 2026). It follows the WAI-ARIA editable combobox pattern with list autocomplete. Choices can be grouped under a name, and each can have a one-line description and extra search words ("BIA" finds the Bureau of Indian Affairs regions). Typing narrows the list to choices where every typed word starts a word of the name, group, description or search words, or appears inside one; names that start with what was typed come first, then names with a word that starts with it, then the rest, so "hop" lists Hopi before Bishop Paiute. It takes the same form field options as the plain dropdown below. Down and Up open the list and move; Enter picks; Escape closes the list and puts back the current choice; Tab leaves without changing it. A status line tells screen readers how many choices match. The choice the keys are on and the picked choice look as in the plain dropdown below. It is hidden in print, where every view is printed.

**Rounded switch.** One shared switch for a few short choices that each show a different panel, `src/lib/components/RoundedSwitch.svelte` (Trevor, Nov. 27, 2026: "should create common component"; mockup B1 in `design/citation-styles/rounded-switch/`). The choices sit in one rounded track, and the chosen one is a rounded pill filled in the action color, in bold type, not color alone. It follows the WAI-ARIA tabs pattern: one tab stop, the arrow keys move between choices and choose, and Home and End jump to the first and last.[^apg-tabs] The page draws the panel and gives its id. The Cite dropdown's citation styles are the first to use it.

**Dropdown.** One shared dropdown, `src/lib/components/Dropdown.svelte`, for picking one choice from a short list where a choice should be shown as it will look, such as the font setting. It follows the WAI-ARIA select-only combobox pattern rather than the browser's own `<select>`, because a `<select>` list cannot show each choice in its own typeface on every browser and device.[^apg-combobox] The label sits above the box and the picked choice's hint below it. Down, Up, Enter, Space or a letter opens the list; Up and Down move; Home and End jump to the first and last; Enter, Space or Tab picks; Escape closes the list without closing a dialog around it. The choice the keys are on is tinted `selection` with a ring-colored bar at its left, and the picked choice has a check mark and bold type, not color alone. Given a name, it is a form field: a hidden input sends the picked choice, and where scripts do not run the browser's own `<select>` stands in for it, so the form still works. It can also hold its label inside the box, before the picked choice, and fill its column, as the gift panel on the Sponsorship page does. The Sponsorship page and the Account page's gift form use it for "Toward" (Trevor, Nov. 24, 2026, found the browser's own list out of place in dark mode). Every other pick-one field uses it too, in the workspace as on public pages, or the searchable dropdown when the list is long, such as the nations, organizations, contributor pages or birth years (Trevor approved this on Nov. 24, 2026, improvements report item R10). As a form field it does what the `<select>` it replaced did: `required` (an empty choice stops the form, marks the box and moves to it, unless the form checks its own answers), `disabled`, `invalid` with the error it is `describedBy`, `form` for a field of another form, a label kept for screen readers only (`hideLabel`) where a row or heading already names the box, and choices in named groups. A hint or error between the label and the box goes in its `message` snippet, where other fields show theirs. A pick sends a change event, so a filter form can send itself. Only the shared components, the date field's calendar and the essay editor's toolbar keep a `<select>`; `npm run check:shared-components` fails on any other, and on a `<table>` outside `DataTable` other than the few form grids and totals it lists.

**Star.** A reader can star a page and keep a private note on it, with no account (Trevor asked for this on Nov. 21, 2026, and approved the plan and the build on Nov. 22; [decision record 0032](../docs/decisions/0032-starred-pages-and-shared-collections-on-the-reader-s-device.md)). An icon button with Lucide's `star` icon, labeled "Star this page", sits before Print in the masthead on a page with a lasting address (`src/lib/starred-items.ts`). Those are nations, glossary entries, stories and, once published, sections of the code, essays, forms and sources. It is a toggle button (`aria-pressed`), so its name stays the same; a starred page shows a filled star, its tooltip says "Starred," and a status message says what changed. "Starred Pages," in the Library menu and in the account menu, opens `/starred`, which lists the starred pages newest first. Each shows its kind and the day it was starred, a "Your note" box (plain text, up to 2,000 characters, saved as the reader types) and "Remove star," which can be undone. Stars and notes are kept in the browser's local storage (`starred-items`) on that device only, so nothing is sent to the site; Settings, Data exports them with the other reader data and can delete them. Step 2, keeping stars in step across devices through the reader's sign-in, waits until the model code has final section numbers.

"Share a collection," on the same page, makes a link to chosen starred pages, up to 100, with a title (up to 120 characters) and an optional description (up to 500), as Trevor decided on Nov. 21, 2026. The link carries the whole list after `#c=` in `/starred/shared`; the part of a link after `#` is not sent to the server, so the site stores nothing and never sees what was shared.[^url-fragment] The link names no one and never holds notes. The shared page shows the title and description as plain text, under the label "A reader's collection, shared by link," so it is not mistaken for the site's own text (site copy never calls itself "the project"). It names each page from the site's own search index, so a link cannot put other words on the site. "Star all of these" adds the pages to the reader's own stars. A link that cannot be read says so and points to the nations and the glossary.

**Follow.** A signed-in reader can follow a nation's page, the model code or one of its sections, or an essay, and is told when it changes (Trevor asked for this on Nov. 23, 2026; [decision record 0039](../docs/decisions/0035-follow-pages-with-notices-that-never-quote.md)). An icon button with Lucide's `bell` icon, labeled "Follow this page", sits after Star in the masthead on those pages (`src/lib/components/FollowButton.svelte`, `src/lib/following.ts`). For a signed-in reader it is a toggle button (`aria-pressed`); a followed page shows `bell-ring`, its tooltip says "Following," and a status message says what changed. For a signed-out reader it opens a short dialog that says following needs an account and offers "Sign in," which comes back to the page. Following, in the account menu under the profile button, shows the number of new updates in the action color ("1 new"). `/following` lists the updates newest first, each with the page's title, its kind and "Updated" with the day, and a "New" tag until the page is opened; then the pages followed, each with "Stop following" (`bell-off`); then the email choice: no email, a daily summary (the default) or a weekly one, with a note while the site cannot send email yet. A notice and an email say only that a page changed and link to it, never what changed, so they cannot show what a nation asked to have taken off its page. Essays, and the model code once it is built, also link to "What changed" (`history`); nation pages have no public version history. Without scripts the button is hidden. How updates are found and sent is in [COLLABORATION.md](COLLABORATION.md#following-pages).

**Print.** An icon button with Lucide's `printer` icon, labeled "Print this page", in the masthead beside Download (Trevor, Nov. 22, 2026), opens the Modal titled "Print this page" (asked for by Trevor on Nov. 21, 2026, after the delivery dialog of Westlaw, where a reader chooses what prints before printing). Westlaw splits its dialog into "Layout and Limits" (for example term highlighting, footnotes at the end or inline, and a cover page) and "Content to Append" (extra lists to add);[^westlaw-delivery] this dialog follows that split with two sections: The dialog's Print button sits at the bottom right, and "Reset print choices to defaults" is an icon-only button (Lucide `rotate-ccw`) beside Close in the top bar, which says aloud that the choices were reset (Trevor, Nov. 23, 2026; `topButtons` and `actionsAtEnd` in `Modal.svelte`).

- **What to print:** a checkbox for each part the current page has, found when the dialog opens from the page's `data-print-part` markers: Page details, Review status, Seal or logo, Maps, Tables, Document history and Sources. All are on by default. The main text always prints; menus, buttons, request links and the footer never do. "Page details" is a line that exists only on paper, under the site's name and content version, giving the page's address and the date printed, so a paper copy leads back to the current version (Westlaw's cover page plays the same role).
- **Layout:** where citations go (note numbers with the References list, each note under its paragraph, or no note numbers), whether each outside link prints its web address, and whether defined terms keep their dotted underline. Each choice is offered only when the page has notes, outside links or defined terms.

"Print" at the bottom closes the dialog and opens the browser's print window, where the reader can also save a PDF and set paper size, margins and scale. Text size and fonts follow the reading settings. Choices are kept on the device in local storage (`print-options`, non-default values only) and become `data-print-*` attributes on the root element, set before paint by the same inline script as the reading settings; `global.css` reacts to them only inside `@media print`, so the screen never changes and the browser's own Print command follows the same choices. Notes under their paragraphs are copied in on the window's `beforeprint` event and removed on `afterprint`.[^mdn-beforeprint] Without scripts the button is hidden and pages print with every part.

Each section added at the end of a page, now Document history and Sources, starts on a new sheet of paper (`break-before: page`; Trevor, Nov. 22, 2026). A section the reader leaves out is not drawn, so it leaves no blank page, and the main text never starts with a break. New sections of that kind join the rule in `global.css`.

When the model code's provision pages are built, their parts join the list in `src/lib/print-options.ts` the same way: commentary, drafting notes, alternatives and version history are the expected ones. Leaving a part out of a print is a reader's choice; a page's notices (such as "This is a fictitious example") are never optional.

**Notices.** One style for status: "This is a working draft" on the home page; "Model text, not law in force" with the version and last change on every section, following legislation.gov.uk's status box; "You are offline; showing content as of [date]" when offline. A notice is a `role="note"` block, not an alert.

**Section heading.** Track label, then the section number at display size in the track color, then the title as the page's `h1`.

**Alternatives.** A tab list: one tab per alternative plus "Compare side by side." Arrow keys move between tabs (WAI-ARIA tabs pattern). With scripts off, all alternatives are shown in order, each under its own heading.

**Placeholder.** `{{nation.name}}` renders as "[Name of Nation]" with the placeholder fill and rule. The variable's identifier is available to assistive technology through a visually hidden label such as "placeholder: name of the nation."

**Glossary link and hover card.** A dotted underline marks a defined term. The card opens on hover after a short delay and on keyboard focus, stays open while the pointer is over it, and closes with Escape, as WCAG 1.4.13 requires for content on hover or focus.[^wcag-1413] The link itself goes to the full entry, so the card is never the only way to the content. The card shows the term, its expansion and a one-line summary, holds no links of its own (`role="tooltip"`, tied to the link with `aria-describedby`), and appears for a mouse pointer or keyboard focus, not a touch, since a tap follows the link. Summaries come from `/glossary/terms.json`, which the glossary pages are built with, fetched once when first needed. The Previews setting turns cards off.

**Link previews.** Links to a nation's page inside a page's text get the same kind of card, the way Wikipedia and Wikiwand preview articles (Trevor approved this from the Wikiwand review on Nov. 22, 2026). It shows the nation's name, its seal where its page shows one, its recognition status, its states and its page's review status, all copied from the cited facts on that page, from `/nations/previews.json`, built with the site and saved for offline reading. Links in menus, the sidebar, tables, dialogs and search results get no preview, because they sit in long lists, and neither does a link to the page the reader is on. `LinkPreviews.svelte` handles both kinds; the Previews setting (stored as `glossaryCards`) turns both off. Previews of model code sections and essays will use it too once those pages exist.

"Show the paragraph before" and "Show the paragraph after" sit above and below it; a section letter between paragraphs ("B") comes with the paragraph after it. Print shows neither the buttons nor the panels, and the service worker never saves `/quote-context` answers, since they depend on the words asked about.

**Glossary pages.** The glossary page and each entry's page are built from one module, `src/lib/server/glossary-entries.ts`, and one component, `GlossaryEntry.svelte`. An entry follows the layout of a printed dictionary entry, as Merriam-Webster's explanatory notes describe it (Trevor asked on Nov. 22, 2026):[^merriam-webster-notes] the headword in bold; an italic label for the part of speech (`part_of_speech`), or "abbreviation of" the expansion for an acronym, with ", legal term" for a legal term; the usage label as a small badge with an icon (Preferred, Acceptable, Only in context, Avoid); "other forms" with the variants in bold; then the senses, each numbered 1, 2, 3, even when there is only one (`other_senses` holds the senses after the first; Trevor asked on Nov. 23, 2026 for every definition to be numbered, and the bold colon that opened each sense in Merriam-Webster's style was dropped because it read as an unexplained mark), with an italic label for a sense that needs one, such as "often capitalized". Where a law dictionary defines the term, a line after the senses names the dictionary and its headword and quotes a short phrase from the entry, with a citation (`law_dictionary`). Black's Law Dictionary (12th ed. 2024) is under copyright, so the glossary never reproduces a full entry; `scripts/validate_records.py` refuses a quotation over 20 words, and Trevor chose not to use the public domain 1891 and 1910 editions (Nov. 23, 2026). The usage note follows under "As used here" (it replaced "How the project uses it"), then "Use instead" and "See also" with the other entries' names in capitals, as a dictionary sets cross-references. When the model code enacts a definition of the term (`code_definition`), a box with a section sign names that section and says that its definition is the one with legal effect. An entry's citation is a numbered marker in its text (see Citations below) for a source in the page's References section, where each source is listed once, in the project's Indigo Book style (formatted at build time by citeproc-js with `pdf/styles/project-law-review.csl`, as the print edition is), with links back to its markers and followed by every entry that cites it, as on nation pages. `/glossary/terms.json` lists every entry's term, usage and a one or two sentence summary with no citations, for the hover cards and for saving offline. An entry's own page does not list the entries near it alphabetically (removed Nov. 23, 2026; Trevor saw no benefit).

**Where to read it.** A cited case or law that can be read in more than one place has a small book icon after its citation, after its markers at the head of a case or law box (`src/lib/reading-links.ts`). Trevor asked on Nov. 23, 2026 that these places stand apart from the citation, and chose the design from mockups the same day: Lucide's `book-open-text`, the number of places and a down arrow, with no border around them, so it reads as a mark beside the citation rather than a toolbar button. Selecting it opens the list below, and the arrow turns over; its name for screen readers is "Where to read it, N places". The list is set apart by a rule down its left side and has one line for each kind of place, with no headings: first the copy this site keeps, "Saved copy (official report, PDF, Nov. 23, 2026; SHA-256)", which for a Supreme Court case is the official report, since quotations follow it; then the official and free texts followed by Westlaw and Lexis, separated by middle dots, with no mark for a subscription on the page (Trevor: "assume people understand"), though screen readers hear "(subscription)"; then "Further reading: Wikipedia". Without scripts the icon is hidden and the list shows. Print leaves it out, and copying a citation from its preview leaves it out too.

**Citations.** Every page cites the way Wikipedia does (Trevor asked on Nov. 22, 2026; on Nov. 23, 2026, he asked that selecting a marker go to the References list, with the preview kept for pointing). A claim, a fact or a sentence ends with a small number in square brackets, such as [3], one for each source; the number is the source's place in the page's References section, numbered in the order the page first cites it. Prose (commentary, glossary entries, stories and essays) gets its markers from `[@key]` citations in Markdown (`src/lib/server/markdown.ts`); data pages, such as a nation's facts, get them from the facts' sources (`src/lib/server/source-uses.ts`, `CitationMarks.svelte`). Both write the same HTML (`src/lib/citation-marks.ts`).

- **Beside the marker.** On wide screens, pointing at a marker or moving to it with the keyboard shows the citation in a small window under it, as Wikipedia's Reference Previews do;[^reference-previews] the window stays while the pointer is over it, so its links and its copy button can be used. Selecting the marker (click, tap or Enter) goes to the source's entry in the References list, which is highlighted and whose number links back to the marker (Trevor asked on Nov. 23, 2026: "clicking the number for source should jump to the bottom," with no second window and no close button). From the keyboard, Tab on a marker whose window is showing moves into it, Tab past its last control goes on to whatever follows the marker, and Shift+Tab or Escape returns to the marker. The window shows the source's number, the citation (each outside link opens in a new tab and says so to screen readers) and the place in the source the marker cites. Its copy button, an icon (Trevor, Nov. 23, 2026), puts the full citation, as the References list gives it in Indigo Book form, on the clipboard, with italics when pasted into a word processor; its icon turns to a check for a moment, and a status message says so to screen readers. Phones and other screens without hover get no window: a tap goes to the list (`CitationPopup.svelte`).
- **In the References list.** Each entry's number links back to the place in the text that cites the source. A source cited in several places also gets a letter for each, a, b, c (after z come aa, ab), each linking back to one marker, as Wikipedia's backlinks do (Trevor, Nov. 22, 2026). The marker or entry a link goes to is highlighted for a few seconds, and the highlight goes sooner when the reader clicks or taps elsewhere; keyboard focus keeps its own ring. On paper the letters are left out and the number stays.
- **Without scripts,** with the Citations setting "Go to the References list," and on paper, a marker is a link to its entry in the References list, and the entry's number links back. The Citations setting can instead open the note under the paragraph.

Each note links to the original page and, where the source record has one, its archived copy with the capture date, for example "archived copy, Nov. 21, 2026"; the Citations setting can hide either link. The margin layout planned for wide screens (the note beside the line) is not built; the window beside the marker does its work.

**Panels.** Outlined (`line`) or filled (`surface`). One panel per purpose; the design does not chop pages into rows of identical cards (project choice).

**Summary list.** A `<dl>` of label and value pairs for nation facts and for "Check your request," following the GOV.UK summary list. A fact never shows its values as bullets (Trevor chose "label rows" on Nov. 23, 2026, from mockups in the project files, `design/nation-page-lists/`): values that each have a label, such as a governing body's name, members, how it is chosen and term, make small label rows inside the value (`.label-rows`, the labels small and muted); other values stack one per line with a thin dashed rule between them (`.stacked-list`, still a `<ul>` for screen readers). A value never gets a label that only repeats the fact's label or names its source (Trevor, Nov. 27, 2026): a page reads "Governing body: Tribal Council" and "Head of government: President", never "Name:", "Title:", "Title in the federal directory:" or "Agency:", because the citation marker already says where the value comes from. For the same reason a value is never followed by a citation written out, such as "91 Fed. Reg. 4102, 4105" after "Federally recognized": the Recognition row shows only the status and its markers, and the recognition badge at the top of the page gives the legal basis with each law linked. The value the fact is named for comes first, with no label, and any labeled values under it, such as members and term, stay label rows. Two sources giving the same value share one line with both markers. When lines next to each other in one fact cite the same sources at the same places, only the last carries the markers, as a paragraph's citation covers the whole of it; a line cited to another page of the same source keeps its own marker (`shareMarks` in the nation page's server code). Branches of government show one branch or body per line, with the powers a body holds in small type under it, and "Separation of powers" as a label row below them (Trevor chose "one line per branch" on Nov. 27, 2026, from mockups in `design/branches-of-government/`; `src/lib/governance-branches.ts` splits the recorded words, and words that are not a plain list stay one line). A note every value shares, such as "Law codes" or a state's name, is said once under the fact's label. Records with dates and citations, such as treaties and Gaming Commission enforcement actions, go in the shared data table instead. A list of names, such as Nearby Nations, sits in columns without bullets. Numbers stay only where order matters, as in a document's versions, and a single version has no number.

Inside, each contact is a row with a Lucide icon and a label (Physical address, Mailing address, Phone, Fax, Email) beside its value; in a narrow card the value moves under its label. **Tables.** For data a reader compares across rows. Wide tables scroll inside their own box, so the page never scrolls sideways. Data tables use the `DataTable` component, the project's own, not a data grid library (`spikes/tables/REPORT.md`; Trevor chose this on 2026-11-20). With `addFilter` the menus are hidden until the reader adds one from an "Add filter" button (Lucide `list-filter-plus`), and each added menu has an X to take it away; a filter set in the page address shows as added. Title row on the table" on Nov. 27, 2026; mockups in `design/table-title-row/`). At its right are icon buttons, always in this order: Search (where the table has one; it opens a box in the row), Columns, "Copy link" where the table is shareable, download (always there), and full screen (Lucide maximize and minimize, the same as the map; when the browser refuses full screen the table fills the window instead, and Escape leaves either). Leaving rows out belongs to the charts only (Trevor chose this on Nov. 23, 2026, after an eye-off button on every row of every table looked out of place: on a plain list, leaving a row out only hides it, while on a chart it redraws the scale and shares). A long list is drawn as one full table, as the Native nations are (943 rows, about 130 KB compressed), or split into short pages, never drawn as a virtual-scrolling one: the table spike measured that a virtual-scrolling grid left most rows out of the page, so find-in-page, printing and reading without scripts could not reach them (`spikes/tables/REPORT.md`, "SVAR Svelte DataGrid: rejected").

**Column widths.** A reader can change the width of any column in any table in the main column, data tables and tables written in Markdown alike (Trevor asked on Nov. 23, 2026; `src/lib/tables/resizable-columns.ts`, attached to `main` in the layout). Each header cell has a handle on its right edge, a thin rule that turns into the link color when pointed at. Dragging it sets the width, and double-clicking it fits the column to its widest line, up to 40 em. The handle is a focusable separator, following the WAI-ARIA window splitter pattern, so Left and Right arrows narrow and widen the column (Shift for bigger steps) and Enter fits it. Its target is 24 px wide. Once a column has been changed, the table switches to fixed widths, may become wider than the screen and scroll in its own box, and shows a "Reset column widths" button underneath. Widths are kept in em, so they follow the reader's text size, and remembered in the browser for each table on each page. Print sizes tables again for the paper. Tables with one column or no header row, header cells that span columns, grids such as the date picker, text being edited, and tables marked `data-fixed-columns` get no handles.

The shapes carry the meaning; the two vote colors (`vote-majority`, `vote-dissent`, from the Okabe-Ito palette) are checked by `npm run check:color-vision`. The card gives the case's name, citation, date and outcome, the Justices in the majority, in dissent and who took no part, each with the opinion they wrote, and icon buttons for the earlier and later case, copying the citation and opening the case. It is drawn in the top layer, beside the mark, or as a sheet along the bottom of a narrow screen. The cases take one stop in the tab order: the arrow keys move between them, Home and End go to the first and last, Enter opens the case and Escape closes the card. Across draws the bench chart for every Justice in the cases shown, and newest first turns the list around.

**Every count opens to its records.** Trevor asked on Nov. 23, 2026 that "all tables should be clickable into the data to explain itself." A number in a table or chart leads to the list of what it counts, each row of that list leads to the nation's page or the document, and each fact in it carries its source marker. On Data and charts, a bar not about provisions opens its own page, `/nations/data/<chart>/<bar>` (`chartRecords` in `src/lib/server/data-charts.ts`): the nations with that kind of court, each court named and cited; the nations with that constitution search result, with the constitutions their pages list; or the gaming compacts with that dispute clause, each opening the compact and summing up its clause with where it sits. On the map, every count opens the list of its nations, including each count in a view's by-state table. In the nation index, each state opens that state's list. In Find recourse, each tag on a provision (method, kind of dispute, setting, how it starts, result and state) narrows the list to the provisions that share it. The essay editor's planned Chart block is meant to use the same data shape.

**Scrollbars.** Trevor asked on Nov. 22, 2026, for scrollbars that match the design. Every scrollbar has a teal thumb on a track the color of the side rails (tokens `scrollbar-thumb` and `scrollbar-track`), set once on the root with the standard `scrollbar-color` property, which every box that scrolls inherits.[^mdn-scrollbar-color] The thumb has at least 3:1 contrast with its track and with the page in both themes, the non-text minimum that MDN points to for this property;[^wcag-1411] with "More contrast" on, it becomes ink. Forced colors mode resets the property so the system's own scrollbars return. The width is left to the browser, because `scrollbar-width: thin` makes the thumb harder to grab. `scrollbar-color` works in Chrome and Edge 121, Firefox 64 and Safari 26.2 on macOS and iOS, and has been Baseline since December 2025.[^web-features-scrollbar-color] There is no `::-webkit-scrollbar` fallback for older Safari: giving those pseudo-elements a size turns the Mac's overlay scrollbars into ones that always show,[^chrome-scrollbar-styling] and older Safari already draws light or dark scrollbars from each theme's `color-scheme`.

**Recognition.** Wherever a nation's recognition status is shown, its legal basis follows it in parentheses, and each citation in it links to the official text (Trevor asked on Nov. 22, 2026): Code of Federal Regulations parts and sections to the eCFR, United States Code sections to the Legal Information Institute, and the Statutes at Large (from 1951), Public Laws (from 1995) and the Federal Register to GovInfo's link service (`src/lib/legal-citations.ts`). Those are the address forms the project's source records already use. A legal basis with no such citation, such as a state executive order, links to the source the fact came from. Lists show a short form, such as "(25 C.F.R. Part 83)"; the nation's page shows the whole legal basis. This is a link on the authority itself, not a citation marker; the fact's own source keeps its marker and its entry in the References section.

**Recognition badges.** Trevor chose these on Nov. 22, 2026, from the mockups in project files `design/recognition-markers/` (wording set 2). They appear under a nation's name, in the nations lists, in search results (the masthead's results and the search page) and in the map's lists. Each recognition status a nation holds is a badge with a Lucide icon and a short label. "Federal" is filled (`landmark`). "State: Massachusetts" is outlined (`map-pin`), one badge for each state. "Petitioning" has a dashed outline (`file-clock`). "Territorial: Guam" (`scroll-text`) and "Congress" for the Native Hawaiian community (`scale`) are outlined on the panel color. Fill, border style, icon and words tell them apart, so color is never the only cue. No federal recognition gets no badge (Trevor: "if no federal, just don't say anything"). A nation that is only state recognized shows one state badge, and a nation with no recognized status shows none. The federal answer comes first, then each state in alphabetical order. Each badge's tooltip and screen-reader text is the full wording, such as "State recognized (Massachusetts)". In the nations lists each badge keeps its short legal basis beside it. Search results and the map's lists show the badges alone, since the legal basis is one step away on the nation's page. The nation page's At a glance box keeps the full wording of every status, "No federal recognition" included, with its legal basis and sources. On paper a filled badge prints as an outline. Badges are the same size as the page's other badges, such as who keeps the page and its history: a one-pixel border and the pill's height, even where the badge is a button (Trevor, Nov. 24, 2026, found the federal badge larger than the rest). On a nation's own page each badge is a button that opens a small panel saying how the nation came to hold that status (Trevor, Nov. 23, 2026: "clicking the badge should invoke how it got that status"). Where the record holds only the federal list, the panel says the site has not yet found the document by which the United States first recognized the nation, rather than guess. The panel is a native popover, so Escape and a click elsewhere close it. Lists keep plain badges. Code: `src/lib/recognition-badges.ts`, `src/lib/recognition-explanation.ts` and `RecognitionBadges.svelte`.

Rules (project choice):

- Color marks meaning and structure: the masthead band, links, the two tracks, the main action, placeholders, focus, errors and the map classes. Reading text stays dark ink on warm paper.
- Color is never the only cue.[^wcag-141] Links are underlined; tracks are named; compared text is struck through or underlined as well as highlighted, including added text in a nation page's changes; map tiles print their counts; pie slices carry numbers that lead their lines in the key; each timeline kind has its icon, in the key, on the year rail's lanes and on its cards; and on the map's category views each category has its own shape as well as its shade (`src/lib/map-shapes.ts`, Trevor, Nov. 27, 2026): a diamond for the darkest shade, a square, a circle, and a triangle for the lightest, on small areas' dots, on offices and in the key, on both the tiled and the drawn map. States and large land areas, too big for a shape, carry a pattern over their shade in the same order (Trevor chose patterns over a shape on each state, Nov. 27, 2026): the darkest shade stays solid, then stripes, dots, and a grid for the lightest. The Land Areas view marks its groups the same way, federal lands as the darkest shade, state reservations as the next and statistical areas as the next.
- Colors that stand for different things side by side (the chart slices and the timeline kinds) must also look different to readers with a color vision deficiency. `npm run check:color-vision` (`tools/check-color-vision.mjs`, run in CI) simulates protanopia and deuteranopia with Viénot, Brettel and Mollon (1999), tritanopia with Brettel, Viénot and Mollon (1997), and the milder protanomaly and deuteranomaly with Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes (2009) at severity 0.6, as DaltonLens implements them,[^daltonlens] in both themes, and fails if any two colors of a set come closer than a CIEDE2000 difference[^ciede2000] of 10 (8 for tritanopia, which no seven-color palette keeps at 10). The floors are the project's working judgment, not a published standard; the research is in the color vision write-up (Nov. 27, 2026). No colors stay apart for a reader with no color vision at all, which is why the rule above comes first.
- The track colors are close in lightness, so they rely on hue and on their labels. Orange and teal remain distinct for the common red-green color vision differences, which is why the pair suits the tracks (project analysis), but the words "ADR track" and "Peacemaking track" always appear.

### Color and Native design

The lead author asked the project to look into "Native American color theory." The research session [2026-11-21-color-research](../research/sessions/2026/2026-11-21-color-research.md) found that there is no single one, and that some color sets are sacred:

- "There is no single American Indian culture or language."[^nk360] Color meanings belong to each nation. In the Arizona Department of Education's *Tribal Seals and Flags*, the same color means different things for different nations, and its entry for the White Mountain Apache Tribe describes "the four Sacred colors, black, blue, yellow and white."[^arizona-seals] The Navajo Nation describes its flag's colors as those of "the four sacred mountains that define balance and order in Diné life," inspired by "the sacred practice of sandpainting."[^navajo-flag]
- The International Indigenous Design Charter, written "for both non-Indigenous and Indigenous designers," asks for design that is Indigenous led and community specific, and warns that "secret and sacred material is often restricted under customary law and therefore unsuitable for publication."[^design-charter]
- Sadie Red Wing (Lakota, Spirit Lake Nation): "If you do not know what you are communicating by using Native American symbols, don't use them," and "You cannot represent the tribes of California with New Mexican anything."[^red-wing]
- Native-led organizations use saturated, contemporary color. The live stylesheets of the Native Governance Center and the National Congress of American Indians, for example, pair deep teals with warm reds, clay or marigold (observed in each site's CSS, not in a published brand guide).[^org-palettes] The artist Jeffrey Gibson, who represented the United States at the 2024 Venice Biennale, has said, "We've been dismissed as garish and too much because of our use of color."[^gibson]

What the project does (project analysis):

- Treats color as design, not as "Native meaning." The palette carries no tribal symbolism. (The footer said so until Trevor asked on Nov. 22, 2026 for its notes to be removed.)
- Never uses a nation's directional, ceremonial or flag colors, or a medicine-wheel set, as decoration.
- Keeps teal and orange as plain fields. No feathers, patterns or borders that turn the palette into a "Southwest" costume standing in for 575 nations. No Native-authored source found names that palette as a cliché; avoiding it follows from the guidance against pan-Indian imagery above.
- Brings the commissioned Native artist in as a co-designer of the palette, not a reviewer at the end, as IllumiNative's industry guide advises against consultants brought in "to only rubber stamp or fix an issue at the end of the creative process."[^illuminative]
- Keeps the nation profile template neutral, so one aesthetic is not wrapped around every nation. A nation's own seal or flag appears only with its permission and is never recolored.

### Type

One family, Atkinson Hyperlegible Next, from the Braille Institute, which offers it in seven weights with italics and says it covers more than 150 languages.[^atkinson] Its letterforms are built to be told apart, which suits an accessibility-first reference (project analysis). The site uses 400, 600 and 700 only.

- **Scale:** 1.25 from a 16 px body (12 points, the browser default): 14, 16, 20, 25, 31.2 and 39.2 px. Trevor asked on Nov. 22, 2026 for a smaller standard size than the earlier 18 px, which read large on a laptop; readers can still change it with the text size setting.
- **Line height:** 1.6 for reading, 1.25 for headings.
- **Measure:** the text width the reader picks in Settings, a share of the page's column, 100 percent by default (Trevor, Nov. 23, 2026). Until then it was at most 58ch, inside the 45 to 90 character range Practical Typography recommends;[^butterick] a `ch` is the width of the digit zero, which in Atkinson Hyperlegible Next is about 1.5 times an average letter, so 58ch held about 86 characters of English text (measured in Chromium at 18 px, Nov. 21, 2026). Because `ch` follows each element's own font, the old cap gave a notice, a hint and a paragraph three different widths; a percentage gives them one.
- **Case:** sentence case for every heading and label ([style guide, section 6](../docs/style-guide.md#6-mechanics)); no text in all capitals.
- **Fallbacks:** Noto Sans, Noto Sans Cherokee, Noto Sans Osage and Noto Sans Canadian Aboriginal, loaded with `unicode-range` only when a page contains characters Atkinson Hyperlegible Next lacks. Names are written exactly as each nation writes them, including the ʻokina in Hawaiʻi ([style guide, section 3](../docs/style-guide.md#3-writing-about-native-nations-and-people)). Text in a Native language carries a `lang` attribute where the language is known.
- **Reader's choice:** in Settings a reader can switch the body text, the headings or both to Noto Serif or OpenDyslexic. Noto Serif was picked over Literata and Source Serif 4 because it covers ʻ, ō, ʷ and ʔ and matches the Noto fallbacks. OpenDyslexic was asked for by Trevor; a study found it did not improve reading rate or accuracy for students with dyslexia,[^wery] so the site makes no claim about it. Both are under the SIL Open Font License; OpenDyslexic reserves its name, and the site serves its files whole and unchanged, which the license FAQ says keeps the name usable.[^ofl-faq] Details are in the research session [2026-11-21-reading-settings-fonts](../research/sessions/2026/2026-11-21-reading-settings-fonts.md). Each body font choice points the `--font-family-reading` custom property at another font token, so the map labels follow too; each heading font choice points `--font-family-heading` the same way. OpenDyslexic's four faces are a small style sheet of their own at `/opendyslexic.css`, read only by a reader who picks it or opens Settings, instead of being part of the style sheet every page loads; `src/app.html` adds it before the first paint for a reader who picked it for the body or the headings, so the font shows as before.
- **Hosting:** the font files are served by the site itself, not by a third-party font service, so offline reading works and no reader's address goes to another company (project choice). Its license is the SIL Open Font License, kept beside the files.

### Space, radius and layout

An eight-step space scale from 4 px to 72 px; 6 px corners on controls and 10 px on panels; a 44 px minimum target. All values are in [design-tokens.json](design-tokens.json).

The page uses the whole width of the screen, with a gutter that grows from 16 px on a phone to 48 px on a wide screen. Until Nov. 21, 2026 it was held to 1,240 px and centered, which left wide empty margins on large screens; Trevor asked for them to go. The trade-off (project choice): running text stays at the reading measure, because long lines are harder to read and to track back to the start of the next line,[^butterick] while tables, maps, panels of contents, the masthead and the footer use the full width. Where a page has more than text, such as a nation's page, a second column beside the text uses the space; the `side-column` token (40 rem) caps it. Every width on the site comes from the `layout` tokens, so one change moves them all (Trevor asked on Nov. 23, 2026 that widths be "consolidated against the entire project"): `measure` for running text, notices, panels and page wrappers; `field` (32 rem) for one-line text fields and menus, and `field-short` (12 rem) for dates, times and amounts, while text areas and the label, hint and error around a field take the measure; `popover` (28 rem) for boxes that open over the page, such as menus, link previews and citation popups; `popover-form` (24 rem) for a short form that opens over the page, such as the sign-in panel; `panel` (40 rem) for dialogs and boxes that stand alone, such as sign-in; `card-min` (18 rem) for the narrowest card or column in a grid; `label-column` (14 rem) for the labels of a label and value list; and `side-column` (40 rem). Fixed sizes remain only for icons, handles, small pictures and the map. The measure holds paragraphs, lists and other blocks of text, not headings: `.reading` sets it on what it contains rather than on itself, so a heading and a table inside it use the width of the column. A heading held to a measure meant for 18 px text broke after a few words, as in "Tribal court opinions: Mohegan / Tribe of Indians of Connecticut", and a table held to it squeezed each column down to a word or two (Trevor, Nov. 23, 2026). Links in a table break only inside a word longer than its column, so a column of links is never crushed to a few letters.

## 8. Themes

- **Choices:** Light and Dark, switched by one button in the masthead that shows a sun in the light theme and a moon in the dark theme (Trevor, Nov. 21, 2026). Until the reader presses it, the device's `prefers-color-scheme` setting decides.[^mdn-color-scheme] Once pressed, the choice stays on that device. "Match this device" in Settings, or clearing the site's data, returns to the device's setting.
- **How:** the root element carries `data-theme="light"` or `"dark"` when the reader picks one; otherwise the `prefers-color-scheme` media query decides. A small inline script sets the attribute before the page paints, so there is no flash of the wrong theme. The choice is stored on the device only.
- **Print:** a print stylesheet uses the light theme, drops the navigation and margin tools, and prints each note's full citation and each link's address. Readers choose which parts print in the Print dialog (section 6, "Print").

## 10. Offline and installable

- **Install:** a web app manifest and a service worker make the site installable.
- **Caching:** on install the worker saves the core pages, the scripts and styles they load (read from the saved pages), the reader tools they open on demand (downloading a page or a table), Atkinson Hyperlegible Next and the static files (`src/lib/offline-shell.ts`). Every other script and style, including the video call kit, the editor and the map library, is saved the first time a page uses it, and a saved collection saves the scripts and styles its pages load. The list of every built page is a separate file that the worker reads only for saving and showing collections, so the worker's own script stays small. Atkinson Hyperlegible Next is cached first; Noto fallbacks and the fonts picked in Settings are cached the first time they are used, and picking a font downloads all its weights at once so it also works on pages read later offline. A new build keeps saved font files whose names (which carry a hash of the content) have not changed. Sections, profiles, the glossary and sources use stale-while-revalidate, following web.dev's and MDN's caching guidance.[^webdev-caching][^mdn-caching] Cache names carry the build version, so each build replaces the old copy once the reader updates (see Updates below). Saved files that did not change are carried into the new copy rather than downloaded again.
- **What is saved:** every page a reader opens is saved as they read it. A reader also chooses what to save on the Read offline page (`/offline`), the layout Trevor approved on Nov. 26, 2026 after asking for accurate counts and sizes, a finer and more modular choice, a view of what they chose and a quicker check for updates. At the top, three cards: "On this device" (the space the site uses, from the Storage API's estimate, how much of it the reader chose and how much was saved as they read, and "Keep when space runs low," which asks the browser for persistent storage[^mdn-storage]), "Saved by you" (how many pages) and "Updates" (when the site last checked, with a check button, or how many saved pages a waiting update changes and about how much they take, with "Update now"). Opening the page checks for a newer version. Below, "Choose what to save" is one outline of the site drawn from the build's catalog (`tools/write-offline-catalog.mjs`, `src/lib/offline-choices.ts`): parts (Nations, Laws, Cases, Supreme Court, Biographies, Stories, Glossary, Other pages, Documents this site holds), their groups, groups split by year where that helps (the Federal Register, agency decisions), runs of 50 pages named by their first and last page, and pages. Each row has a tri-state box, how much of it is saved ("All saved", "N of M saved"), whether the waiting update changes it, and its count and size. Ticks wait in a bar at the bottom ("Save N pages, X MB", "Remove …", Cancel, Apply). "Everything" or "Saved by you, X MB" switch the outline; "Find a page" searches every page by name. "Saved as you read" says how many pages the reader opened and offers Clear. Settings > This device shows only the three cards, with "Choose what to save" linking to the page. A button in the masthead, after Star, saves or removes the page being read ("Save this page on this device", Lucide `hard-drive-download`, a toggle), which is how laws, cases and biographies are usually saved. The worker keeps the choices (whole parts and groups, and single pages; a run is kept as its pages, since runs move as pages are added), so new pages of a chosen part are saved after an update. Counts come from the catalog and the saved files; sizes come from the built files, and for pages the Worker draws on request, from an estimate fitted to their page data. Saving runs six files at a time inside the service worker, with progress on the rows being saved. Documents keep their own sets: the United States documents as a whole or one by one, and each nation's documents as that nation's set, as its page saves them.
- **What stays online:** `/offline` has a table of what can be saved and what stays online, with the reason for each (Trevor, Nov. 23, 2026). Saved: the site's own pages and data files, pictures on a page, transcripts and the drawn map. Online only or a link: a nation's seal or logo (never cached, so a withdrawal takes effect at once); copies whose only source is the National Indian Law Library, code publishers' editions, and articles and books, which are links (decision 0031 and the National Indian Law Library rule); video and sound, which the worker never saves (it skips `video/` and `audio/` responses and partial `206` responses); and the zoomable map, whose tiles under `/map-tiles/` the worker never saves.
- **Saving copies of documents:** the copies the site serves under `/copies/` are saved only when the reader asks (`src/lib/offline-documents.ts`), in two kinds of set: "United States laws and government documents" in This device, and one nation's constitutions, laws and tribal court opinions (decision 0047) from icon buttons on the "Laws and agreements" heading of its page, with the count and size beside them (`NationDocumentsOffline.svelte`, through the `actions` snippet of `Collapsible.svelte`), since all nations' documents together come to several gigabytes (Trevor, Nov. 23, 2026). The build publishes each set's list (`/copies/offline/federal.json`, `/copies/offline/nations/<id>.json`, and the short `/copies/offline.json` with every set's size). Saved copies live in their own cache, named `documents`, which updates leave alone, because a new copy of a document always gets a new address. Each saved copy carries its SHA-256. When a new build installs, the worker reads the new lists of the sets the reader keeps and deletes every saved copy that is no longer listed or whose SHA-256 changed, so a nation's request to remove a document reaches saved copies at once; if any list cannot be read, it deletes nothing that time. The kept sets are then marked unfinished, and the next page load saves their new copies. Copies the bucket lacks redirect to the original and are not saved. A saved copy answers `/copies/...` offline; others come from the network as before.
- **ZIP downloads:** Trevor chose on Nov. 23, 2026 to offer three ZIP files. "All the data in one file" (`/zip/data.zip`) is built with the site from the files each page already offers (the list of nations, each nation's record and sources, the provisions, the glossary, the list of copies) and the stories; a story's full text is in it only where it is in the public domain or a nation's law, since a downloaded file cannot be withdrawn. One nation's documents (`/zip/nations/<id>.zip`, from the box on its page) and the United States documents (`/zip/united-states-documents.zip`, from Downloads) are put together by the Worker when asked for, streamed from the bucket (`src/lib/server/zip-stream.ts`, `document-zips.ts`) from the build's list of the set, so a removed copy is never in them. Each holds a README, the copies under `documents/`, `documents.csv` (title, where it was published, our address, size, SHA-256) and `SHA256SUMS.txt`. The worker never saves `/zip/`.
- **Checking a file:** Trevor asked on Nov. 23, 2026 for every download and document to come with a way to check it. After the build, `tools/write-checksums.mjs` writes `/checksums.json` (`{ built, files: { "/path": { sha256, bytes } } }`), `/checksums.txt` (the form `sha256sum -c` reads) and the short `/checksums/downloads.json` without the copies and each nation's own files. A download is a built spreadsheet, ZIP, PDF, Word or EPUB file, a model court form under `/help/forms/`, or a JSON file the Download menu offers; the copies' hashes come from the capture index through `/copies/list.csv`. The Downloads page hides each file's SHA-256 behind a fingerprint icon (Trevor, Nov. 24, 2026: the hashes read as clutter); opened, it shows the hash, a `.sha256` file in the form `sha256sum -c` reads, naming the file by its dated name, and a Verify File icon. A click on any SHA-256 the site shows copies it (Trevor, Nov. 24, 2026; `src/lib/copy-hash.ts`, `CopyHash.svelte`): the hash is a button, reached with Tab and copied with Enter or Space, shaded on hover; once copied the shade deepens to the link color and glows for two seconds, then fades back, with no icon or word beside it (Trevor, Nov. 24, 2026: "we don't need it to say copied or have the copy symbol"), and a status line tells a screen reader. Where a line names a hash without showing it ("SHA-256" after a saved copy, the Copies of documents table), a copy icon alone follows the word; it turns to a check and "Copied" shows beside it. The markup is the same whether a Svelte page draws it or it is built as HTML text, and the layout does the copying for every page. Every ZIP carries `SHA256SUMS.txt` in the same form; each copy in a References list has "SHA-256" after its "Saved copy" link (Trevor named the link "Saved Resource" on Nov. 23, 2026, and it became "Saved copy" when the places to read a case moved into one list the same day), and Copies of documents has a SHA-256 column. The Verify File page (`/verify`; a section of the Downloads page on Nov. 23, 2026, a page of its own again from Nov. 24 at Trevor's request, and the Downloads page sends `#checking` links there with their query) uses `FileVerifier.svelte` and `src/lib/sha256.ts` to work out a chosen file's SHA-256 in the browser, never uploading it, and either compares it with the hash in the link (`/verify?sha256=<hash>&name=<file>`) or looks it up in `/checksums.json`. Files up to 256 MB use the browser's own `crypto.subtle`; larger ones are read in pieces. The ZIPs of documents are put together on request, so they have no hash of their own; the files inside them are checked instead.
- **Never cached:** any record with `sensitivity.restricted: true`, which is never published, exported, cached offline or served by any API ([data model, sensitivity flags](../docs/data-model.md#sensitivity-flags)). When a record is withdrawn, the next service worker update deletes it from every saved copy.
- **Offline notice:** "You are offline. Showing content as of [version, date]."

### Updates

How a reader who keeps the site on a device gets a new edition (project design, asked for by Trevor on Nov. 21, 2026). Each build has its own build version, the website version plus the build time, and the service worker names its cache after it. The three track versions come from release-please (`content/version.txt`, `site/version.txt`, `pdf/version.txt`), so a build can change the website without changing the content.

1. **Detect.** The browser checks the service worker script on every navigation, and a new build always changes it, because the script contains the build version.[^webdev-lifecycle] SvelteKit also checks the build version when the tab regains focus, on data responses and on a timer; when it sees a new build, the page asks the browser to check the worker at once.[^sveltekit-updated] The worker never saves SvelteKit's version file (`/_app/version.json`), so that check always reaches the server; a saved copy would hide a new build from an open tab until the next check. "Check for updates" in Settings does both on demand.
2. **Install in the background.** The new worker saves the new build's scripts, styles and core pages into its own cache and then waits. It downloads only what changed, as Trevor asked on Nov. 22, 2026. Every page carries its build version, so every page differs from the last build's copy even when nothing on it changed: in a test, two builds of the same files differed in all 1,108 pages, and only there. So each build publishes `/offline-fingerprints.json`, a fingerprint of every file a reader may save, taken with the build version blanked out (`tools/write-offline-sizes.mjs`, `src/lib/offline-fingerprints.ts`). The new worker takes the same fingerprint of each saved file in the old cache. When the two match, it writes the new version into the old copy and saves that instead of downloading it; the result is exactly the file the server holds. Script, style and font files carry a hash of their content in their names, so one with the same name is copied as it is. Saved pages that changed are listed and downloaded on the next page load. This covers every saved page, not only kept collections, so a page the reader opened still opens offline after an update. In a test of a one-line change to one nation's page, a reader keeping Nation profiles made 8 requests (about 1 MB) instead of 3,155 (121 MB). It does not take over on its own: taking over mid-visit would mix two versions in one page.[^webdev-lifecycle] While it waits, the old worker keeps saving pages it reads only if they come from its own build (each built page names its build), so saved copies never point at scripts they lack.
3. **Withdrawals first.** As soon as the new worker installs, every saved page that is not in the new build is deleted from the saved copies, without waiting for the reader. A nation's request to withdraw material therefore reaches every device on its next visit online ([charter, commitment 6](../docs/charter.md#6-the-right-to-correct-restrict-or-withdraw)).
4. **Ask.** A button in the masthead, a refresh icon with the number of changes in a badge, shows only while an update waits (Trevor, Nov. 27, 2026: in the header, "then it won't block"; it was a card at the bottom left of the window). It sits in the masthead's row on phones too, not in More. It opens a panel under it (a native popover, as Page settings is) that says what the update brings, read from `/edition.json` on the network: "It brings content version 0.3.0; you are reading 0.2.0," or that only the website changed. Below that it lists every pull request merged since the build the reader is running ("#91 Rework Settings controls…"), as Trevor asked on Nov. 22, 2026, and all of them rather than the latest few, as he asked on Nov. 27, 2026. When they span more than one day they are grouped under the day they were merged, in the reader's time zone, and the list scrolls inside the panel, so the buttons stay in view. Each build reads its merges from git (`vite.config.ts`, `src/lib/recent-changes.ts`) and serves them in `/edition.json`. Cloudflare's build clones only the latest commit, and it skips the build of a commit that a newer push overtakes while it waits (104 of the 200 builds from Nov. 24 to 27, 2026), so a list carried from build to build missed every skipped pull request.[^cf-skip-superseded] The build therefore clones the public repository's commits alone (no files, under a megabyte, about a second) and reads the history of the commit it is building (`WORKERS_CI_COMMIT_SHA`), and it also carries forward what the live site's `/edition.json` lists (`src/lib/server/recent-changes-build.ts`), keeping the newest 1,000 (about 110 KB, a prerendered file outside the Worker).[^cf-build-variables] If neither can be read, the build goes on with what git gives. The reader's page knows the newest pull request its own build has (`virtual:build-change`) and lists the ones above it. It does not count from the time the build ran: Cloudflare builds the commit that was pushed but takes its time a minute or so later, so a pull request merged in between was in neither build's window, and the panel listed nothing (Trevor asked why on Nov. 27, 2026). When the list does not reach back to the reader's build, it ends with "Changes merged before <day> are not listed." When both builds' fingerprints can be read, it also says whether the update changes, keeps or removes the page being read. That comparison leaves out the names of the page's script files, so a change to the scripts alone does not count. It offers "Update now" and "What changed" (the changelog). Closing the panel is "later": the button stays until the reader updates. Until the reader opens it, it covers nothing and takes no focus.
5. **Apply.** "Update now" tells the waiting worker to take over (`skipWaiting`), and the page reloads once it has.[^mdn-skip-waiting] A reader who does not update keeps the button; the browser applies the update by itself once every tab of the site has closed.[^webdev-lifecycle] The button appears only once the new worker has finished installing, so nothing is left to download when the reader presses the button. Taking over is kept to the bare minimum, because the browser holds back the reload until the new worker has taken over. Trevor asked on Nov. 22, 2026 for the button to apply at once. In a test with Nation profiles saved, tidying the old cache while taking over held the reload for about 20 seconds; with the tidying moved after it, the new page loaded in about 1.3 seconds. Once it has taken over, the new worker carries forward, as in step 2, any page the reader opened while it waited, then deletes the old cache, keeping font files that have not changed and the list of kept collections. Requests from the page to the worker (saving, the Data tab) wait for that tidying; loading a page does not, except offline, when a page not yet carried forward is looked for again once it ends.
6. **Save again.** On the next page load, the page asks the new worker to save the saved pages that changed and whatever the reader's choices still lack from the new build, and a status line shows the progress. When more than 100 chosen pages are missing, they are not filled in on its own, unless the reader's own save was cut short: they wait on the Read offline page until the reader saves them. A page the Worker draws on request has no built file to compare, so it is carried into the new build when the page data it is drawn from is unchanged and every script and style it loads is still built. "Other pages" once grew from a few dozen pages to nearly 2,000 when cases and biographies were added, and a reader who had kept it was sent all of them without asking (Nov. 24, 2026); cases, Supreme Court cases and biographies now have their own collection, "Cases and biographies". Removing a collection stops a save of it that is still running. If the connection drops, it continues on a later visit.
7. **What the reader sees offline.** The offline notice names the content version of the copy being read, and Settings shows the content and website versions and the build date, so a stale copy is always labeled.

**Downloaded books.** A PDF or EPUB on the reader's device cannot be updated by the site. Each edition will print its edition number and the address `/downloads?edition=<number>`; that page compares the number with the current print version and says whether a newer edition is out, with a link to what changed. The print templates do not print the address yet.

## 11. Performance and hosting

- **Stack:** SvelteKit and TypeScript with `@sveltejs/adapter-cloudflare`, deployed to Cloudflare Workers with static assets, as Cloudflare's SvelteKit guide describes.[^cloudflare-sveltekit] Content pages are prerendered at build time; only the request form, and later sign-in and review, run on the server.
- **Budgets (project choice):** under 100 KB of JavaScript compressed on a section page; fonts subset to the characters used. The one exception is the map page.   - **Shaded views.** Views such as Public Law 280, the Violence Against Women Act's special Tribal criminal jurisdiction and the Indian Health Service Areas shade each land area by a fact about the nations whose entries name it (`src/lib/shaded-map-views.ts`, Trevor, Nov. 23, 2026). Each nation is sorted into one category, with an order of precedence written beside the categories, and an area named by several nations takes the first category in that order. Areas with no fact stay in `map-class-0`, and are worded as not recorded rather than as a no, since the record is only as complete as its sources. The category is a summary for a national map; the nation's page holds the facts, the state each applies in, and their sources, and the view's note says so. Public Law 280 and the Violence Against Women Act list nations in their tables, so nations with no land area on the map are still listed; the Indian Health Service view lists its Areas, each opening to its nations, and writes each Area's name on the map. The Areas use three shades chosen so neighbors differ, and the names, not the shades, tell them apart. Each view's legend and table are fetched from `/nations/map/views/<view>.json` when it is first chosen, and the map and the table change together once they arrive. Its drawn copy is apart, in `/nations/map/view-drawings/<view>.json`, fetched only for printing or when the tiled map cannot start, and put in the page only then, since thousands of hidden outlines slow every change to the page. Where a nation can have several kinds at once (courts and forums, other jurisdiction laws, agreements with states), the map shades the first kind in the legend's order and the table has a Yes column for every kind. Each view is defined once (`shadedViewDefinitions` in `src/lib/shaded-map-views.ts`) and sorted by one rule (`shadedViewRules` in `src/lib/server/nations-map.ts`); the page, the tiled map, the drawn copy, the list of sources and the built-site check all read from those two, so none of them names a view.
  - **Offices of nations with no land area.** About a third of the nation records name no Census land area, so they did not show on the map (Trevor chose dots for them on Nov. 23, 2026). Each such nation whose record has an office address is marked at that office with a dot ringed in ink, on every view of the tiled map and on the drawn Land Areas and shaded views. `scripts/maps/geocode_nation_offices.py` matches the addresses with the Census Bureau's geocoder, or places a post office box at the middle of its ZIP Code area, and writes `geography/nation-office-points.json`; rerun it when records gain or lose an address or a land area. On a shaded view an office takes its nation's category; elsewhere it goes to the nation's page. The notes say how many nations have neither a land area nor an address and so are not on the map.
  - **How to add a map view.** A shaded view takes these steps, and nothing else in the code changes. (A view that is not shaded, such as the states or regions, also needs its own branch in `TileMap.svelte`, its drawing in `src/lib/nations-map-drawing.ts` and its own table on the page.)
    1. Add its key to `ShadedMapView` and `shadedMapViews` in `src/lib/shaded-map-views.ts`.
    2. Write its categories there, each with a map-class shade from 1 to 4, in order of precedence, with a comment saying what puts a nation in each and which fields of the nation records it reads (`docs/data-model.md`). Most views use the shades 4, 3, 2 and 1 from the first category down; two categories take 4 and 2 so they stay apart in light mode, where 3 and 4 look alike.
    3. Add its definition to `shadedViewDefinitions`: the categories, what an area with no fact is called (worded as not recorded, never as a no), the note beside the map, the table's caption, whether the table lists nations or categories, the column headers, the section of the nation's page that cites the facts, and how the list of sources words them. Leave `sources` out only when there are too many to list, as for courts, where each name in the table goes to the nation's section instead.
    4. Add its rule to `shadedViewRules` in `src/lib/server/nations-map.ts`: `categoryOf` when a nation has one category, or `has` when it can have several kinds at once, and `facts`, the cited facts it reads, for the sources. A view whose categories come from the data, as the Indian Health Service has one per Area, gives `categoriesFrom` and leaves the definition's categories out. A view of a fact about each land area itself rather than its nations, such as the Census count of residents, gives `areaCategoryOf` (and `areaValue` for its table) and lists areas (`rows: 'areas'`). A view with many categories in one shade, such as languages, gives the definition a `legendLabel` so the legend has one entry for them all, and writes their names on the map.
    5. Add an entry to `src/lib/map-views.ts` with its label, group, description and search words, so it is in the views menu.
    6. Decide how it counts by state. Every view about nations gets a "Show by: Nation or State" switch for free (Trevor asked on 2026-11-23 for a version of each view by state, since single dots are hard to read). A nation counts in every state its entry places it in. If a fact names its own state, as a gaming compact, an agreement with a state or state recognition does, give the rule `statesFor` so that kind counts only in the states its facts name. Give the definition `byState: false` when a map by state would say nothing, as for languages (too many categories) or the Indian Health Service's Areas (already groups of states). Views about land areas (`areaCategoryOf`) never have one.
    7. Build, run `npm run check:built-site`, which checks that every shaded area, office and state goes to a row of the view's tables, and look at the view in light and dark mode, by nation and by state, with the tiles and without them (the drawn copy).
  - **By state.** Each state takes the shade of the key entry most of its nations fall under, with nations that have nothing recorded counting under the key's last entry, so a state where most nations have nothing recorded is shaded as unrecorded even if some have the fact. A nation with several kinds counts once, under the first, as the by-nation map shades it, and a tie goes to the entry listed first. The by-state table counts every kind each nation has, plus a column for nations with none recorded; its rows are `<view>-state-<code>`, and a link to one opens the view by state.
  - **Nations by state** shades states by how many nations have a recorded presence there, and draws no nation's land ([decision record 0007](../docs/decisions/0007-map-shows-state-presence.md)).
  - **Bureau of Indian Affairs regions** draws the twelve regions from the Bureau's own boundary file (`geography/`), with a note that they are the Bureau's administrative areas, not nations' lands. Trevor chose on 2026-11-21 to draw the official outlines even though the Navajo Region's follows the Navajo Nation's land.
  - **Land areas** draws the Census Bureau's 2020 American Indian and Alaska Native areas in three shades: federal reservations and trust lands, state reservations, and the Census Bureau's statistical areas ([decision record 0029](../docs/decisions/0028-map-adds-census-land-areas.md)). Areas too small to see get a dot. Each area links to a table row naming the nations whose entries cite that Census area. The view is heavy, so the page fetches its table from `/nations/map/land-areas.json` the first time a reader chooses it, and its drawn copy from `/nations/map/view-drawings/lands.json` only for printing or when the tiled map cannot start.
  - **Layout.** The contiguous United States (the 48 contiguous states and DC) fills a framed, labeled box at the top. Alaska, Hawaiʻi, Guam and the Northern Mariana Islands, American Samoa, and Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands sit in a row of smaller framed, labeled boxes below. Each box has its own equal-area projection, fitted to the box, and its own scale bar. Together they show that every box is at a different scale and none is in its true place (Trevor, 2026-11-22). Every shape is drawn only by its own box's projection. The earlier composite projection from d3-composite-projections placed the islands in the open water beside the contiguous states with no frames, which read as their real positions.
  - **Code.** `src/lib/map-geometry.ts` projects the shapes once, in `vite.config.ts`, so the browser gets SVG paths and no map library. `src/lib/nations-map-drawing.ts` turns them into each view's SVG markup. The page shows that markup with `{@html}` from a universal load. The outlines are sent once, in the page, and not again in the data the page hydrates from. A reader who arrives from another page without reloading gets the same markup from `/nations/map/drawings.json`. To add a view, add its drawing to `drawNationsMaps` and its entry and key to the page's list of views. The grayscale print version for the book is not built yet.
  - **Layout.** Trevor chose on 2026-11-22, from mockups in the project files (`design/map-layout/`), a smaller map pinned on the left with the view's table scrolling beside it, relying on zooming for detail. On wide screens the map is sized to fit the window's height and stays in place while the table scrolls. On narrower screens it is pinned to the top of the window and the table scrolls under it, when the window is tall enough. Print shows the map and then the table, as before. Map titles (the page heading and the view names) are in title case (Trevor, same day). The view picker, the Show switch and the Go to menu sit in a bar on top of the map, as part of the map (Trevor, 2026-11-23). The Show switch is the one the charts use for Bars, Pie and Table (`src/lib/components/ViewSwitch.svelte`), with Nations and States beside their icons (Trevor chose mockup 2A on 2026-11-24). On narrower screens only the map is pinned, so the picker and the key scroll away and most of a phone's screen is left for the table (mockup audit, 2026-11-23). There is one tiled map on the page, moved into the view on screen; changing the view or showing it by state swaps its layers, so the map keeps its place and its tiles instead of reloading (Trevor, same day). The distance scale is at the bottom left of the map, above the credit line. Beside the map there is no note on how to use it, no row count and no Download CSV button (Trevor, 2026-11-23); a shaded view keeps one note on what its shades mean. Each view's table, and its by-state table, is a CSV file under `/nations/map/tables/`, built from the same columns and rows as the page (`src/lib/map-tables.ts`), and the masthead's Download menu offers the one for the view on screen.
  - **Details.** Selecting a place on the map shows its details in the column beside the map, in place of the table, with a back arrow ("Table") that brings the table back as it was (Trevor chose mockup 3A on 2026-11-24, after the earlier preview stuck over the table and its list of nations scrolled in a small box). The details grow with the page rather than scrolling in a box of their own. Each fact is a row of its name and value. Each count of nations is a button that opens its nations under it, each linked to its page, or on a shaded view to the part of it that holds the view's fact and its sources, with a filter box when the list has 12 or more. A nation's details show its recognition, its states and the view's fact about it. The details are one component, `src/lib/components/MapDetails.svelte`, which a nation's page also shows under its Land Areas map when an area is chosen, with the area's kind, the size of its outline and a link to it on the map page (mockup 5). A nation's details also show its seal or logo where its reuse status allows, its website, and its main office's address, phone and email, from a small file a nation (`/nations/<id>/preview.json`) fetched when they open (Trevor, 2026-11-23). A place that only one nation's entry names opens that nation's details, naming the place. The details link to the full page (the nation's, or the state's). The row's id goes into the address, so the details can be linked to; a link from another page to a row opens its details.
  - **Tables.** Each table has one row per state, region or area, with its name and its count. The name opens the list of its nations, with the arrow before the name, and a row with no nations has nothing to open (Trevor, 2026-11-22). Selecting a place on the map opens its details.
  - **Zooming and moving.** Trevor asked on 2026-11-22 for the map to zoom and move. Every view, and the small map on each nation's page, can be zoomed up to eight times (the "Land areas" view sixteen). Buttons above the map zoom in, zoom out and show the whole map again (Lucide icons). The keys + and - and 0 do the same whenever focus is in the map. The scroll wheel over the map zooms around the pointer (Trevor, 2026-11-23), as do a trackpad pinch and a two-finger pinch on a touch screen. Once zoomed in, the mouse, one finger or the arrow keys move the map, and tabbing to a place off screen brings it into view. Scrolling out once the map is at its whole size scrolls the page again, so the map never traps a reader scrolling past it. Zooming changes only the drawing's `viewBox` (`src/lib/map-zoom.ts`, `src/lib/components/ZoomableMap.svelte`), so it adds no map tiles or library, fetches nothing, tracks no one, and works offline. Text, dots and line widths are multiplied by `--map-unzoom` so they keep their size on screen while the shapes grow. Printing always prints the whole map, and without scripts the map is drawn as before with no buttons.
  - **Nation pages.** Each nation's page also draws a small map of its states from the same shapes and boxes (see [Nation pages](#nation-pages)). Its outlines keep 5 percent of their points, rounded to whole map units, so the map stays light on each of the 980 pages.

## 12. Words in the interface

The interface follows the style guide's voice: plain verbs, sentence case, "they" for people, no praise or alarm. In addition (project choice):

- Name things by what readers know: "Make a request," not "Submit a ticket."
- Errors say what went wrong and how to fix it, in the interface's voice.
- Empty states say what will appear and how to add it, for example "No requests yet."
- The project never writes in a nation's voice. Profile pages say what a source records ("The nation's code provides"), and the review panel says plainly when the nation has not yet reviewed the page.

1. **Research with real readers.** Short interviews or usability sessions with a few people in each role, especially Tribal court staff and peacemakers, before the templates are built. Nothing here has been tested with them.
2. **Advisor review** of the request flow and the nation profile wording.
3. **Icon set:** settled: Lucide ([section 6, Icons](#6-components)).
4. **Art:** sizes for the cover, part openers and the home page space, to give to the art contest.
5. **Color, second pass.** Trevor's review on Nov. 20, 2026 found the first palette stale and somewhat sad; he chose teal and burnt orange, now in the tokens. Judge it again once the site is built, and set the final palette with the commissioned Native artist ([Color and Native design](#color-and-native-design)).
6. **Template builder:** how placeholders and alternatives become a nation's draft ordinance.
